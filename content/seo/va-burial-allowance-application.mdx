**Quick answer:** You can apply for a VA burial allowance online or by mailing VA Form 21P-530EZ. For a qualifying non-service-connected death on or after October 1, 2026, VA lists $1,032 for burial and $1,032 for a plot when both benefits apply. Eligibility and deadlines depend on the circumstances of the death and the benefit you’re claiming. If you’re planning ahead, compare your estimated funeral costs with the allowance your family may qualify for to see whether additional burial coverage could help.

## Who can claim the burial allowance?

A surviving spouse or child, parent, estate representative, or another person who paid the bill may be eligible. VA looks at who paid the burial and funeral costs, whether another organization will reimburse them, the Veteran’s discharge status, and the circumstances of the death. For example, VA may consider whether the Veteran was receiving VA compensation or died while receiving care at a VA facility or one under contract with VA. Check the full eligibility list before counting on a payment. [VA burial allowance eligibility](https://www.va.gov/burials-memorials/veterans-burial-allowance/)

An eligible surviving spouse already listed on the Veteran’s VA profile may receive an automatic payment for certain plot, interment, or transportation costs after VA learns of the death. Check with VA before filing if you expect an automatic payment or need to ask about additional expenses.

## How much can VA help pay in 2026?

A burial allowance helps with funeral and burial costs. A plot or interment allowance helps with the gravesite or the cost of placing remains there. VA also handles eligible transportation expenses separately. The date of death matters because the amounts changed October 1, 2026. [VA burial allowance amounts](https://www.va.gov/burials-memorials/veterans-burial-allowance/#burial-allowance-amounts)

| Type of claim | Amount VA lists |
| --- | --- |
| Qualifying non-service-connected death on or after October 1, 2026 | $1,032 burial allowance and $1,032 plot allowance where eligible |
| Qualifying non-service-connected death October 1, 2025, through September 30, 2026 | $1,002 burial allowance and $1,002 plot allowance where eligible |
| Qualifying service-connected death on or after September 11, 2001 | Up to $2,000 for burial |

Say your written funeral and cemetery estimates add up to $6,500. If VA approves both $1,032 allowances, the two payments total $2,064. That leaves $4,436 before any other benefits or funds. Your costs and the amount VA approves can differ. Check which bills are due before any reimbursement arrives.

If you’re planning your own funeral, use that example to work out what your family might still need. Start with the benefits your family may qualify for, then factor in savings set aside for the funeral and any life insurance already available for those bills. If there’s still a gap, you can compare [burial insurance options](/resources/final-expense-insurance-for-veterans). This type of life insurance, also called final expense insurance, can give the person you name money to help with funeral bills after a covered death.

## How to file the claim

1. **Gather the paperwork.** Set aside the death certificate showing the cause of death and any discharge records, such as a DD214. VA recommends providing separation records covering all periods of service. If you paid to transport the Veteran’s remains and are claiming those expenses, keep the itemized receipt in your name.
2. **Choose how to submit.** Apply on VA.gov or download Form 21P-530EZ and mail it to the address in the current instructions. Include copies of the documents that apply to your claim.
3. **Keep a claim folder.** Save a copy of the application, the submission confirmation or mailing record, and the itemized bills. If VA asks a question later, you’ll have the figures and names in one place.

[VA Form 21P-530EZ and online application](https://www.va.gov/forms/21p-530ez/)

## How long do you have to apply?

For a non-service-connected burial allowance, you usually have two years from the Veteran’s burial to file. That deadline doesn’t apply to plot, interment, or transportation claims, or if the Veteran died while receiving care at a VA or VA-contracted facility. For a service-connected death, VA lists no deadline for the burial or transportation allowance. If VA changes a dishonorable discharge after death, it says to file within two years of that change. [VA filing deadlines](https://www.va.gov/burials-memorials/veterans-burial-allowance/#is-there-a-time-limit-for-filing)

## Know who to call about each part of the bill

Start with VA for the claim amount, eligibility, and application status. Ask the funeral home for a written balance and payment dates. If a cemetery bills separately, ask which costs the cemetery collects and which the funeral home collects. Keep a note of who said what. That way, an answer about the plot won’t be mistaken for an answer about the whole funeral.

If the Veteran already had burial or other life insurance, the beneficiary can call the insurer to ask how to file a claim. That’s separate from the VA application. Check what paperwork the insurer needs and when your funeral bills are due so you can plan for any gap while the claims are pending.

## Keep the claim and your funeral budget separate

Start with three figures on paper: the total funeral and cemetery bills, the benefits VA has confirmed, and what your family has available when the bills come due. If you’re planning ahead, you can use those figures to see whether savings or existing insurance may cover the difference. If you’re arranging a funeral now, ask the provider for due dates and available payment terms. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I apply if the Veteran was cremated?**

Yes, cremation can qualify for VA burial benefits if the other eligibility requirements are met. Keep the cremation provider’s itemized paperwork with your claim records.

**What if I don’t have the Veteran’s DD214?**

VA recommends including the DD214 or other separation records. If you cannot find them, use VA’s instructions to request the military service records and ask what you can submit while you wait.

**Is the burial allowance the same as eligibility for a VA national cemetery?**

These are separate benefits with different requirements. Cemetery eligibility concerns where someone can be buried. The burial allowance helps pay certain costs when VA approves the claim.
