**Quick answer:** A free-look period gives you time to review a newly delivered life insurance policy and return it under the rules that apply. The deadline and return steps depend on the policy and state. Read the notice promptly, compare the contract with what you expected, and resolve questions before that deadline.

## Read the review notice before putting the policy away

A new policy can arrive in a thick envelope or as an electronic file. It is easy to save it for a quiet weekend. The problem is that the time allowed for review may already be running.

Find the free-look notice first. The [New York insurance regulator's consumer guide](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/consumers/life_insurance) explains the purpose of this review right and points buyers to the policy cover page. Its stated time limits apply in New York. Use the notice and rules for your own policy to find the deadline that matters to you.

Keep the date of delivery, the notice, and any related message together. If you are unsure when the review clock started, get that point resolved promptly. Do not count from the day you happened to open a file unless that is the date that applies.

Free look gives you a chance to cancel a new policy without penalty under the rules that apply. It is separate from a cancellation made much later. Read the notice's refund terms as well as its deadline. Treating the two processes as the same can lead to confusion about any money due back.

## Start with the cost and the job the policy must do

For a final expense policy, begin with two plain questions: What am I paying, and what have I bought? Locate the premium amount, how often it is due, and how long the contract requires payments. Then find the death benefit and the terms that govern it.

Compare those terms with your goal. Perhaps you wanted help with a funeral and a few related bills. The policy should be judged against that need and the monthly budget you chose, rather than a vague feeling that more coverage must be better.

Read any limits or exclusions carefully, including terms that affect a benefit early in the policy. A headline coverage amount does not explain every possible claim. If a provision is unclear, have its meaning explained while you still have time to decide.

The [California life insurance guide](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/105-type/95-guides/07-life/life-ins-guide.cfm) describes common policy terms and the importance of reviewing a new contract. California's legal details are specific to that state. Our guide to [premiums and coverage amounts](/resources/life-insurance-premium-vs-coverage-amount) can help you separate the payment from the benefit you are reviewing.

## Check the people and details on the contract

The review is also a good time to catch a name typed wrong or an old address carried into the file. Check the insured person's details, who owns the policy, and the named beneficiaries. These are different roles, so read each one rather than assuming a familiar name appears in the right place.

Check the payment instructions too. If you chose a monthly bank draft, look for the actual amount and scheduled date. A plan that relies on one account should not quietly use another. Confirm any correction through the insurer's process and keep the response with the policy.

For example, an adult child may help a parent read the documents, while the parent owns the policy and a sibling is named to receive a payable benefit. That arrangement is not explained by saying “the family policy.” The actual names and roles need to match what the owner intended and was permitted to select.

If someone helped with the application, ask them to explain anything you do not recognize. The aim is a clear record you can use later. It should not take a family member guessing what a note or an unfinished form meant.

## Use specific questions to resolve a mismatch

A useful question points to the exact item you want explained. “The payment shown here is different from what I expected” gives the company a clearer starting point than “this policy looks wrong.” Keep the document page or heading nearby if it is easy to find.

Separate a request for information from a request to return the policy. You may only want an explanation. Or you may already have decided not to keep it. Make that choice clear so a conversation about a term is not mistaken for a completed return request.

Our guide to [questions for a final expense insurance agent](/resources/questions-to-ask-final-expense-insurance-agent) can help you put concerns into plain language. You do not need to understand every insurance term before speaking up.

If the deadline is near, say so at the start. A pending question does not necessarily extend your review rights. Follow the applicable notice, and use the insurer's official contact details rather than relying on a general conversation to preserve a deadline.

## If you return the policy, follow the actual instructions

Read how the notice says to return or cancel the policy during free look. Confirm where the request must go, what it must include, and how the deadline is measured. An electronic request, a phone call, or a mailed document may not all have the same role.

Keep proof of what you submitted and when. Then confirm that the company received it and ask how any refund will be handled. Do not assume it will arrive by a certain day or through the account you prefer. The terms and process for that policy control.

Stopping a bank draft is not the same as completing a policy return. If you have chosen to return the contract, deal with both the return process and any payment instructions directly. This helps you track the result instead of wondering whether a missed payment was treated as your request.

If you keep the policy, save the final version and any confirmed corrections. Leave a trusted person a way to locate the insurer and policy number when needed. The time spent reviewing now should leave you with a contract you understand and records your family can find.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many days do I have to review a new policy?**

Check the free-look notice and the rules that apply to that policy. There is no single time limit we can give every reader. Delivery dates, policy type, and state rules can matter, so resolve an unclear deadline promptly.

**Does asking a question mean I have returned the policy?**

Do not assume so. A question about coverage and a request to return a contract are different actions. If you decide to return it, follow the stated process and keep proof of your request.

**Can I use free look to review a policy I have owned for years?**

Free look concerns the review right tied to a new policy. An older contract may have other choices for changes or cancellation, but the costs and results need a separate review. Do not apply a new-policy notice to an old contract.
