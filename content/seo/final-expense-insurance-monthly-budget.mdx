**Quick answer:** List your regular income, recurring bills, and spending that changes from month to month before discussing burial insurance quotes. Mark figures that need checking and think about a range you would feel comfortable discussing. This worksheet does not estimate insurance prices or decide a coverage amount. It helps you explain your own budget more clearly.

## Use your own figures as the starting point

Choose a recent period that reflects how you normally pay bills. Gather the records you use for household spending and note any item that was unusual. There is no need to put private figures in a public form. The purpose is to understand your own starting point as you think about insurance questions. You can discuss your goals before completing this page.

## Create four simple lines

Write four headings: money coming in, regular bills, changing expenses, and open questions. Leave a blank space beside each item until you have a figure you trust. Add notes about bills that arrive less often than monthly. Keep the worksheet readable. A clear page with a few honest gaps is more useful than a precise-looking total built from guesses.

## Check whether the month was typical

Ask what changed during the period you chose. A repair or a missed bill can make one month look unlike another. Avoid treating an unusually quiet month as a promise about future spending. ## Look at the pattern as well as the total

Imagine a month in which the bills look manageable on paper, but several payments fall close together. A single monthly total may not explain why that part of the month feels tight. Alongside the amounts you normally consider, it can help to note roughly when money comes in and when regular bills are due.

This is a household planning exercise, not a promise that an insurer offers a certain payment date or billing method. If payment arrangements matter to you, ask about the actual option being discussed. Keep that question separate from the amount you would feel comfortable spending.

## A practical budget discussion checklist

Use your own records if they help. Keep the worksheet private and share only the planning information you want to discuss. This is not an insurance application or a premium estimate.

- [ ] **Regular income.** I have considered the money I normally rely on, without counting an unusual payment as a lasting pattern.
- [ ] **Regular bills.** I have allowed for the household expenses that already have a place in my budget.
- [ ] **Changing expenses.** I have noted items that vary, rather than assuming every month looks like the least expensive one.
- [ ] **Less frequent bills.** I have left room to think about expenses that do not arrive every month.
- [ ] **A comfortable range.** I can explain a starting point for discussion without treating it as an actual quote.
- [ ] **Open questions.** I know which figures or payment details I still want to clarify.

**Do I have to share every bill with an agent?**

**Should I use one unusually low-spending month?**

**Does a comfortable monthly range determine the right coverage amount?**
