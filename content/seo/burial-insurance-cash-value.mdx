**Quick answer:** Some permanent policies let the owner access a value during life through choices such as borrowing or surrender. That feature is called cash value. Its current amount can be much smaller than the coverage shown on the policy. Loans and withdrawals can affect what remains, so check current figures before using it.

## Cash value and the death benefit answer different questions

The death benefit is what the policy may pay after the insured person dies, under its terms. Cash value is a value in some permanent policies while they are in force. Both may appear on one page, but they have different jobs.

The [New York regulator's whole life FAQ](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/faqs/consumer_faqs/life_insurance?page=2) explains how a policy's face amount differs from cash value. The name burial insurance does not, by itself, tell you what features a contract has. Read the policy type and its values before you count on being able to use money now.

For example, someone might see the coverage amount and think that full amount is there to withdraw. That is not what the coverage figure means. Write down the death benefit and current cash value as two distinct figures. If you are looking at an amount paid when a policy ends, label that as well. Keeping the labels with the numbers helps you use each one correctly.

## Read the table for the policy year you are in

Start with the policy year you are in. A table may show values for future years too. Those later figures are not all money you can use today. Check which year a line refers to before taking it from the page.

The [Texas insurance guide discusses cash value](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/pubs/consumer/cb018.html). It can take years for a policy to build value. The pattern also depends on the type of insurance. Use the table for the actual policy. A general idea about growth cannot tell you what that table says.

A statement may show a current value next to an example of what could happen later. Keep those figures apart. If there are guaranteed and non-guaranteed columns, read and keep both labels. Picking favorable numbers from each column can create a result the contract does not promise.

Our guide to [life insurance brochures and their limits](/resources/life-insurance-brochure-questions) explains why a broad product description is different from the terms and values of a policy you own.

## A loan and surrender are different actions

A policy loan and a surrender are different actions. A loan leaves money owed under the policy's terms. Surrender means giving up the policy. Either action can affect the insurance your family expects to have.

Money owed on a loan, plus related interest, can reduce amounts paid later under the contract. If you surrender, the amount you receive may differ from the cash value shown on a page. Policy terms and debt can affect the amount. Ask for current figures before you act.

Bring your original purpose back into the decision. If you bought the policy to help with funeral costs, find out how a loan or surrender would change that plan. The amount arriving in your account today is one part of the picture. What remains for the people you meant to help is another part that needs an answer.

## Do not count the same money twice in a funeral plan

Do not add a policy's full death benefit and cash value as if they were always two amounts paid together. The contract says how the benefit is paid. Cash value is not an extra death payment just because it appears on a statement.

For a reading example only, suppose a statement lists a $10,000 death benefit and $700 of cash value. That does not establish a $10,700 payment at death. The two lines have different meanings. These sample figures explain the labels. They are not a quote or a forecast of cash value.

An old note showing the face amount may also miss a later loan or other change. Have the insurer confirm the current amount payable under the contract before using it in a funeral plan.

There is no need to judge the feature by a label alone. Give the figures the jobs they describe, then use them in your plan. Our [existing-coverage review guide](/resources/existing-life-insurance-before-burial-coverage) can help you separate confirmed details from guesses. That keeps a hopeful total from standing in for the money your family could actually receive under the policy.

## Use the feature in the context of the coverage you need

Cash value may catch your eye when comparing policies. Keep the reason for the purchase in view as well. The amount of coverage, payments, death benefit, and other terms still matter to a funeral-cost plan.

Someone mainly hoping to help relatives pay a funeral bill may have different goals from someone building a larger financial plan. Compare the full policy for your purpose. One feature cannot answer every question about whether the coverage fits that purpose.

For a policy you already own, get current facts before taking out money, borrowing, surrendering, or replacing it. Tax and public-benefit questions can depend on your own facts and may need expert advice. A general article cannot work out those results for you. Start by naming the action you are considering and the figures it would change. That gives the next conversation a clear question to answer, rather than letting a cash-value label settle the whole decision.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does every burial policy have cash value?**

The name alone does not tell you. Check whether the contract is whole life or another type of insurance, then look for its cash-value table. If the policy includes cash value, its terms and current values explain what is available.

**Is cash value the amount my family receives when I die?**

It is a different figure from the death benefit. The amount payable after death depends on the contract and current policy details, including any relevant debt. Do not automatically add cash value to the coverage amount.

**Should I buy burial insurance mainly to build savings?**

Start with the purpose of the purchase and compare the full policy, including required payments and coverage. Whether a product fits a broader savings or financial plan is a separate question that deserves advice based on your situation.
