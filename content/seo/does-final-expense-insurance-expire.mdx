**Quick answer:** A final expense policy written as whole life is designed to last for life when its terms are met and required premiums are paid. Term life used for final costs lasts for a set period and may end or change at renewal. Check the policy type first. Missed payments, surrender, and policy loans can also affect whether coverage stays in force.

## Look for the policy type on the first pages

“Final expense” describes the reason for buying coverage. It does not by itself tell you how long a contract lasts. The same is true of the phrase “burial insurance.” Look for the actual policy type and the dates in the contract.

Whole life is a common form of final expense coverage. The [California Department of Insurance's life guide](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/105-type/95-guides/07-life/life-ins-guide.cfm) describes whole life as coverage designed for a person's lifetime, while term life covers a stated period. Both still have terms you must follow.

Do not rely on the premium amount or the sales label to identify the type. If the cover page is unclear, ask the insurer to point to the policy's name, premium schedule, and end or maturity provisions. Keep that answer with your policy copy.

## Whole life still needs its required payments

A whole life policy does not usually end just because you get older. But “for life” is not a promise that you can stop paying whenever you choose. Check how long premiums are due and what the contract requires to keep coverage active.

Some contracts call for payments over a set number of years. Others call for them over a much longer period. Do not assume yours becomes paid up after ten or twenty years, or at retirement. The payment schedule must say so.

A fixed premium means the required amount is set under the policy's terms. It does not mean the payment has been made. Our [fixed premium guide](/resources/fixed-life-insurance-premium) explains that distinction. Keep payment notices where you can find them, especially after a bank account change.

## Term coverage has a time limit

If you use term life to help cover final costs, check the end of the term. That date can arrive while you still need coverage. The [NAIC's life insurance overview](https://content.naic.org/consumer/life-insurance.htm) explains that term policies provide a benefit if death occurs during the covered period.

Some contracts allow renewal or conversion under stated rules. Renewal can mean a higher premium and may have an age limit. Conversion may have a deadline. Neither option should be assumed from the word “term.” Ask what the contract allows before the date is close.

**Hypothetical example:** Someone buys a 20-year term policy at age 67 to help family with future bills. The initial term reaches age 87. If there is no suitable renewal or conversion choice, that plan may leave a later gap. This is a timing example, not a description of a specific product.

## A missed payment needs quick attention

If a payment fails, contact the insurer rather than waiting for the next bill. Ask whether the policy is still active, the amount due, and the last day to resolve it. Use the written notice to confirm the deadline.

A grace period can give time to pay, but its length and effect depend on the contract and applicable rules. Do not assume that every policy allows the same number of days. Ask how a claim would be handled if death occurs while a payment is overdue.

If coverage has lapsed, ask about reinstatement. That is a request to restore it under the policy's rules. It may require payments or evidence about health. Mailing money does not by itself prove the policy has been restored. Obtain confirmation of the status and effective date.

## Loans and surrender can change the result

Cash value is not the same as the death benefit. If you have borrowed from a policy, the unpaid loan and interest can reduce what is paid at death. Depending on the contract and balance, loans can also put coverage at risk. The [Texas Department of Insurance's guide](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/pubs/consumer/cb018.html) explains that cash value access and policy loans affect life coverage.

Request a current statement if you do not know whether there is a loan. Ask what amount is owed and what could happen if you make no further payments toward it. Do not assume an old loan has gone away.

Surrender means giving up the policy under its terms. A cash payment at surrender is different from keeping a death benefit for the person you named. Before choosing it, ask for the current values and options in writing. Our [cash value guide](/resources/burial-insurance-cash-value) explains the basic terms.

## Check maturity dates and review the policy each year

A contract may list a maturity age or date. Ask the insurer what happens then under that policy: whether coverage continues, a payment is made, or another provision applies. There is no need to guess from an age mentioned in an advertisement for a different product.

Once a year, confirm the policy is active, the required premium is current, and the people named to receive the money are still correct. Check for an upcoming term or payment date. Add the insurer's phone number to the note your family will use after a death.

An early waiting period is a separate issue. It concerns what benefit is payable during the first years. It does not mean the policy ends when the period is over. Our [waiting period guide](/resources/final-expense-insurance-waiting-period) covers that point.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does burial insurance end at age 80 or 85?**

An upper age for new applicants is different from an end date for an existing policy. Check your contract. A whole life policy can continue beyond its new-application age limit when the required payments and other terms are met.

**Can I stop paying once I have paid in the coverage amount?**

Do not assume so. Adding up past premiums does not establish that a policy is paid up. Read the payment schedule and ask the insurer for its current status before stopping a payment.

**Is a policy still active after the insured person retires?**

Retirement alone does not answer that question. Individually owned and work-related coverage can have different rules. Confirm who owns the policy, its type, and whether retirement changes the coverage or payments.
