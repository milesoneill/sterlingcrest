**Quick answer:** If your term policy still has a conversion option, you may be able to move some or all of your coverage to an eligible permanent policy without a new health exam. Check the conversion deadline now. Then compare the available permanent policy with what your household needs for the years left on the mortgage.

## Find the conversion deadline first

Term life insurance provides a death benefit for a set period if the insured person dies while covered and the claim is payable. Conversion is a policy feature that can let you switch eligible term coverage to permanent life insurance under the insurer’s terms. Permanent life insurance is designed to last beyond a fixed term when the policy’s requirements are met.

Read the conversion clause on your current policy and call the insurer if a date is unclear. The last day to convert can come before the end of the term. Ask which policy types are available, how much you can convert and whether your insurer allows a partial conversion. The [Texas Department of Insurance’s life insurance guide](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/pubs/consumer/cb018.html) explains the distinction between converting a policy and renewing it.

## Work out when the mortgage and the policy end

Put both timelines on one page. Write down the date the current life policy’s level-premium period ends, the last day you can convert, and the expected last mortgage payment. The level-premium period is the time during which the scheduled premium stays the same. If the mortgage and insurance end years apart, decide which housing costs your household would need help paying during those extra years.

Say your term policy’s level-premium period ends in 2028 and your mortgage is scheduled to run until 2036. That’s roughly eight years of payments after the initial term. In this hypothetical example, the family first checks how much is expected to be owed in 2028 and whether it could carry the payment on one income. The policy might still have a renewal option, but the household needs to know what those later premiums would cost.

## Compare the options on the same page

| Option | What to ask |
| --- | --- |
| Convert some or all of the policy | What permanent policy can I move to, what premiums would be due, and what happens to the term coverage I don’t convert? |
| Renew the existing term policy if the contract allows it | How does the premium change after the initial term and what’s the last year it can renew? |
| Apply for a new term policy | What amount and term can I apply for, will the insurer review my current health, and what is the confirmed effective date? |
| Keep the current coverage until it ends | What mortgage balance and other expenses would the household need to handle afterward? |

Check which amounts, benefits and premiums are guaranteed under each option. For permanent insurance, ask what payments are required to keep the policy in force. If you’re considering a new policy, compare the actual offer with your contract’s conversion options. A quote does not confirm that you qualify. See our [guide to choosing a term for the mortgage](/resources/choosing-life-insurance-term-for-mortgage) for a way to put the loan and coverage dates next to each other.

## Ask what happens if your health has changed

If you have a conversion right, the original contract may let you use it without going through a new health review. Confirm the requirements with your insurer. A new application can involve underwriting, which is how a company assesses health and other information before making an offer. If a new application could still be pending when your conversion period ends, ask the current insurer how long it needs to process a conversion.

Decide how much of the insurance need ends with the mortgage and whether any need lasts longer. A family might want coverage for the remaining loan years, for example, while also planning separately for final expenses. Price the coverage each goal needs and check whether the premiums fit the budget. [Book a brief phone consultation](/book) if you’d like to walk through the two timelines and compare the options before changing the policy.

Keep a record of the request and any insurer confirmation. Before ending an existing policy, confirm what the new or converted coverage includes, when it takes effect and what happens to any amount you expected to keep.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I convert my term policy after it expires?**

Check the conversion language and ask the insurer for the exact last date you can submit a request. Some policies close the conversion window before the term itself ends.

**Does conversion require a medical exam?**

A qualifying conversion can allow an eligible move to permanent insurance without a new exam. Verify that your policy has that right, that it’s still open, and what the insurer needs to process it.

**Is renewing the same as converting?**

Renewing extends eligible term coverage under the existing contract. Conversion changes eligible term coverage into an available permanent policy. The premiums and how long coverage can last can differ.

**Can I convert only enough to cover part of my mortgage?**

Ask your insurer whether partial conversion is available and if there’s a minimum amount. Also check what happens to the rest of the term benefit.
