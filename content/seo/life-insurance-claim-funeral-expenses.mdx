**Quick answer:** To claim life insurance after a death, call the company listed on the policy. Ask how to make the claim. The person named to receive money will usually need forms and a death certificate. The claim does not pay the funeral home by itself. Discuss the home’s bill as a separate step.

## Start with the insurer, even if a different company sold the policy

After a death, it can be hard to know which call comes first. Start with the name on the policy and check its current phone number. An agent may help you find it. The claim itself goes through the company that holds the policy.

Tell the company who has died. Ask how the person named on the policy should begin the claim. Have the insured person's name and policy number available if you can find them. If your papers show an old company name, find out who now holds the policy. Check this before sending private records.

The [North Carolina insurance guide](https://www.ncdoi.gov/consumers/life-insurance) identifies claim forms, a death certificate, and the policy contract as common starting documents. The insurer should tell you which forms and records it needs. Do not assume that one relative's experience with another insurer describes this claim.

## Find out who needs to submit the claim

The person arranging the funeral and the person named as beneficiary may be different. The oldest child may keep the records or pay a deposit. Neither task decides who has a right to claim the money.

A daughter might organize a service while her father is the policy's beneficiary. She can help find the papers. The company needs to explain who must sign and what proof it needs. If more than one person is named, each may have forms to fill out.

The [Texas regulator's life insurance guide](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/pubs/consumer/cb018.html) explains that the beneficiary is the person named to receive the policy's money. If you are unsure who is named, ask how to find out. Do not send private records to a whole family group while you try to sort this out.

## Ask which documents and submission methods the company accepts

A useful first call ends with a short, specific list of tasks. You can ask the insurer:

1. Who needs to sign the claim, and which form should that person use?
2. What type of death certificate is required, and is a copy accepted?
3. Can the claim be sent through a secure online account, or where should it be mailed?
4. How will we know it arrived, and how can we check for a missing item?

The funeral home may help you get death certificates. Find out which type the insurer needs before ordering or sending them. The correct form and destination matter more than sending a large bundle of papers that nobody requested.

Keep the claim number, the contact details, the date sent, and copies of what you submitted together. If the insurer later asks for a document, you can tell whether it is a new request or something already sent. Ask it to explain any unclear field rather than guessing.

## Keep the funeral bill and the insurance claim on separate tracks

A family may need to choose services before the insurance claim is complete. Ask the funeral home when its bill is due and how it can be paid. If the plan involves payment from the policy, discuss that with both the home and the insurer. Be clear on what each will do.

Do not promise a provider a payment date based on a general article or someone else's claim. The claim may need more facts, and its outcome depends on the policy. The funeral home has its own billing terms.

For example, a relative may say, “There is insurance, so everything is covered.” That statement leaves two questions open: what is payable under the policy, and what will the funeral actually cost? Compare the itemized bill with confirmed resources. Our [funeral expenses checklist](/resources/funeral-expenses-checklist) can help you identify costs that need their own answer.

## Follow up on a specific missing step

When you follow up, ask whether the claim is complete and whether the company needs anything else from the claimant. Record the answer and the next action. Find out what stage the claim has reached. That gives you a better basis to follow up than just asking when money will arrive.

If you cannot locate a policy, start with the records you can find and contact the company shown on them. Our guide to [missing life insurance records](/resources/missing-life-insurance-policy-records) can help you plan a search. An old letter does not, by itself, prove that coverage is still in place.

A dispute over who should be paid may need more than routine claim help. The same is true if it is unclear who has legal power to act. Contact the insurer and, where needed, an appropriate professional or your state insurance department. You can plan ahead for a future loss. But new coverage cannot pay for a death that has already happened.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need the funeral bill before contacting the insurer?**

You can contact the insurer to request claim instructions as soon as you are ready. Ask what this policy requires. Do not delay that first conversation simply because the family is still making funeral choices.

**Can a relative help a beneficiary with the forms?**

A relative can help organize records or join a conversation if the company’s rules for consent and privacy are met. The insurer should explain which steps the named person must do and what consent a helper needs.

**Will the funeral home file everything for us?**

Ask the provider exactly which tasks it has agreed to handle and which remain with the beneficiary. Help getting a death certificate is one task. Filing the claim or accepting a policy as payment are other tasks. Confirm each one.
