**Quick answer:** Final expense insurance is commonly structured as permanent life insurance for a smaller expense, such as a funeral. Term life covers a set period and can fit a larger need that ends, such as replacing income during working years. The right comparison starts with the amount you need and how long you need it.

## Put each expense on a timeline

Term life insurance covers a specified period. If the insured person dies while the policy is active, the death benefit is paid under its terms. Permanent life insurance is designed to stay in place longer as long as the policy requirements are met. Many final expense policies are a form of whole life insurance intended for a smaller need, such as funeral and burial costs. Read the policy itself to see what type it is. [NAIC Life Insurance Buyer’s Guide](https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/publication-lig-lp-consumer-life.pdf)

List the obligations you want to protect. A mortgage might have 17 years left. A household may want to replace income until the children are grown. A funeral expense could arise after either of those periods ends. Put a separate dollar amount and time frame next to each line.

## Compare the policy features that matter to those expenses

| Feature | What to ask about term life | What to ask about final expense |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coverage duration | When does the term end? Can it be renewed or converted under the contract? | What keeps coverage in force? Are there contract provisions that can reduce benefits? |
| Premium | How long is the quoted premium in effect? What happens at renewal? | What payments are required and for how long? |
| Benefit | How much is payable while the policy is active? | How much is payable in early policy years and afterward? |
| Application | What information and exams does this insurer require? | What health questions or underwriting apply to this policy? |

A renewal extends coverage under the contract’s renewal rules. A conversion can let you move eligible term coverage to a permanent policy under the contract’s terms. Check the deadline and ask what the new premium would be before relying on either feature. [Washington Office of the Insurance Commissioner life insurance guide](https://www.insurance.wa.gov/sites/default/files/2025-03/life-insurance-guide.pdf)

## Run the numbers using an example

Suppose you want $160,000 available to help replace income and pay debts while you’re working. Separately, you want $9,000 available for a funeral even if you live past the end of that period. Start by marking when the larger need is expected to end. Then see how existing savings or insurance might handle the funeral at that time.

If you already have term insurance, your beneficiary may be able to use proceeds for funeral costs if a claim is payable while it is in effect. The amount earmarked for the funeral should be counted only once. For instance, if the family needs the whole benefit to pay down a mortgage, the same dollars can’t also be used to cover the funeral. [NAIC Life Insurance Buyer’s Guide](https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/publication-lig-lp-consumer-life.pdf)

## Compare each option at the end of the term

Try looking at the plan again from the point when your term policy would end. If you expect to pay off the mortgage that year, you may no longer need the same amount for that debt. If the funeral budget still shows a gap, check how much you would need to build in savings by then or what coverage you could keep beyond that point. Write down the years and amounts so you can compare them against the actual contract.

## Look at what you already have before changing it

Find your policy summary and confirm the death benefit, the current premium, and the date it is due to end or change. Ask the insurer which renewal or conversion options remain available. Make a note of any employer-provided coverage you are counting on and what happens if you leave that job.

If an offer for a new policy arrives, compare it against the current policy before making a decision. A change in health can affect what is available. Keep your current policy active while you verify the effective date and terms of any new contract you plan to use. [NAIC Life Insurance Buyer’s Guide](https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/publication-lig-lp-consumer-life.pdf)

When you know how much you want to protect and for how long, [schedule a consultation](/book) to review available coverage for each expense.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can term life pay for a funeral?**

A beneficiary can generally use a payable death benefit for a funeral. Check whether the policy would still be in effect during the period you want to cover and how much the family needs for other bills.

**Will term insurance renew at the same price?**

Renewal rights and premiums depend on the contract. Ask the insurer for the renewal schedule and deadline before using a current premium to plan future costs.

**Can I keep term life and add final expense insurance?**

You can compare separate policies for separate needs. Consider how much your existing coverage already provides and what total premiums your household can sustain.
