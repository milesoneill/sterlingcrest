## What can funeral flowers cost?

The arrangement you choose makes a real difference. A small gift sent to a home is a different purchase from flowers designed to sit on a casket. Start with the kind of tribute you want rather than one large “flowers” allowance.

[Heaven & Earth Floral in West Palm Beach](https://www.heavenandearthfloral.net/sympathy-flowers) lists these florist's-choice examples. The listed prices were reviewed in October 2026; the page does not give an effective date.

These are specific products from one local florist, not a Florida average or a promise of your final checkout total. Verify delivery, tax, size, and the chosen design before ordering. A larger design or a different florist may have a different price.

You also do not need to buy every type. One family might choose a single casket arrangement. Another might prefer a standing spray at a memorial. Decide which purchase belongs in your household's plan before adding up the options.

## How much does a newspaper obituary cost?

An obituary's length, photo, publication, and print dates can change its cost. A short death notice and a full life story may be sold on different terms, so compare the exact format you intend to use.

For a clear Florida example, [The Wakulla Sun's obituary page](https://thewakullasun.com/send-obituaries/) lists $100 for up to 500 words. Text over that limit costs an extra $25 per 100 words. These are that Wakulla County paper's listed terms, checked on October 27, 2026, not a Palm Beach newspaper quote or a statewide rate.

Under that schedule, a 600-word obituary would cost $125: the $100 base plus $25 for 100 extra words. The page says the staff reviews the word count and sends an invoice. It does not state a photo charge, so this example does not price a photo or other options. It shows the text calculation under one paper's stated terms.

A useful way to control this part of the budget is to draft the text, count the words, and compare the versions you actually want. Keep names, service details, and key family information accurate. Then decide whether the longer life story, a photo, or another print date is worth its added cost, if any, to your family.

## Find out who is collecting each payment

You may order flowers from a florist and buy an obituary directly from a publication. A funeral home may also arrange either purchase for you. The difference matters because the person who supplies the item and the business collecting your payment may not be the same.

The [FTC calls outside-vendor purchases made by a funeral home on your behalf “cash advances”](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/funeral-costs-pricing-checklist). Flowers and obituary notices are examples. Some providers pass along the vendor's cost; others add a service fee. The FTC explains that an added fee must be disclosed in writing.

Keep the outside item and any handling charge separate in the estimate. If the funeral home lists flowers, find out which arrangement the line buys and whether you will pay the florist again. This avoids treating one planned purchase as two expenses or assuming a placeholder covers every design you might choose.

If you choose the $325 casket arrangement instead of the $175 spray, that example subtotal becomes $450. You would be choosing one design in place of the other, not buying both.

Find out whether relatives plan to send their own flowers before deciding what you will buy. Their gift does not need to be part of your own budget. Do not count an obituary twice if the funeral package already includes it.
