**Quick answer:** Plan on roughly $500–$700 in total for funeral flowers. A newspaper obituary may add about $200–$500. These are broad planning estimates, not fixed prices. The location, size of the tribute, publication, and chosen extras can move your total above or below those ranges.

## How much should you budget for funeral flowers?

Set a total budget for the flowers your family wants to buy. You do not need to price every stem or buy every type of arrangement. About **$500–$700 in total** is a useful starting point. [Funeralocity's national cost guide](https://www.funeralocity.com/average-funeral-price) gives that range for flowers purchased by families arranging end-of-life services.

This is a broad estimate. It is not a measured national median or a required amount. A simple tribute can cost less, while large displays can cost more. The number and size of the arrangements, flower choices, season, and delivery all affect the final bill.

Set one limit for the flowers your household wants to fund. Then tell the florist the setting, your preferred style, and that total budget. Let the florist suggest a design that fits. You do not need to choose from every option on display.

Relatives and friends may send their own tributes. Those gifts do not need to be part of your household's flower budget. If several family members want to contribute, agree on one person to place the order so you do not buy the same tribute twice.

## What can an obituary cost?

For a newspaper obituary, **$200–$500** is a broad starting allowance. [Funeral-director guidance published by Neptune Society](https://neptunesociety.com/ask-a-funeral-director/grief-support/how-much-does-an-obituary-cost) gives that range while noting that prices differ around the country. Larger-city newspapers, more space, photos, and extra publication dates can increase the total.

First, decide what you want to share. A brief notice can share the death and service details. A fuller obituary can tell the person's story and name the people and places that mattered to them. An online tribute may be useful if that is how friends and family stay in touch.

Ask for the total cost of the version you want to publish. Find out whether a funeral-home website notice is already included. Newspaper placement may cost extra. You do not need to learn a paper's billing formula to set a budget.

## Put these costs beside the whole funeral budget

Flowers and obituary notices are often outside the funeral home's headline price. They should be part of your plan, but they are only one piece of the total.

For perspective, the [National Funeral Directors Association's 2023 study](https://content.nfda.org/news/media-center/nfda-news-releases/id/8134/2023-nfda-general-price-list-study-shows-inflation-increasing-faster-than-the-cost-of-a-funeral) reported national median costs of **$8,300 for a funeral with viewing and burial** and **$6,280 for one with viewing and cremation**. Those figures exclude cemetery interment, grave markers, and outside purchases such as flowers and obituary notices. They are 2023 benchmarks, not current quotes or complete spending limits.

The [Funeral Consumers Alliance](https://www.funerals.org/get-help/how-to-save-money/how-to-pay-for-a-funeral-or-other-arrangements/) notes that a typical funeral can cost **$10,000 or more**. A useful plan looks beyond one service package. Include the final resting place and the tributes you want. Allow for any gathering your family plans to pay for, too.

Our [funeral expenses checklist](/resources/funeral-expenses-checklist) can help you keep these groups together. If a package already includes a cost, count it once.

## Keep optional spending within a limit you choose

A personal tribute does not need to be an expensive one. Decide what would feel meaningful to your family, then choose where to spend within your limit. You may prefer a shared story and a simple display to a room full of flowers.

Before placing an order, confirm the delivery charge, tax, and any separate handling fee. If the funeral home arranges flowers or publication for you, ask whether its charge is already in the total you have been given.

The [FTC's funeral pricing guide](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/funeral-costs-pricing-checklist) explains that outside purchases made through a funeral home can include a service fee. A total should make clear what you are buying and who will collect payment. Keep an unpaid outside order separate from an amount already paid through the funeral home.

If relatives are sharing expenses, agree on who is responsible for each group of costs. That can prevent a last-minute surprise. It also keeps the focus on the farewell, not a long shopping list.

## Take the next step from a budget to a plan

If you are planning ahead, write down the kind of service you prefer and a broad total you would like to prepare for. Use the estimates above as a starting point. Your choices can change the amounts. You can make a useful plan before selecting every detail.

Then compare that goal with money set aside and any life insurance already in place. Keep funds needed for daily bills and emergencies separate from money you truly intend to leave for final expenses.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is $500–$700 the cost of one flower arrangement?**

No. It is a general estimate for the total flowers a family buys for a funeral or memorial. The actual amount can be lower for a simple tribute or higher for a larger display.

**Are flowers and an obituary included in the national funeral median?**

No. NFDA’s 2023 national funeral medians exclude outside purchases such as flowers and obituary notices, as well as cemetery interment and grave markers. Include those costs in your broader plan.

**Do I need exact funeral prices before scheduling a consultation?**

No. Start with your preferred type of service, a broad planning goal, and a comfortable monthly budget. The complimentary phone consultation can help you compare coverage options while the details are still open.
