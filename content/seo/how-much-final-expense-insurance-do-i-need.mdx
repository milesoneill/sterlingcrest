**Quick answer:** Estimate your final expense insurance need by adding the costs you want covered, then subtracting funds already set aside for them. Use local funeral prices and check existing life insurance first. The remaining gap is a starting point. Compare that amount with policy terms and a premium you can afford to keep paying.

## Start With Your Plans, Not a Standard Benefit Amount

Write down the kind of service you would want: burial, cremation, a memorial, or another plan. Ask a family member to review those wishes with you. A clear plan gives you something specific to price.

You do not have to settle every detail today. Start with your main choices and note any questions. If you want a basic explanation of the policy first, read our [final expense insurance overview](/insurance/final-expense-insurance).

## Get an Itemized Local Estimate

Call a funeral home and ask about the services you have chosen. The FTC explains that funeral providers must give prices by phone when asked. When you visit to discuss arrangements, you have the right to a written General Price List.

Ask what the total includes. Check whether cemetery charges, a marker, flowers, or transport are separate. Use the FTC's funeral pricing checklist to compare the same items at each provider. A package name alone will not show every detail.

Keep each estimate and its date. That makes it easier to update your plan as prices or your wishes change.

## Fill In Your Coverage Worksheet

Copy these lines onto paper or into a note. Enter prices from your own research. Leave an item blank until you can check it.

- Funeral or memorial services you want: $____
- Burial or cremation items outside that estimate: $____
- Other final bills you choose to plan for: $____
- Extra amount you choose for uncertain costs: $____
- Total planned expenses: $____
- Savings reserved for these expenses: minus $____
- Existing insurance you plan to use for them: minus $____
- Remaining gap to discuss with an agent: $____

Check for double counting. A casket listed in a funeral package should not appear as a second cost. Likewise, do not count the same savings twice. Set aside money for other goals before deciding what is available here.

## Read the Result as an Estimate

Here is a made-up example of the math: $12,000 in planned expenses minus $3,000 reserved for them leaves a $9,000 gap. These are sample figures, not funeral prices, a quote, or a suggested policy size.

Your own result may be higher or lower. It does not mean an insurer offers that exact amount or will approve an application. If the result is zero, check your assumptions before buying extra insurance. You may already have enough funds for this goal.

## Check Existing Coverage Before Buying More

Gather your policy statements. Confirm the death benefit, who receives it, and how long the policy lasts. Decide how much of that money is already meant for other family needs.

The NAIC recommends reviewing your current life insurance when you assess a new policy. It also cautions against canceling current coverage before you get the new policy. Ask an agent to explain the terms before you make a change.

For debts or estate questions, check who is responsible with a qualified professional. Do not assume a relative must personally pay every bill you leave behind.

## Compare the Monthly Cost and the Actual Benefit

Bring your worksheet to a licensed agent. Ask for the premium at each available coverage amount you are considering. Look at how that payment fits alongside rent, food, medicine, and other regular bills.

Read the benefit schedule and exclusions too. Ask whether a waiting period or graded benefit would affect the amount paid for a covered claim. Terms and eligibility depend on the policy and insurer. A headline benefit alone does not explain the whole contract.

Our [final expense cost guide](/resources/how-much-does-final-expense-insurance-cost) explains pricing factors. You can also compare [simplified and guaranteed issue policies](/resources/simplified-issue-vs-guaranteed-issue-final-expense-insurance).

## Keep Your Family Involved

Tell the person who may handle arrangements where to find your wishes, estimates, and policy details. Ask the insurer how a beneficiary would report a claim and what documents they may need. Avoid assuming a payment date.

Revisit the worksheet when your plans, savings, or family needs change. A dated record helps you see what changed and why. Keep the old version so you can compare your choices at the next review.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is $10,000 enough final expense insurance?**

It depends on your plans and existing funds. Compare that benefit with your itemized expenses and read the policy terms. A round number alone cannot tell you whether your family would have enough.

**Do I need another policy if I already have life insurance?**

You may not. Review the coverage you have, how long it lasts, and which expenses it is meant to cover. An agent can help you assess a gap before you buy more.

**Should I count all my savings in the worksheet?**

Count only money you intend to reserve for these costs. Keep your emergency needs and other goals in mind. Check that the person handling arrangements would be able to access the funds when needed.

**What if the premium for my target amount is too high?**

Ask an agent to compare available benefit amounts and their premiums. Review your planned expenses and decide which costs matter most. Do not assume you must accept the first quote or buy more coverage than your budget supports.
