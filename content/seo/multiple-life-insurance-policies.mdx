**Quick answer:** Yes. You might keep workplace coverage and an individual contract, or use separate policies for different goals. Each comes with its own cost and terms. A second insurer still reviews your application. Work out the gap in your funeral plan and the combined payments before seeking added coverage.

## More than one policy can serve more than one purpose

One contract may not address every need. A family might have coverage through work alongside an individual policy. Someone who bought coverage for an earlier stage of life may later want to review how funeral costs would be handled.

The [Texas regulator's life insurance guide](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/pubs/consumer/cb018.html) describes group and individual coverage. It also explains why you should review what you own before making changes. That does not mean the same mix is right for everyone. The useful question is what each policy is doing in your plan.

Give each one a plain purpose. It might help with funeral costs, help a spouse with bills, or address another need you can name. If you cannot explain why an added policy belongs in the plan, pause before adding another bill. A second contract is useful only if its purpose and terms make sense alongside the first.

## Confirm what is already in place before adding a new amount

An old policy page may not tell you the current status of coverage. A workplace benefit may have its own terms. Before adding the amounts, check which policies are active and who they cover. Find out how long the coverage lasts under its terms.

Do not add amounts for different insured people into one person’s funeral plan. If one policy insures you and another insures your spouse, the combined figure does not describe what would be payable when either one of you dies. Check the insured name before adding any amounts.

Keep unknown details marked as unknown. An old page is not proof of current coverage. Nor does a missing page show that a policy ended. Our guide to [reviewing existing insurance before burial coverage](/resources/existing-life-insurance-before-burial-coverage) offers a way to organize the starting information.

For a funeral-cost plan, keep insurance apart from money saved for the same bills. The [New York regulator's consumer information](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/consumers/life_insurance) explains how needs and other resources affect the amount of coverage to consider. Use that idea to find the gap. A different name alone is not a reason to add another policy.

## An additional policy still has its own application

A new insurer may ask about insurance you already own and other coverage you are applying for. Give full, truthful answers. Having coverage elsewhere does not remove the new company's review or its limits on what it will offer.

How much a company will offer depends on its rules and your facts. This article cannot promise how many policies or how much coverage you can get. A quote is a starting point for the option being discussed, not permission to stack any amount of coverage.

Make clear whether you plan to keep the existing policy or replace it. Those are different choices. The company needs to know which one you plan to make. State your actual plan on the form. Be clear even if you are still comparing choices and have not decided to buy.

## Adding coverage and replacing coverage are different decisions

Keeping one policy while buying another can leave both sets of payments in the household budget. Replacing a policy means giving up existing coverage as part of a different decision. Do not mix up the two because a new payment looks attractive on its own.

Before a replacement, understand what would be lost and what the new policy would provide. The insurer or a licensed agent can explain the steps and how the contracts differ. Do not cancel a policy just because you have a new quote or have sent in a form to apply.

For an added burial policy, consider the total bill after the purchase. Two bills that each seem easy to pay can become a strain together. Our [coverage-needs guide](/resources/how-much-final-expense-insurance-do-i-need) can help connect the amount you want to plan for with what is already in place.

## Make several policies easier for your family to find

When you deal with more than one company, a clear record helps. Identify each insurer, the insured person, the policy number, and where current details can be found. Keep work benefits apart from policies you own. Then relatives can see who to call for each.

Beneficiary choices also belong to each policy. Do not assume that updating one company's record changes another contract. You might name the same person on each, or choose different people. Each record needs to match the choice you made for that policy.

A short cover sheet can tie the plan together. It does not replace any of the contracts. Label the purpose of each policy and the date its status was checked. That gives your family a practical starting point and helps you review whether an added policy still fills the need you had in mind.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does a second policy automatically replace my first one?**

No. A new policy and a decision to end old coverage are separate. State your plan clearly when you apply and check what steps are needed. Do not treat a new quote as notice that the old policy has changed.

**Should I add the coverage amounts together?**

Only after confirming the policies are active and understanding their terms. A simple total can hide differences in duration, benefits, or who is insured. Use facts you have checked to work out what funeral costs still need a plan.

**Can I get a quote while keeping my current policy?**

You can discuss an added policy without deciding to cancel existing coverage. Explain what you own, what you want to keep, and the need you are trying to address. Any offer depends on the new insurer’s rules.
