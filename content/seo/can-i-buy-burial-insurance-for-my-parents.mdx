**Quick answer:** You may be able to buy burial insurance for a parent when they take part and the insurer approves the application. Ask the insurer to confirm the consent, ownership, and insurable-interest rules for your situation. Available options depend on your parent’s age, health, state, and the policy’s requirements.

## Start with your parent’s agreement

Helping a parent buy coverage is a choice to make together. Tell them what you hope the policy would help with. Make sure they want to explore it.

Agreeing to discuss options is different from agreeing to apply. Your parent should know what the policy offers and take part in the steps the insurer requires.

For example, Florida has a rule about written consent. Under [Florida Statutes section 627.404](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/627.404), the person being insured generally must apply for or agree in writing to an individual life policy. This rule concerns people who can legally enter a contract. There are exceptions, and other states have their own rules. Ask the insurer what applies to your parent.

You can help compare options and make a list of questions. Do not pretend to be your parent, sign their name, or try to buy coverage in secret. For help with the first family discussion, see [how to talk to parents about burial insurance](/resources/talk-to-parents-about-burial-insurance).

## What does insurable interest mean?

You may hear the term **insurable interest**. It means having a valid reason to insure someone else’s life. Your ties to that person matter.

The [National Association of Insurance Commissioners](https://content.naic.org/consumer/life-insurance.htm) says immediate family members generally have an insurable interest. That does not settle every part of an application. The insurer still needs to review the proposed plan.

Ask how this rule applies to you and your parent. Explain why you want the policy. For example, you may plan to pay for the funeral and want help with that cost. Being their child does not mean you can skip the other steps.

## Choose the owner, payer, and beneficiary

These roles have different purposes. Agreeing to pay the bill does not settle who will own the policy.

- **The insured person:** Your parent, whose life the policy would cover.
- **The policy owner:** The person who holds the policy and has the rights set out in its terms.
- **The premium payer:** The person who makes the payments. A premium is the cost of the policy.
- **The beneficiary:** The person or people named to receive a death benefit if it is payable under the policy.

The [Texas Department of Insurance’s life insurance guide](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/pubs/consumer/cb018.html) explains what a beneficiary does. Ask your specialist to walk through all four roles. Have them confirm which setup the insurer allows.

For example, you might want to pay while your parent keeps control of the policy. Say so before you apply. Do not assume that paying the bill makes you the owner.

## Choose a goal and a payment your family can maintain

Start with the expenses you want to plan for. Would your parent prefer burial or cremation? Is there existing insurance or money already set aside? What part of the expected cost remains uncovered?

Local funeral prices can help you estimate [the amount you want to plan for](/resources/how-much-final-expense-insurance-do-i-need). The [FTC’s funeral pricing checklist](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/funeral-costs-pricing-checklist) separates funeral costs into basic services, other services and merchandise, and cash advances. Check what a funeral provider’s total includes instead of assuming it covers every expense. You can still begin an insurance consultation without collecting prices or completing a worksheet.

Next, discuss the ongoing payment. If you will pay, consider whether it fits alongside your own expenses. If siblings will contribute, agree on who will manage the bill and how you will handle a missed contribution.

A useful question is: “Could we still make this payment during an expensive month?” Compare coverage with that answer in mind.

## Compare the actual policy before applying

Ask for an explanation of each available option in plain language. A shared label such as burial insurance does not tell you every term of a policy.

Use these questions during the call:

- Who can own this policy, and what must my parent complete?
- What is the proposed coverage amount and payment schedule?
- Are premiums fixed, or can they change?
- What benefits apply at the start, and are there any limits or exclusions?
- What health questions, records, or other checks are required?
- What must happen before coverage takes effect?

Write the answers beside each option so you are comparing the same points. If an answer is unclear, ask the specialist to show where it appears in the policy information.

Review any existing coverage before considering a change. Do not cancel a current policy just because you have requested a quote or submitted a new application.

## A simple way to move from questions to an application

Begin with a conversation about your parent’s goals, age, state, and a comfortable budget. Ask what information is needed for an initial discussion and what is required only if they choose to apply.

Then take these steps:

1. Confirm that your parent wants to explore coverage.
2. Explain the proposed owner, payer, and beneficiary arrangement.
3. Compare the options available for their circumstances.
4. Have your parent review and complete their required application steps.
5. Review the insurer’s decision and issued policy before treating the process as complete.

Keep room for questions throughout. An application is a request for coverage; the insurer determines whether it can issue the policy and on what terms. A quote alone is not confirmation that insurance is in force.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I get burial insurance for my parent without telling them?**

Plan to involve your parent and obtain the required consent. Do not use their personal information to apply secretly or complete their signatures. Ask the insurer what participation is required for the proposed policy.

**Can I pay for my parent’s policy?**

Ask whether the insurer permits the payment arrangement you have in mind. Confirm the payer and owner separately, including who receives notices and handles payment changes. Do not assume paying the bill gives you control of the policy.

**How much does burial insurance for a parent cost?**

There is no single price for every parent. Age, health, tobacco use, coverage amount, and the policy’s features can affect the premium. Ask a licensed specialist to compare the available options for your parent and a payment that fits your family’s budget.

**What if my parent cannot understand or complete an application?**

Pause before applying. Explain the situation to the insurer and seek appropriate legal guidance if needed. Do not assume that being their child or holding a power of attorney meets every consent or signature requirement.
