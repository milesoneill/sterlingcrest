## Can You Get Final Expense Insurance With Heart Disease?

Yes. Heart disease doesn't automatically block you from coverage.

Your options depend on your specific condition, how well you manage it, and which carrier reviews your application. Some insurers specialize in cardiovascular conditions and offer better terms than others.

Two paths exist. Simplified issue policies ask health questions but skip the medical exam. If your heart disease is stable and well-managed, you may qualify for faster approval and immediate coverage. Guaranteed issue policies accept everyone regardless of health. If your condition is serious or uncontrolled, guaranteed issue ensures acceptance but includes a two- to three-year waiting period before full benefits kick in.

## How Heart Disease Affects Your Eligibility

Carriers evaluate several factors during underwriting.

They look at your diagnosis: coronary artery disease, congestive heart failure, arrhythmia, valve disease, or history of heart attack or stroke. Each carries different risk. They assess control. Stable conditions managed with medication and regular monitoring get better terms than uncontrolled or worsening symptoms.

Your medications matter. Taking prescribed drugs as directed shows active management. Recent hospitalizations count. ER visits, overnight stays, or cardiac procedures in the past 12 to 24 months can affect simplified issue eligibility. Timing and severity of major events matter too. A heart attack five years ago with no complications is viewed differently than one six months ago.

Underwriting varies by carrier. One insurer may decline an applicant with atrial fibrillation. Another may offer simplified issue at standard rates. This is why working with an independent brokerage helps. We direct your application to the carrier most likely to offer favorable terms based on your specific profile.

## Simplified Issue Policies for Stable Heart Disease

Simplified issue policies ask health questions. No medical exam, blood work, or physician records required.

You may qualify if your heart disease is stable. Carriers look for applicants who take medications as prescribed, see their cardiologist regularly, haven't been hospitalized recently for cardiac reasons, and have no recent ER visits related to their heart.

Benefits: approval often comes within days. Many policies provide immediate coverage or very short waiting periods. Your beneficiaries receive the full death benefit if you pass away shortly after the policy starts. Premiums run lower than guaranteed issue because the carrier assumes less risk by asking health questions.

Carriers use different guidelines. One may ask about hospitalizations in the past 12 months. Another asks about 24 months. One may accept applicants with a heart attack history if it occurred more than two years ago. Another requires five years. This variation is why comparing multiple carriers matters. An independent brokerage identifies which insurers are most likely to approve your application based on your specific condition and history.

## Guaranteed Issue: Coverage Without Health Questions

Guaranteed issue policies accept all applicants within the age range. No health questions asked.

If you have serious or uncontrolled heart disease (recent heart attack, advanced congestive heart failure, multiple hospitalizations, unstable angina), guaranteed issue ensures you won't be denied. Acceptance is guaranteed if you fall within the carrier's age limits, typically 50 to 85.

Trade-offs exist. Most include a waiting period, often two to three years. If you pass away during that time from natural causes, your beneficiaries typically receive premiums paid plus interest, not the full face amount. After the waiting period ends, the full benefit is paid regardless of cause. Premiums run higher than simplified issue because the carrier assumes greater risk without evaluating health.

Guaranteed issue is designed for people who can't qualify for simplified issue due to serious conditions. It provides peace of mind that your family will receive financial help for final expenses, even if your heart disease is advanced.

## How Heart Disease Affects Your Rates

Heart disease can increase premiums. Rates vary widely.

Carriers price based on condition type and severity, your age, coverage amount, tobacco use, and overall health. A 65-year-old with controlled hypertension and no heart attack history generally pays less than a 75-year-old with congestive heart failure and recent hospitalizations.

Different carriers price the same applicant differently. One insurer may specialize in cardiovascular conditions and offer competitive rates. Another may charge significantly more or decline coverage. This happens because carriers use different underwriting models and risk assessments.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Will I be automatically denied if I have heart disease?**

No. Many carriers offer coverage designed for applicants with cardiovascular conditions. Your eligibility depends on condition type, how well it's controlled, and which carrier evaluates your application. Guaranteed issue policies accept all applicants regardless of health.

**Do I need a medical exam?**

Most final expense policies don't require one. Simplified issue asks health questions but skips exams, blood work, or physician records. Guaranteed issue requires no health questions and no exam.

**What if I've had a heart attack?**

You can still get coverage. If your heart attack occurred several years ago and your condition is stable, you may qualify for simplified issue. If it was recent or your condition is unstable, guaranteed issue ensures acceptance. Carriers have different timeframes and guidelines for heart attack history.

**Can I get coverage with a pacemaker or defibrillator?**

Yes. Many applicants with pacemakers or ICDs qualify. If your device functions properly and your condition is stable, you may qualify for simplified issue. If your condition is more serious, guaranteed issue is available.

**How much will it cost?**

Premiums vary based on age, condition type and severity, coverage amount, and carrier. Comparing multiple carriers helps identify competitive rates. An independent brokerage provides personalized quotes based on your circumstances.

**What if my heart disease worsens after I buy a policy?**

Your coverage and premium are locked in once your policy is issued. Changes to your health after that don't affect your coverage or rates. Your beneficiaries receive the full death benefit as long as premiums are paid and the policy stays active.
