**Quick answer:** Check the policy’s written effective date and ask the insurer whether coverage is in force. The day you apply, the day the policy is issued, and the day protection begins can differ. Then check the benefit schedule to see what would be payable if death occurs in an early policy year.

## Find the date that governs the coverage

An effective date is the date coverage begins under the policy or applicable agreement. The application date shows when you asked for insurance. The issue date shows when the insurer issued the policy. These dates can serve different purposes. Find the effective date in the policy documents and ask the insurer whether any steps remain before the policy is in force. In force means the coverage is active under its terms. [NAIC Life Insurance Buyer’s Guide](https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/publication-lig-lp-consumer-life.pdf)

If a quote shows a proposed start date, mark it as a date to confirm. Keep the actual policy and the insurer’s confirmation together when you receive them.

## Use a simple date checklist

| Date or status | Where to look |
| --- | --- |
| Application submitted | Application copy or confirmation |
| Policy issued or delivered | Issued contract or delivery notice |
| Effective date | Policy documents and insurer confirmation |
| Initial premium received and applied | Payment record and insurer billing confirmation |
| Policy in-force status | Ask the insurer to confirm directly |
| End of any graded period | Benefit schedule and relevant policy anniversary |

Put the records in order and ask the insurer to explain any dates you cannot reconcile. If the first scheduled bank draft was declined or reversed, ask whether the payment is still due and how that affects coverage. Keep a note of the answer and obtain updated confirmation if you need to make another payment.

## Read any temporary coverage agreement separately

Some applications may come with a conditional receipt or a temporary insurance agreement when payment is collected. A conditional receipt describes what must happen for any interim protection to apply. If you receive one, check the conditions, limits, and dates on that document. Ask the insurer to confirm how it applies to your application. The rules for a particular form can vary by state and insurer. [New York Department of Financial Services individual life application outline](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2024/07/indiv_life_application_outline_20240708.pdf)

For example, say you applied on November 10 and see a withdrawal on November 12. Rather than using either date to mark the policy active, ask whether the payment was received and applied and what the insurer shows as the effective date. Save the response alongside the issued policy.

## Separate the start date from the amount payable

A policy can be in force and still have a graded death benefit, which can limit the amount payable for certain deaths during early policy years. If you see a graded schedule, ask what amount applies in each year and which dates start and end that period. Check whether an exception or exclusion affects a particular situation. [New York Department of Financial Services consumer guide to life insurance](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/consumers/life_insurance)

Suppose a policy starts in November, and the family wants to know what would be payable the following January. Start with whether coverage is in force. Then use the schedule to identify which policy year January falls in and which benefit rules apply. Ask the insurer to confirm that interpretation in writing.

## Check the status after a weekend or holiday

If your first payment is scheduled for a weekend or bank holiday, check what the insurer’s records show after the payment posts. Ask whether it needs anything else, such as a signed delivery acknowledgment or an updated payment method. Keep the representative’s written response or a dated note of your call. When a family member checks the file later, the dates will tell them which confirmation was current.

## Confirm the transition if you’re replacing coverage

Find out when the old policy is paid through and confirm what is in force with both insurers. The NAIC recommends comparing the old and new contracts and keeping the current policy in place until you receive the new one. Before making a change, verify when the new coverage takes effect and review any new early benefit limits. [NAIC Life Insurance Buyer’s Guide](https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/publication-lig-lp-consumer-life.pdf)

If you’re trying to compare the timing and benefits of available options, [schedule a consultation](/book) and bring the policy date and premium questions you want to ask.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Am I covered as soon as I submit the application?**

Ask the insurer what, if any, coverage applies while it reviews your application. If you received a temporary agreement, confirm its conditions and dates in writing.

**Does an automatic bank draft prove the policy is active?**

A withdrawal is evidence of a payment transaction. Ask the insurer whether it applied that payment and whether it currently shows the policy as in force.

**What if my policy dates disagree?**

Send the insurer the date shown on each document and ask which governs the start of coverage. Request a written answer or updated copy if the insurer corrects a document.
