**Quick answer:** Direct cremation can cost much less than cremation with a viewing or funeral service. One West Palm Beach provider, Northwood Funeral Home, lists direct cremation at $1,285–$1,685 on its March 16, 2026 price list. That is a provider example, not a Florida average. The total for your plans depends on the service and any separate charges.

## Compare the same kind of cremation

Direct cremation has no viewing or ceremony with the body present. A cremation funeral can add a viewing and body care. A ceremony and other services can add to the cost, too. A memorial gathering after cremation is another choice. These arrangements should not be treated as one price category.

The [FTC's description of funeral types](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/types-funerals) explains the difference. Start with the plan you want to price. Is it cremation alone, a gathering later, or a service with the body present? You can change your mind, but the provider needs to know which plan to price.

If two advertisements have very different prices, look at this question first. One might be for direct cremation while the other includes time at a funeral home. The lower number alone does not explain why the prices differ.

## Use actual prices as a starting point

[Northwood's 2026 price list](https://northwoodfh.com/services/) offers a $1,285 online direct-cremation option and a $1,685 option with personal guidance. Both include a provider-supplied alternative container. Even without a ceremony, the way you make plans can affect the price.

For another local example, [Cremation With Care's Palm Beach County list](https://www.cremation-with-care.com/FL/Palm.Beach.County/general-price-list), effective September 22, 2026, shows $995 with a purchaser-supplied alternative container or $1,290 with its container. The crematory fee is included. The list shows separate charges for medical-examiner approval and certified death certificates.

These are examples from two providers, not a survey or recommendation. Use them to recognize the questions behind a price. Your provider can confirm which charges apply to the person and the plan.

## Check five items before relying on a total

Ask for the full price of the plan you described. Then check these points:

- **Transfer:** Is collection included, and is there a distance or after-hours charge?
- **Care:** How much refrigeration or other care is included before cremation?
- **Container:** Does the total include the container used for cremation?
- **The process:** Is the crematory charge already part of the total?
- **Afterward:** What happens to the ashes, and are an urn, delivery, or a gathering separate?

A fee shown elsewhere on a general list may already be inside the package. Do not add it again just because you can see a separate price. At the same time, do not assume an urn or memorial event is included because the advertisement uses the word cremation.

The [FTC's phone-shopping guide](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/shopping-funeral-services-phone-or-online) recommends asking what is included and whether there are other costs for items such as transport and death certificates. Ask for the answer in terms of your own plan.

## See how small extras change a real example

Cremation With Care lists medical-examiner approval at $95 and certified death certificates at $15 each. Using its $1,290 container-included option, adding that approval charge and five certificates would total $1,460: $1,290 + $95 + $75.

We added the listed charges for this example. It is not a new provider package or a full quote for every family. An urn, shipping, or a gathering could add to the cost. Other charges may apply. Its list also describes weight limits and an additional charge for some cases.

This is the useful test for any estimate: can you explain how the total was reached? If a figure is still unknown, keep it outside the confirmed subtotal rather than quietly treating it as zero. You can then see which answer is needed next.

## Choose where a gathering fits

A family can discuss the gathering separately from the cremation process. For example, you may want a small event at home later, or you may want the funeral home to arrange a memorial service. Those choices create different spending questions.

Write the event as its own group in your budget. Think about the place, food, travel, and any items you want to display or share. Use only what fits your family. A memorial does not need to copy someone else's arrangements to be meaningful.

If you are considering a viewing before cremation, ask for a price for that specific plan. Do not assume a direct-cremation package can be changed into a viewing package by adding just one small charge.

## Connect the plan with a funding goal

Once you know the kind of plan you want, discuss the amount you hope to prepare for. Include any gathering or other costs you want your family to have funds for, rather than treating the cremation figure as the whole goal.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does direct cremation include a funeral service?**

Direct cremation is a plan without a viewing or ceremony with the body present. A memorial event or service can raise separate costs. Ask for the price of the arrangement you actually want, rather than comparing package names alone.

**Is the lowest advertised price necessarily the complete amount?**

Check the container, transport, care, crematory fee, and any separately listed charges. The lower-priced option may assume that the family supplies an item or uses a different arrangement method. Read the stated conditions before comparing totals.

**Can I discuss burial insurance while we are still deciding?**

Yes. The call can start with the broad plan and a comfortable budget. You do not need a provider quote to schedule, and the conversation does not commit you to buying coverage.
