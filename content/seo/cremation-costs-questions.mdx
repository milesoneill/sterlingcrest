**Quick answer:** Direct cremation commonly falls around $1,000–$3,000, depending on the provider and location. A funeral with a viewing and cremation costs more. NFDA reported a $6,280 national median in 2023. Start with the type of service you want, then include any memorial gathering, final resting place, and separate charges in your total budget.

## Start with the total for the type of cremation you want

A useful general budget for **direct cremation is about $1,000–$3,000**. Cremation provider [After's cost guide](https://www.after.com/articles/direct-cremation-cost), updated in March 2026, gives that range. It is broad provider guidance, not a fixed national price; some providers and areas fall outside it.

Direct cremation is different from a funeral that includes a viewing before cremation. The [National Funeral Directors Association's 2023 study](https://content.nfda.org/news/media-center/nfda-news-releases/id/8134/2023-nfda-general-price-list-study-shows-inflation-increasing-faster-than-the-cost-of-a-funeral) reported a **$6,280 national median for a funeral with viewing and cremation**. That figure includes services and merchandise beyond the cremation process itself. It excludes cemetery interment, markers, and cash advances such as flowers and an obituary.

These figures help you choose where to start. They should not be combined or treated as two prices for the same arrangement. You may want a simple cremation and a family gathering later. Include both in your overall goal.

## Decide whether you want a viewing, a memorial, or both

Direct cremation takes place without a viewing or ceremony with the body present. A memorial gathering can happen afterward. A funeral with a viewing before cremation needs more services. These include care and a place for the viewing.

The [FTC's guide to funeral types](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/types-funerals) explains these distinctions. They matter more than a package name. Two offers may both say cremation. One may cover the process alone, while the other includes a service at the funeral home.

Think about how your family wants to gather. A small event at home and a funeral-home ceremony create different budgets. Neither needs to copy someone else's service. Choose the kind of farewell you want first so the prices you compare describe the same plan.

## Find out what the advertised total includes

Use the general range to begin, then confirm the complete amount for your arrangement. A low advertised figure is useful only when you know what it buys.

Check these main parts of the total:

- Collection and transport to the provider or crematory.
- Care before cremation, including the time allowed.
- The container used for cremation and the crematory charge.
- Return of the cremated remains and the type of container supplied.
- Required paperwork and any separately charged permits or certificates.

The [FTC's phone-shopping guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/shopping-funeral-services-phone-or-online) recommends asking what is included and what other charges may apply. This is especially useful when comparing an online package with an offer from a funeral home.

A service fee listed elsewhere may already be part of the package. Confirm that before adding it to the total again. Also check whether extra distance, a longer period of care, or shipping would change the price for your situation.

## Add the other parts of the farewell you want

The cremation bill may not be the whole amount your family needs. A memorial service, flowers, obituary, keepsake urn, travel, or a final resting place can create separate costs. Include only the choices you want to fund, but do not leave them out simply because they are billed by another business.

For a small gathering, consider the place, refreshments, and any paid help. If you want burial of the cremated remains or placement in a cemetery niche, include those arrangements in the plan as well. The direct-cremation range does not include that purchase.

Keep a short budget with three groups: cremation, the gathering, and the final resting place. Mark anything already included in a package. This gives you a total goal. You do not need to price every possible extra.

Our [funeral expenses checklist](/resources/funeral-expenses-checklist) can help you organize the broader plan. It is also fine to leave an optional choice open while you decide what matters most.

## Compare complete plans without adding unwanted services

Ask more than one provider for the total cost of the same arrangement. Say whether you want direct cremation, a viewing, or a memorial service. That makes the comparison fair.

Then look at the differences that would matter to your family. Is collection included? How will the remains be returned? Does the service you want fit the package? A slightly lower headline price may be less useful if it leaves out something you have already decided you need.

You can keep a meaningful farewell within a limit you choose. Separate a must-have from something you would enjoy if it fits. If the total is too high, adjust the plan before agreeing to it, rather than assuming every suggested extra belongs in your arrangements.

## Connect the total with a funding goal

Once you have a broad plan, compare its total with money set aside and any life insurance you already have. Think about the whole amount you want to help your family prepare for, rather than using the cremation price alone.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does a $1,000–$3,000 direct cremation budget cover a funeral service?**

That range is broad guidance for direct cremation, not a full funeral with a viewing. A memorial event, upgraded urn, cemetery arrangement, or separately charged items can increase the total.

**Why is the national $6,280 figure higher?**

NFDA’s 2023 median is for a funeral with a viewing and cremation. It includes more services and merchandise than direct cremation. It is a historical national benchmark, not a current quote for every family.

**Can I discuss burial insurance while we are still deciding?**

Yes. The call can start with a broad goal and a comfortable monthly budget. You do not need a provider quote to schedule, and the conversation does not commit you to buying coverage.
