**Quick answer:** When discussing a final expense insurance quote, ask about the coverage amount, the monthly payment, and the insurer being discussed. Explain what you want to protect and what fits your budget. You can ask these questions during a phone call. Notes are optional; you can start with your questions.

## Start with what matters to your family

Before focusing on a price, think about what you would like the insurance to help your family with. You might want to discuss funeral expenses or other costs you have in mind. Explaining your goal gives the conversation a useful starting point.

Next, think about a monthly payment that would feel comfortable alongside your regular expenses. You can share a range rather than one exact figure. Your priorities help the agent understand what you want to explore.

If you are unsure where to begin with an amount, our [coverage-needs guide](/resources/how-much-final-expense-insurance-do-i-need) can help you organize your thoughts. You can also discuss that question during your consultation.

## Work through these six checks

Use this list during your phone conversation or read through it beforehand. Check an item when you feel clear about it. The list is optional; it is here to make the discussion easier to follow.

- [ ] **What do I want to help my family cover?** Describe the expenses or family needs you have in mind. Ask: “How can we use that goal as a starting point?”
- [ ] **What monthly payment feels comfortable?** Consider your regular expenses and share a budget range. Ask: “Can we discuss options around this monthly budget?”
- [ ] **What coverage amount are we discussing?** Keep this separate from the monthly payment. Ask: “Can you repeat the coverage amount for this option?”
- [ ] **What is the monthly premium?** Premium means the payment for the insurance. Ask: “What monthly payment goes with that coverage amount?”
- [ ] **Which insurer is being discussed?** Ask for the company’s name so you can keep track of the option. Ask: “Which insurance company is this quote for?”
- [ ] **What would I like explained?** Ask about anything that is unclear and the next step you are considering. Ask: “Can you explain that part in everyday language?”

## Ask questions in plain language

You do not need to know insurance terms to have a useful conversation. A clear question such as “Is that the monthly payment?” can be more helpful than trying to use an unfamiliar phrase. Ask the agent to repeat an amount whenever you need.

It can also help to say the information back in your own words: “So we are discussing this coverage amount at this monthly payment. Have I understood that correctly?” That helps you both follow the same option.

A few useful questions are:

- How does this amount relate to the family goal we discussed?
- What monthly payment goes with this option?
- Could you explain that term with a simple example?
- What would the next step involve if I want to move forward?

## Keep a few notes if they help

Some people like to jot down a few details during a call. Others prefer to focus on the conversation. If you take notes, label the coverage amount and the monthly payment.

The example below shows one way to organize your own thoughts. It is an optional note-taking format, not something you need to complete or send to Sterling Crest.

| Item | My note | Helpful question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Family goal | I want to discuss funeral expenses. | What amount could we use as a starting point? |
| Monthly budget | I have a comfortable payment range in mind. | Can we discuss options around this budget? |
| Quote details | I want to remember the amount and monthly payment. | Can you repeat those two figures together? |

## Avoid three easy mix-ups

- **Mixing up coverage and premium.** The coverage amount and the payment are different figures. Keep the labels together when you repeat them or take notes.
- **Starting with a payment that stretches your budget.** Share a comfortable range at the start. A useful conversation considers your household expenses along with what you want to protect.
- **Feeling you need to understand everything before calling.** Questions are a normal part of discussing insurance. You can begin with a general goal and ask for help understanding the options being discussed.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need a quote before scheduling a consultation?**
No. You can start with your questions and what you want to accomplish. The phone conversation gives you a chance to discuss your goals, budget, and insurance options with Sterling Crest. There is no need to get a quote elsewhere first.

**What if I do not know how much coverage to ask about?**
Start by explaining the expenses or family needs you have in mind. You do not need to arrive with a perfect number. You can discuss a coverage amount alongside a monthly payment that feels comfortable for you.

**How should I explain my monthly budget?**
A simple range is a useful starting point. Think about your regular expenses and what you can comfortably set aside for insurance. Share that range early so the conversation stays focused on what works for your household.

**What if I do not understand an insurance term?**
Ask for it to be explained in everyday language. You can also repeat back what you understood and ask whether you have it right. A question about the coverage amount, monthly premium, or next step belongs in the conversation.

**Do I need to prepare anything for the phone call?**
You can begin with your goals, a general idea of your monthly budget, and any questions. Notes are optional. You do not need a quote or a completed checklist to speak with Sterling Crest.

**What happens after we discuss my options?**
You can ask any remaining questions and decide on your next step. Sterling Crest consultations are complimentary and no-obligation. If you would like to move forward, ask the agent to explain what the application process involves.
