**Quick answer:** Start with the insurer, quote date, proposed coverage amount, payment amount and frequency, and the written terms supplied. Use the checklist below to spot missing details and prepare focused questions. Keep the full quote beside your notes so you can ask about the same offer, rather than relying on a price remembered from a call.

## Start with the complete quote

Gather the email, document, or message you actually received. Keep the date and the name of the person who supplied it. If you only have a price from a phone call, write that down and ask what written material you can review.

Keep an existing policy in a separate folder if you want to discuss it too. That makes it easier to distinguish what you already own from a new offer. Your goal at this stage is a clearer understanding, without assuming that buying or replacing coverage is the next step.

## Work through these six checks

Use a check mark once you have located the detail and understand what it describes. Leave a row open when you need an answer. A blank is a useful next question, not a reason to guess.

- [ ] **Who supplied the quote?** Record the insurer named on the material and the agent or brokerage contact. Ask: “Who can answer questions about this specific offer?”
- [ ] **Which version are you reading?** Save the quote date, document title, and any reference shown. Ask: “Is this the current version of the offer we discussed?”
- [ ] **What amount is proposed?** Find the coverage amount on the quote. Keep it distinct from the payment amount. Ask: “Where is the proposed coverage amount shown in writing?”
- [ ] **What payment is shown?** Record both the amount and the stated payment frequency. Ask: “Does this figure match the payment schedule being described?”
- [ ] **Which written terms came with it?** Keep the complete pages and mark any missing attachment or unclear label. Ask: “Which document explains the term I want to understand?”
- [ ] **What still needs an answer?** List unresolved points and the person or document needed to clarify each one. Ask: “What remains to be confirmed before I decide whether to proceed?”

## Ask questions that lead to clearer answers

Point to the exact line that raised your question. “Can you explain the payment line on page one?” gives the person helping you a clearer starting point than “Is this a good policy?” Ask for a plain-language explanation, then ask where you can find it in the written material.

The Texas Department of Insurance advises shoppers to look beyond price and understand the coverage and cost being discussed. New York’s insurance regulator also encourages readers to ask for explanations of unclear policy wording. Those guides provide consumer background; their state-specific rules do not apply everywhere.

- What family goal does this proposed amount address?
- Which details are shown here, and which still need confirmation?
- Can you explain this term using the document in front of us?
- If I want to discuss another amount, what written information should I request?

## Keep an answer beside each open question

A short question log is easier to use than scattered notes. Keep the original wording alongside the answer, the date, and where the answer came from. That gives you something specific to return to if a later conversation sounds different.

| Item | My note | Next question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Payment schedule | Amount recorded; frequency not yet confirmed. | Which payment frequency does this figure use? |
| Document wording | One label needs an explanation. | Where can I read the full wording behind this label? |
| Family goal | I want to discuss a particular planning expense. | How was the proposed amount chosen for that goal? |

## Avoid three easy mix-ups

- **Keeping only the price.** Save the surrounding written material too. A cropped figure leaves you without the labels and context needed to follow up.
- **Combining different offers in one set of notes.** Give each quote its own date and source. Do not carry an answer from one offer into another unless it has been confirmed there too.
- **Treating a question as an answer.** Mark uncertain details as open. A brochure, a friend’s policy, or a general online guide cannot fill in missing terms for your individual offer.

## Choose a useful next conversation

Circle the two or three unanswered points that matter most to you. You might want help reading a payment line, understanding an unfamiliar term, or relating a proposed amount to your family’s plans. Bring the quote itself so the discussion can stay grounded in what you received.

If you are still working out the amount you want to discuss, our [coverage-needs guide](/resources/how-much-final-expense-insurance-do-i-need) is a useful separate starting point. Keep the question of your planning goal distinct from the details of a particular offer.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What if I have only received a price over the phone?**
Record the amount, who supplied it, and the date. Ask what written details are available and how to get them. You can still prepare questions, but leave anything you have not seen or confirmed marked as open.

**Should I focus on the payment or the coverage amount first?**
Record both, with clear labels. They answer different questions: what amount is proposed and what payment is being quoted. Ask how each relates to your goals rather than judging the offer from one figure alone.

**What should I do if the spoken explanation and document seem different?**
Note the exact line and what you understood from the conversation. Ask the agent or insurer to explain the difference using the current written material. Keep the question open until you have a clear answer you can refer to.

**Can I bring a quote I received elsewhere?**
You can bring the material you want help reading. Keep its source and date visible and highlight your main questions. A review should start with your goals and the actual offer, without assuming you need to make a change.

**Does completing this checklist mean coverage is in place?**
No. This is a tool for organizing a quote discussion. Use the insurer’s actual documents and confirmations to establish the status and terms of any individual policy.

**Do I need to apply during a Sterling Crest consultation?**
No. Consultations are complimentary and no-obligation. You can use the conversation to ask about the information you received and consider what you still need to understand before deciding on a next step.
