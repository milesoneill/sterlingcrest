**Quick answer:** Check the date, insurer name, proposed amount, payment details, and written terms on a final expense quote. Mark anything you cannot find or do not understand. Ask which details still need confirmation. Keep the quote with your notes so you can review the same offer during your next conversation.

## Start with the quote you actually received

Put the date and the name of the person who supplied the quote at the top of your notes. Save the full document, not just a cropped price. Record the insurer named on it. If all you have is a phone estimate, ask what written details are available before treating it as a complete offer.

## Separate what is shown from what is assumed

Make two columns: written details and open questions. Copy the proposed amount and payment details only if they appear on the page. Leave blank spaces where details are missing. A short label can raise a question without answering it. Avoid filling gaps with an ad, a friend’s experience, or a guess about how another contract works.

## Turn unfamiliar terms into clear questions

Circle words you would not feel comfortable explaining to someone else. Ask where each answer appears in the proposed terms. The Texas insurance regulator’s [consumer guide](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/pubs/consumer/cb018.html) is a useful starting point for general terms. It does not show the details of your own offer or make Texas rules apply in another state.

## Keep a simple record of each response

Add an answer date beside each open point. Note who responded and whether there is a document you can save. The New York regulator’s [life insurance guide](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/consumers/life_insurance) also offers consumer background. Use it to frame questions, while asking the insurer or licensed professional about the actual terms relevant to you.

## Choose the next conversation around the gaps

Before you book a discussion, pick the two or three issues that matter most to your family. You might want a clearer explanation of a payment line or help reading a proposed amount. Use the [final expense service page](/services/final-expense) for a next step. Keep the written quote in reach so the conversation can stay specific.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is a price enough to judge a quote?**

A price alone leaves questions unanswered. Keep the rest of the proposed terms and ask what remains to be confirmed.

**What if I received the quote by phone?**

Write down what you heard and when. Ask what written details you can review before making a decision.

**Should I bring an existing quote to a discussion?**

You can bring it if you want help reading it. Mark the parts that matter to you rather than preparing a new set of notes from memory.

**Can this checklist confirm my coverage?**

No. It helps you organize questions. The insurer’s actual documents and decisions determine the details of an individual policy.
