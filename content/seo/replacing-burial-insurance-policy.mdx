**Quick answer:** A proposed burial insurance replacement needs a look at both the old policy and the new offer. Compare what you would pay, what the new contract provides, and what you would give up. Your age and health now may affect a new offer, so don't end old coverage before the new policy is active and you understand its terms.

## Start with the problem you want to solve

A reason to review coverage does not mean you need a new policy. You may want a different payment, a different benefit amount, or a clearer understanding of what you already own. Name that goal before discussing a new policy.

If the monthly bill is hard to manage, find out what choices your current insurer can offer. If your concern is a missing record or an old address for a beneficiary, you may just need to update that record.

A smaller monthly bill may come from a smaller benefit. That might fit a changed need, but it means less coverage at a different cost, rather than the same policy made cheaper. First decide which part of the current arrangement you want to change.

The [Alabama guide to switching policies](https://aldoi.gov/consumers/SwitchPolicy.aspx) says to look closely at a proposed switch and hear from both the current company and the new seller. Your current insurer can explain the features of the policy you already own; the new seller needs to explain the proposed change. The forms and notices needed for a switch depend on the rules that apply to you.

## Understand what you already have

Use the current policy and details from its insurer as the starting point. An old brochure, a memory, or a bank payment cannot explain every term. Confirm the policy's status and what the regular payment applies to.

Note the amount of insurance, the payment schedule, and the people named in the records. If the policy has features or values you do not understand, have the current insurer explain them. A new plan may not keep each feature, even if the benefit amount looks the same.

A policy bought years ago was priced using your age and details at that time. A new insurer uses the facts it needs now, so a new offer may cost more. The old policy may also contain features that are not carried over to the new one. Read both the old contract and the new offer to see what each provides.

Our guide to [reviewing existing insurance before buying burial coverage](/resources/existing-life-insurance-before-burial-coverage) can help establish what is already in place.

## Put the current and proposed policies beside the same questions

Compare the benefit amount, payment, payment schedule, and contract terms. Keep each difference clear rather than calling the policies the same. If the new payment is smaller because the amount of insurance is smaller, make that clear.

Find out what would happen to any values or features in the old policy if you ended or changed it. For example, where a policy has cash value, the amount available on surrender may differ from the total premiums you have paid. Check the value and any charges in that contract rather than treating past payments as money that moves to the new policy.

The [Texas life insurance guide](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/pubs/consumer/cb018.html) explains that a switch can bring costs and a fresh application. A new contract needs its own review. Years spent paying for the old policy do not, by themselves, set the terms of the new one.

A useful review explains what changes and how it would help with your goal. It also explains what you would give up. A lower payment by itself is not the whole analysis.

## Keep the application and cancellation decisions separate

Shopping, applying, receiving an offer, and ending an existing policy are separate actions. Be clear about which one you are taking. Requesting information does not require you to cancel anything.

The new insurer may need more details before it can decide what to offer. An early discussion does not settle that decision. Review the actual offer and effective-date information when it is available.

Be open about the coverage you already own and the change you are considering. Answer the application's questions accurately. If the change needs forms or notices, have the licensed agent explain the steps.

Before ending an old policy, confirm the new coverage is active and understand its terms. Know what ending the old policy would mean. Keep the companies' confirmation details so you can follow what happened. A bank payment or a phone call may not complete every step.

## A review can end with keeping the policy

A comparison is useful even when no purchase follows. You may discover that the current policy still addresses the goal, that a record needs updating, or that a proposed change does not solve the original problem.

If a proposed change leaves an important question unanswered, pause that decision. You can keep reviewing without authorizing cancellation. Be particularly clear about who is arranging each step and how the current insurer will confirm any instruction you eventually give.

Our [annual policy review guide](/resources/annual-final-expense-policy-review) offers a broader way to revisit records and family goals. A decision to switch needs a closer look at both contracts. The point is to understand the change before taking an action that affects existing protection.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does comparing a new quote cancel my current policy?**

A quote request is a shopping step. It is not the same as instructing your current insurer to cancel coverage. Be clear about any action you authorize and confirm policy status with the relevant company.

**Should I tell the new agent that I already have insurance?**

Yes. Give accurate information about your current coverage and the change you are considering. That helps the professional explain the planned change and any process that applies to it.

**Can a review end without buying another policy?**

Yes. Keeping the current coverage, correcting records, or getting more details can be reasonable outcomes of a review. The comparison should address your actual concern, not assume a new purchase is required.
