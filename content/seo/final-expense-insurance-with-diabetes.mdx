## Can I Get Final Expense Insurance if I Have Diabetes?

Yes. Diabetes doesn't disqualify you from Final Expense Insurance. Carriers design policies for people with chronic conditions, including Type 1 and Type 2 diabetes. Your options depend on how well you manage your diabetes, whether you have complications, and which carrier reviews your application.

Final Expense policies use simpler underwriting than traditional life insurance. Many offer simplified issue policies that ask health questions but skip the medical exam. Others provide guaranteed issue policies that accept everyone regardless of health history. The right policy depends on your diabetes profile.

Been declined before? You still have options. This guide explains what underwriters evaluate, which policy types fit which diabetes profiles, and how an independent brokerage finds coverage others miss.

## Why Final Expense Insurance Works for Diabetics

Final Expense Insurance helps families cover funeral, burial, and end-of-life costs—typically $5,000 to $25,000. Smaller coverage amounts let carriers accept applicants with health conditions that would disqualify them from larger policies.

Traditional term or whole life policies demand medical exams, detailed health records, and strict underwriting. A high A1C or diabetic complications often trigger a decline or unaffordable premiums. Final Expense carriers design policies for older adults and people with chronic conditions. They expect diabetes among applicants and price accordingly.

Two structures make this work. Simplified issue policies ask health questions but skip the exam. Guaranteed issue policies eliminate health questions entirely, accepting everyone within the age range. Both provide real death benefits your family can use for funeral expenses, medical bills, or other final costs. Guaranteed issue costs more and often includes a waiting period before full benefits start.

## What Underwriters Evaluate for Diabetics

Carriers assess specific factors to gauge risk. Understanding these helps you predict which policy type you'll qualify for.

**Type 1 vs. Type 2.** Type 1 carries higher risk because it requires insulin and often starts earlier in life. Type 2, especially managed with oral medication or diet, gets more favorable treatment. Some carriers accept well-controlled Type 2 for simplified issue but route Type 1 applicants to guaranteed issue.

**A1C levels.** Your hemoglobin A1C measures average blood sugar over three months. Most simplified issue carriers want A1C below 8.0% or 9.0%. Higher levels suggest poor control and typically trigger a decline or referral to guaranteed issue.

**Complications.** Carriers ask about neuropathy, retinopathy, nephropathy, and cardiovascular problems. History of diabetic foot ulcers, amputations, dialysis, or heart attack narrows your options. Applicants with complications usually need guaranteed issue.

**Medication and compliance.** Carriers check whether you follow your prescribed treatment. Insulin use gets noted. Recent hospitalizations for diabetic complications or patterns of non-compliance raise red flags.

**Duration.** How long you've had diabetes matters less than how well you control it. A 20-year history with no complications shows stability.

Every carrier weighs these differently. One insurer might decline an A1C of 8.5% while another approves it for simplified issue. Comparing multiple carriers is critical.

## Simplified Issue for Well-Managed Diabetes

Simplified issue works best for diabetics whose condition is well-managed with no major complications. These policies ask 5 to 15 health questions about recent hospitalizations, chronic conditions, and medications. No medical exam or blood work required.

If your diabetes is controlled with oral medication, your A1C sits below 8.0%, and you have no complications like kidney disease or neuropathy, you'll likely qualify. Approval comes immediately or within days. Coverage starts when your first premium is paid, with no waiting period.

Premiums run higher than traditional life insurance but lower than guaranteed issue. Rates depend on age, coverage amount, tobacco use, and overall health. A 65-year-old with well-controlled Type 2 might pay $60 to $100 monthly for $10,000 in coverage.

The application is straightforward. Answer health questions honestly. Misrepresenting your diabetes or complications can result in claim denial. The carrier reviews your answers and approves you, declines you, or offers a modified policy. If declined for simplified issue, the same carrier may offer guaranteed issue.

Not all simplified issue policies match. Some carriers show more leniency with diabetes. An independent brokerage submits your information to multiple carriers to find the one most likely to approve you at the best rate.

## Guaranteed Issue: Coverage Without Health Questions

Guaranteed issue policies serve applicants who can't qualify for simplified issue due to serious health conditions—poorly controlled diabetes, Type 1, or diabetic complications. These policies ask no health questions. If you're within the carrier's age range (typically 50 to 85), you're automatically approved.

The trade-off is cost and waiting periods. Guaranteed issue premiums run significantly higher because carriers accept all applicants regardless of health. A 65-year-old might pay $120 to $180 monthly for $10,000 in coverage, roughly double simplified issue rates.

Most guaranteed issue policies include a graded death benefit. If you die from natural causes within the first two or three years, your beneficiaries receive only premiums paid, often with interest. Death from an accident during the waiting period triggers immediate full benefit payment. After the waiting period ends, full death benefit pays regardless of cause.

This structure protects carriers from adverse selection—applicants who apply knowing their health is declining. For you, it means guaranteed issue suits long-term planning rather than immediate coverage needs.

Guaranteed issue isn't a last resort. It's a legitimate option for diabetics with complications who want to ensure their family has funds for funeral and burial costs. Understanding the waiting period and budgeting for higher premiums is key. Some carriers offer shorter waiting periods or modified benefit structures, so comparing options matters even within guaranteed issue.

## How Your Diabetes Profile Determines Your Options

Your diabetes profile determines which policy type you'll likely qualify for.

**Well-controlled Type 2, no complications.** A1C below 8.0%, oral medication or diet management, no neuropathy, kidney disease, or cardiovascular events? You should qualify for simplified issue. Apply to multiple carriers to compare rates.

**Type 2 with higher A1C or minor complications.** A1C between 8.0% and 9.5%, or early-stage neuropathy or retinopathy? Some carriers still offer simplified issue while others decline you. An independent brokerage identifies which carriers show leniency. If simplified issue isn't available, guaranteed issue is your fallback.

**Type 1, well-controlled.** Even with excellent control, Type 1 often disqualifies you from simplified issue because carriers view insulin dependence as higher risk. You'll likely need guaranteed issue. A few carriers offer modified simplified issue for Type 1 diabetics with no complications, but these are rare.

**Diabetes with serious complications.** Diabetic amputation, dialysis, heart attack or stroke related to diabetes, or multiple hospitalizations for diabetic complications? Guaranteed issue is your only realistic option. The upside: guaranteed issue accepts you regardless of these factors.

Apply for the most favorable policy type first. If declined, you haven't lost anything—apply immediately for guaranteed issue. An independent brokerage streamlines this by pre-screening your profile and directing you to carriers most likely to approve you.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Will my insulin use disqualify me from Final Expense Insurance?**

No. Insulin use doesn't automatically disqualify you, but it narrows your options. Most carriers route insulin-dependent diabetics to guaranteed issue rather than simplified issue. A few carriers offer simplified issue for well-controlled Type 1 diabetics with no complications, but these are less common.

**Do I need a medical exam to get Final Expense Insurance with diabetes?**

No. Both simplified and guaranteed issue Final Expense policies require no medical exam. Simplified issue asks health questions on the application. Guaranteed issue asks no health questions at all. This makes Final Expense Insurance accessible to diabetics who might struggle to qualify for traditional life insurance requiring blood work and exams.

**Can I get coverage if I've had diabetic complications like neuropathy or kidney disease?**

Yes, but you'll likely need guaranteed issue. Simplified issue carriers typically decline applicants with serious complications like neuropathy, nephropathy, amputations, or dialysis. Guaranteed issue accepts all applicants within the age range regardless of complications, though it costs more and includes a waiting period.

**How much does Final Expense Insurance cost for diabetics?**

Premiums depend on age, coverage amount, diabetes type, and policy type. A 65-year-old with well-controlled Type 2 might pay $60 to $100 monthly for $10,000 in simplified issue coverage. Guaranteed issue for diabetics with complications typically costs $120 to $180 monthly for the same coverage. Rates vary by carrier.

**What happens if I'm declined for simplified issue?**

If a carrier declines you for simplified issue, you can immediately apply for guaranteed issue. Many carriers offer both policy types, so a decline for one doesn't prevent approval for the other. An independent brokerage helps you pivot to guaranteed issue or identifies other carriers with more lenient simplified issue underwriting.

**Does my A1C level affect my Final Expense Insurance options?**

Yes. Most simplified issue carriers look for A1C below 8.0% or 9.0%, depending on the insurer. Higher A1C suggests poor diabetes control and typically results in a decline for simplified issue, directing you to guaranteed issue instead. If your A1C is borderline, comparing multiple carriers increases your chances of simplified issue approval.
