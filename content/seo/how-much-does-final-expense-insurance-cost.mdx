## How Much Does Final Expense Insurance Cost?

Most people pay between $50 and $300 per month for final expense insurance. Your specific premium depends on several personal factors.

Age drives the biggest differences. Coverage that costs a 55-year-old $60 monthly might cost an 85-year-old $250 for the same death benefit. Health status, coverage amount, gender, tobacco use, and policy type all shape your final rate.

A healthy 60-year-old applying for $10,000 in simplified issue coverage typically pays $75 to $125 per month. By age 75, that same $10,000 policy often costs $150 to $200 monthly. Guaranteed issue policies, which require no health questions, generally cost more than simplified issue coverage for applicants in similar age brackets.

Carriers evaluate applicants differently. Comparing options from multiple insurance companies often reveals meaningful price differences for the same coverage. An independent brokerage can show you rates from several carriers at once, helping you identify competitive pricing based on your age, health, and budget.

## Average Final Expense Insurance Costs by Age

Premium ranges shift noticeably with each decade. These estimates reflect typical monthly costs for simplified issue policies for non-tobacco users in average health:

**Ages 50–59:** $10,000 coverage typically costs $50–$90 per month. $15,000 runs $75–$135. $25,000 ranges from $125–$225 monthly.

**Ages 60–69:** $10,000 coverage generally costs $75–$125 per month. $15,000 runs $110–$185. $25,000 ranges from $180–$310 monthly.

**Ages 70–79:** $10,000 coverage typically costs $125–$200 per month. $15,000 runs $185–$300. $25,000 ranges from $310–$500 monthly.

**Ages 80–85:** $10,000 coverage often costs $200–$300 per month. $15,000 runs $300–$450. $25,000 can reach $500–$750 monthly.

These are illustrative ranges, not quotes. Your actual premium depends on your specific health profile, the carrier you choose, your state, and whether you qualify for preferred or standard underwriting. Women often receive slightly lower rates than men at the same age and health status.

## What Determines Your Final Expense Insurance Premium?

Five key factors shape what you'll pay:

**Age:** Premiums increase with age because the statistical likelihood of a claim rises. A 55-year-old and an 80-year-old buying identical coverage will see dramatically different monthly costs.

**Health and underwriting class:** Carriers assign you to a rate class based on your health history, current conditions, medications, and sometimes a brief phone interview. Preferred or standard health classifications cost less than substandard or guaranteed issue rates. Different carriers weigh conditions differently, so one insurer might offer you a better class than another.

**Coverage amount:** Premiums scale with the death benefit. Doubling your coverage from $10,000 to $20,000 roughly doubles your monthly cost, though per-thousand pricing can improve slightly at higher face amounts.

**Gender:** Women statistically live longer, so they often pay less than men of the same age and health for identical coverage.

**Tobacco use:** Smokers and tobacco users typically pay more. Some carriers distinguish between cigarette smokers and occasional cigar users, while others treat all tobacco use the same. If you've been tobacco-free for 12 months or more, many carriers will offer non-tobacco rates.

## Simplified Issue vs. Guaranteed Issue: How Policy Type Affects Cost

The type of final expense policy you choose has a direct impact on your premium.

**Simplified issue** policies ask health questions but require no medical exam. If you qualify based on your answers, you typically receive immediate full coverage with no waiting period. These policies offer the most competitive rates for applicants in average to good health.

**Guaranteed issue** policies accept all applicants regardless of health, with no questions asked. Because the carrier assumes higher risk, premiums run higher than simplified issue for the same coverage amount. Most guaranteed issue policies also include a graded death benefit, meaning if you pass away from natural causes within the first two or three years, your beneficiaries receive only a return of premiums paid plus interest, not the full death benefit. Accidental death is typically covered immediately.

If you have health concerns, you might assume guaranteed issue is your only option. In practice, many applicants with chronic conditions still qualify for simplified issue coverage, which costs significantly less. Comparing both policy types across multiple carriers helps you find the best combination of price and eligibility for your situation.

## How Coverage Amount Impacts Your Monthly Premium

Final expense policies typically range from $5,000 to $35,000 in coverage, though some carriers offer up to $50,000. Your premium scales directly with the death benefit you choose.

For a 65-year-old in standard health, $5,000 in coverage might cost $40 to $60 per month. $10,000 runs $75 to $120. $15,000 costs $110 to $180. $25,000 ranges from $180 to $300 monthly. Doubling your coverage roughly doubles your premium.

When deciding how much coverage you need, consider average funeral and burial costs in your area, plus any outstanding medical bills, credit card balances, or other debts you'd prefer not to leave behind. Some families add extra coverage to help with estate settlement costs or to leave a small inheritance.

Higher coverage costs more each month, but it also provides more financial protection. Balancing your budget against your family's potential expenses helps you choose a coverage amount that makes sense. You can always start with a smaller policy and add coverage later if your budget allows, though premiums will be based on your age at the time you apply for the additional coverage.

## Good to Know

**Do final expense insurance premiums increase over time?**

Most final expense policies are level premium. Your monthly cost stays the same for life as long as you pay on time. Your rate is locked in at the age you apply. If you buy a policy at 65 and pay $100 per month, you'll still pay $100 per month at age 85.

**Is burial insurance cheaper than final expense insurance?**

Burial insurance and final expense insurance are the same product with different names. Both refer to small whole life insurance policies designed to cover end-of-life expenses. Pricing depends on your age, health, and coverage amount, not the label the carrier uses.

**Why do final expense rates vary so much between insurance companies?**

Each carrier uses its own underwriting guidelines, risk models, and pricing strategies. One insurer might specialize in applicants with diabetes and offer competitive rates for that profile, while another focuses on healthy seniors and prices diabetic applicants higher. Comparing multiple carriers helps you find the one that prices your specific situation most favorably.

**What happens if I stop paying my final expense insurance premium?**

Most policies include a grace period of 30 to 31 days. If you miss a payment, you typically have that window to catch up before the policy lapses. Some whole life policies build cash value over time, and if your policy has accumulated cash value, the insurer may use it to cover missed premiums automatically. If the policy does lapse, you generally lose coverage and any premiums paid, though some carriers offer reinstatement options within a limited timeframe.

**Can I pay for final expense insurance annually instead of monthly?**

Many carriers offer annual, semi-annual, or quarterly payment options, often at a slight discount compared to paying monthly. Paying annually can save 5% to 8% over the course of a year. Your insurance professional can show you the payment frequencies each carrier offers and the cost difference for each option.
