**Quick answer:** Ask the cemetery for the rules for your specific plot. Many cemeteries require an outer container to support the grave, and a grave liner may satisfy the requirement. Before paying for a vault, check which containers the cemetery accepts and get the total for the container, delivery, and placement in writing.

## A vault sits outside the casket

An outer burial container surrounds a casket after it is placed in the grave. A burial vault is one type of outer container. A grave liner is another. Ask the provider what each model includes. A vault typically encloses the casket, while a liner may have a simpler design that meets the cemetery’s requirement to support the ground. The cemetery can explain which designs are allowed for the plot you have.

If you already have a funeral quote, look for separate lines for the casket and the outer container. One price can appear in the funeral home estimate and the other in a separate cemetery estimate. [Federal Trade Commission guidance on outer burial containers](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-funeral-rule)

## Start with the cemetery’s written requirement

The Federal Trade Commission says in most places state or local law does not require an outer burial container. Many cemeteries have their own requirements to help prevent the ground from sinking. Start by asking the cemetery to show you the written rule for your plot or section. It can explain whether you need an outer container and whether a grave liner meets the rule. [FTC Funeral Rule guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-funeral-rule)

Ask which sizes and materials the cemetery accepts. If a funeral director tells you a specific item is required by law or by the cemetery, ask them to identify the requirement in writing. Also check whether the cemetery handles installation or requires another provider. This will tell you whom to call for the actual price.

## Compare containers by the full installed price

The FTC’s Funeral Rule calls for covered funeral providers to disclose the prices for the outer containers they offer. Ask to see the outer burial container price list or the applicable section of the general price list. If the cemetery quotes a separate delivery or placement charge, ask for that figure as well. [FTC guidance on price lists](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-funeral-rule)

| Ask for | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Approved liner and vault models | Shows which products meet the plot’s requirements |
| Container price | Gives you the item cost before other charges |
| Delivery and placement | Shows which services are included and who bills for them |
| Timing and due dates | Helps your family know what is due before burial |
| A combined written estimate | Helps catch an omitted or duplicate charge |

For example, imagine a liner priced at $900 with a $250 placement charge. A vault is $1,650 with placement included. The amounts to compare would be $1,150 and $1,650, assuming both are accepted and the quotes include everything else. Those figures illustrate the math. Ask your local providers for their actual prices.

If you’re planning ahead, carry the installed price of the container you’d choose into your full funeral and cemetery budget. Compare that total with savings and life insurance already set aside for these expenses. If that leaves costs your family would have to fund, the difference can help you decide how much additional burial coverage to compare.

## Put the container on the same page as the other burial costs

The National Funeral Directors Association reported a $8,300 national median for an adult funeral with a viewing and burial in its 2023 study, excluding the vault, cemetery, and other specified costs. Use a dated national benchmark for context, then build your own total from written local estimates. [NFDA 2024 report with 2023 General Price List study results](https://nfda.org/Portals/0/2024_NFDA_Cremation%20and%20Burial%20Report.pdf)

Include the funeral home services, casket, cemetery plot, opening and closing of the grave, and any required outer container. Put delivery or installation beside the item it belongs to. If a cemetery package covers one of these charges, note what is included and who will handle it. The goal is one list your family can read without calling each provider again.

## Ask what changes if you choose a different burial arrangement

A cemetery may have different rules for another plot, a green burial section, or the burial of an urn. If you are deciding between options, ask for the written requirements and prices for each. An urn burial, for example, calls for its own questions about an urn container and placement rather than using a casket-burial quote as your budget.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can a grave liner satisfy a cemetery’s vault requirement?**

A grave liner may satisfy a requirement for an outer burial container. Ask the cemetery whether it accepts the specific liner for your plot, and get its confirmation before ordering.

**Does the funeral home’s quote always include the vault?**

Quotes vary. Check whether the container appears as its own line, who will deliver and place it, and whether the cemetery will send a separate bill.

**Do I need the same container for a cremation burial?**

Ask the cemetery for its urn burial requirements and pricing. These can differ from the rules for an outer container around a casket.
