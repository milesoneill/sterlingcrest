**Quick answer:** Start by checking whether the person left funds, prepaid arrangements, or insurance that can help. If those aren’t enough, ask the funeral home for a lower-cost written quote and contact the local county or city about any assistance. Before signing a contract, ask who will be responsible for the bill and when payment is due.

## Find out what was already arranged

Start with any funeral home contract, cemetery paperwork, life insurance statement, or savings record the person kept. Call the funeral home listed on prepaid paperwork and ask for a line-by-line list of what is covered and which amounts may still be due. If you find an insurance policy, call the insurer to ask how the beneficiary can begin a claim and what paperwork is needed.

The estate is the property and money a person leaves behind. If you know who is handling the estate, ask whether it has funds that can be used and how to handle invoices. The timing and rules for using estate funds vary. Keep copies of receipts and ask for legal guidance if it’s unclear who can authorize a payment. [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau guidance on debts after death](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-happens-to-your-debts-after-you-die-en-1463/)

## Ask what you would be signing

A family member can help make calls and gather prices before deciding who will sign. Ask the funeral home to show where the agreement identifies the person responsible for payment. If the family plans to use estate or insurance money, ask how the provider handles the bill if the funds arrive later than expected. Write down any deposit and its due date.

It helps to have two separate lines in your notes, one for the total cost and one for the amount due now. If the provider offers a payment plan, ask for the final total with any fees, the due dates, and who would owe a missed payment. Read the agreement before committing. [FTC funeral costs and pricing checklist](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/funeral-costs-pricing-checklist)

## Call about assistance before agreeing to services

Ask the county or city human services department whether there is a funeral, burial, or cremation assistance program. Some local programs set rules about where the person died, what the family can afford, or which funeral providers participate. For example, Seminole County, Florida, describes assistance for basic cremation in certain circumstances and lists contracted funeral homes. Treat that as an example of how a local program can work and check the rules where you live. [Seminole County cremation assistance](https://www.seminolecountyfl.gov/departments-services/community-services/community-health/cremation-burial-assistance)

- Ask whether you must apply before services are arranged.
- Ask what papers show residence, income, or available funds.
- Ask what services the program pays for and whether it pays the provider directly.
- Ask whom the funeral home should contact to verify the program requirements.

If the person was a Veteran or had an employer benefit, call the program or employer separately. A benefit you expect to receive later may be worth applying for while you work out what is due today.

## Price the simplest arrangements your family would accept

Direct cremation is cremation shortly after death without a formal viewing or ceremony with the body present. Immediate burial is burial without a formal viewing or ceremony with the body present. Either can be followed by a separate gathering. If one of these fits your family’s wishes, ask what the quoted amount includes and what the cemetery or crematory bills separately. [FTC funeral pricing checklist](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/funeral-costs-pricing-checklist)

Suppose one arrangement costs $3,400, and a second written quote for a simpler arrangement comes to $2,000. If you have $1,200 available now, the gap is $2,200 for the first and $800 for the second. Those amounts are examples. Put your own quotes and confirmed cash into the comparison, then talk through what matters most to the family.

## Give one family member the current figures

When several people are making calls, it’s easy to count the same contribution twice. Make one list with the written quotes, the deposit, the people who have confirmed what they can contribute, and the benefits you’re still checking. Put a date beside each confirmed amount so everyone is using the same budget.

## If you are planning for a future funeral

Write down which arrangements you want, what they cost, and where family can find the paperwork. Set aside a line for money they can access quickly. If you’re considering insurance for the remaining amount, you can [schedule a consultation](/book) to compare available coverage and premiums with a licensed agent.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I compare prices by phone?**

Yes. The FTC’s Funeral Rule requires covered funeral providers to give price information by phone when you ask. Request the same services from each provider to compare fairly.

**Will a local program pay for a viewing or memorial?**

That depends on the program. Some pay for basic disposition only. Ask which services are included and whether the family can arrange a gathering separately.

**What if the insurance payout is delayed?**

Ask the funeral home which charges are due now and whether it offers a written payment arrangement. Ask the insurer what paperwork it needs to keep the claim moving.
