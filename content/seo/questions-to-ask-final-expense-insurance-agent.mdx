**Quick answer:** Ask who the agent represents, how the discussion relates to your family goals, and where you can read the terms being described. Explain what you already have and what you are trying to understand. End by identifying the unanswered questions and deciding what details you need before taking another step.

## Begin with the purpose of the call

Write one sentence about why you are looking into coverage. Perhaps you want to understand a quote or check how an existing coverage fits your goals. Share that purpose at the start. It gives you a way to judge whether the conversation answered your question instead of simply covering a long list of products.

## Ask who is speaking with you

Request the person’s name and the name of the brokerage or insurer involved. Ask how you can confirm their role through your own state’s insurance department. The Texas regulator’s [life insurance guide](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/pubs/consumer/cb018.html) includes consumer shopping guidance. Use official details for verification rather than assuming that a familiar logo shows who someone represents.

## Find out how the recommendation was reached

Ask which goals or details from your conversation led to the suggestion. If an answer feels broad, bring it back to the issue you named at the start. Sterling Crest is an independent brokerage whose recommendations begin with client goals. A useful discussion should leave you able to describe why an offer is being considered, without promising an outcome.

## Ask to see the written basis

When a term matters to your decision, ask where to find it in the actual written material. The New York regulator’s [life insurance guide](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/consumers/life_insurance) provides general background, not your contract. Note any point that still needs an insurer’s answer. Do not turn an unclear explanation into a settled fact in your own notes.

## Close with the next useful question

Summarize what you learned and what remains open. Decide whether you need another document, a clearer explanation, or more time to think. The [final expense service page](/services/final-expense) offers a way to arrange a discussion. You can use a short question list to keep that conversation focused, without treating an appointment as a commitment to apply.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many questions should I prepare?**

Start with the ones that affect your decision. A short, focused list can be easier to use than trying to cover every possible issue.

**Can I ask how an agent’s role is verified?**

Yes. Ask for identifying details, then use your own state’s official insurance resources to check them.

**What if I already have coverage?**

Mention it early if you want to discuss it. Do not assume a new purchase is needed before understanding what you already own.

**What should I leave the conversation with?**

Aim for clear notes about what was answered and what remains open. Save the written material relevant to those points.
