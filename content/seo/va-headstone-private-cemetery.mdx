**Quick answer:** VA may furnish a headstone, grave marker, or medallion for an eligible Veteran in a private cemetery. Confirm what the cemetery allows before filing. VA Form 40-1330 is for a government headstone or marker. A medallion for an existing privately purchased marker uses a different form.

## Ask what the cemetery will accept

A headstone generally stands upright. A grave marker can be flat, and a niche marker can identify the space where an urn is placed. VA offers several options, but a private cemetery can have rules about size, material, and placement. Call the cemetery before choosing from VA’s available styles.

Have the grave or niche location handy. Ask who confirms the marker type on the application, who accepts delivery, and who sets the marker. Write down a staff member’s name and ask for the cemetery’s requirements in writing. If your family already bought a headstone, ask whether a VA medallion could fit on it. [VA’s headstone and marker guide](https://www.va.gov/burials-memorials/memorial-items/headstones-markers/)

## Choose the form that matches the memorial

| What you want to request | Where to start |
| --- | --- |
| Government headstone, grave marker, or wall marker | VA Form 40-1330 |
| Military medallion for a privately purchased headstone or marker | VA Form 40-1330M |
| Correction or replacement of a government marker | Review VA’s replacement instructions and describe the issue in the applicable form field. |

A medallion is a military emblem attached to a privately purchased headstone or marker. Eligibility can depend on the Veteran’s service, discharge, and the existing grave marker. VA distinguishes an unmarked grave from one already marked with a privately purchased headstone. Tell the cemetery which situation applies and check VA’s rules before ordering anything. [VA medallion guidance](https://www.va.gov/burials-memorials/memorial-items/medallions/)

## Gather records and check the inscription

Start with a copy of the DD214 or another military discharge document. A DD214 summarizes service and separation information. Use it to confirm the details VA asks for. If you’re missing a record or the service history is unusual, start with VA’s instructions for discharge documents and ask what it will accept.

Check the spelling of the Veteran’s name, dates, and service details before sending the form. If several family members want to help, give one person responsibility for the final review and send the others a copy of the proposed inscription. It’s easier to correct a typo while the form is in draft than after the marker is made.

VA lists online submission through QuickSubmit, mailing, and fax options for Form 40-1330. Use the current form instructions for the correct destination and supporting documents. Keep the signed application and proof of submission. [VA Form 40-1330](https://www.va.gov/forms/40-1330/)

## Get the installation cost in writing

Even when VA supplies the marker, the family should check what the private cemetery charges to receive, place, or install it. Ask whether the quote includes a foundation or base and whether a separate contractor does any of the work. Have the cemetery list each amount and when it is due.

For example, if the cemetery quotes a $450 setting fee and a $200 foundation, your estimate shows $650 for those two items. That’s an example, and your actual quote will depend on the cemetery. Ask whether the amount also includes the cost to attach a medallion if you’re using one, or if the medallion needs a separate installer. [National Cemetery Administration headstone and marker information](https://www.cem.va.gov/hmm/)

Add any installation charge to the full funeral and cemetery estimate. Check whether savings or existing life insurance could help pay those bills. If you’re planning ahead and there’s still a gap, that figure can help you compare burial coverage.

## Confirm who will check the delivery

Ask the cemetery whether its staff will inspect the marker when it arrives. Have one family contact compare the lettering and dates with the saved application. If the marker arrives damaged or the inscription has an error, VA’s instructions describe how to request a replacement. Keep photographs and the application copy handy so you can explain exactly what needs attention. [VA replacement instructions](https://www.va.gov/burials-memorials/memorial-items/headstones-markers/)

## Keep the memorial request and burial claim organized

Put the cemetery quote, marker application, and any burial allowance claim in separate folders. The marker request asks VA for a memorial item. A burial allowance claim asks VA about financial help with qualifying expenses. Review the official requirements for each. The cemetery can answer installation questions while VA can explain its own eligibility and processing rules.

If you’re planning ahead and want to compare private coverage for the costs in your cemetery and funeral estimates, [schedule a consultation](/book). You can bring the written estimates so the discussion starts with the expenses you actually expect.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can VA provide a marker for an unmarked private grave?**

VA can furnish markers for qualifying Veterans in unmarked graves. Check the service eligibility rules and have the private cemetery verify which style it accepts.

**What if the Veteran already has a private headstone?**

VA describes separate options for certain marked graves, including an eligible military medallion. Compare the requirements and confirm what the cemetery will allow on the existing stone.

**Who pays to put the marker in place?**

Ask the private cemetery who will set it and how it bills for installation. VA furnishing a marker and the cemetery’s installation charges are separate questions.
