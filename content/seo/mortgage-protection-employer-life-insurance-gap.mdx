**Quick answer:** Life insurance through work can help your beneficiary pay the mortgage if coverage is in force and the claim is payable. Check the amount, who would receive it and whether you can keep coverage after leaving. Then compare what’s left after other family expenses with what you want to set aside for the house.

## Pull up the benefit certificate

Group life insurance is coverage offered through an organization, such as your employer. The benefit may be a flat dollar amount or a multiple of salary. The beneficiary is the person or entity designated to receive the benefit if the insurer pays a claim. Start with the certificate or plan documents and the latest enrollment confirmation. A benefits portal can show a headline amount without explaining the rules that go with it.

Some employers offer both basic coverage and optional supplemental coverage. Write down the amount of each that is in force now. Check who is named as beneficiary on each part. If you expect an increase from open enrollment or a recent promotion, ask HR when it takes effect before including the higher amount.

## Separate the mortgage from other expenses

Ask what you would want the benefit to do for the house. A family that wants a full payoff needs a different amount from one that needs a year of payments. For the monthly goal, use the full mortgage payment, including property taxes and homeowners insurance if you pay them through escrow. Add any costs you pay separately. Compare the result with what the remaining household income could reasonably cover.

Here’s a hypothetical example. The work plan provides a $160,000 death benefit. The homeowner wants to leave $60,000 available for other household expenses, leaving $100,000 to put toward the home. With a $190,000 payoff goal, there would be a $90,000 gap while the work policy is active. With a $2,100 mortgage payment, a different goal of 12 months of payments would require $25,200 before accounting for other housing bills or income. Use the [mortgage protection worksheet](/resources/how-much-mortgage-protection-insurance-do-i-need) to run both versions with your numbers. If a gap remains after you account for savings and other coverage, compare individual policies based on how much your family would need and how long you’d want that protection to last.

## Ask what changes when your job changes

A change in employment or benefit eligibility can affect group coverage. Ask HR or the insurer what happens if you switch jobs, reduce your hours or take extended leave. Request the answer in writing, especially if there’s already a change on your calendar. The [Texas Department of Insurance’s consumer guide](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/pubs/consumer/cb018.html) explains why it’s worth checking how employer coverage works when you leave.

| Ask your benefits team | What to write down |
| --- | --- |
| When does my current group coverage end if I leave? | The exact date and whether basic and supplemental coverage end together. |
| Can I keep coverage through portability? | Portability means continuing eligible coverage after leaving the group. Ask about cost, available amounts, and the application deadline. |
| Is conversion available? | Conversion lets an eligible participant switch to an individual policy under the plan’s rules. Ask which policy types are available and when the request and premium are due. |
| Will a new employer plan start immediately? | The enrollment date, effective date and whether your expected amount has been confirmed. |

Your certificate and insurer can explain the options that apply. A short deadline can matter more than a quote you haven’t received yet, so check dates before comparing premiums.

## Decide how much you want tied to employment

If the work benefit covers your mortgage goal and your family has other resources, you may decide that it works for now. Put a reminder on your calendar for annual enrollment and review the numbers if your payment, income or household changes.

If losing the work benefit would leave a large gap, compare an individual policy for the part you want to keep independent of your job. Individual term life covers a set period, such as the years you expect to have a mortgage payment. Check the premium, effective date and beneficiary rules before counting any new policy. If you want help reading your certificate alongside your mortgage figures, [book a brief phone consultation](/book). You can start with the coverage you already have and discuss the remaining need.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can my employer life insurance beneficiary use the money for the mortgage?**

A beneficiary who receives a group life insurance benefit can generally decide how to use it. Confirm who is listed and review any conditions in the policy before relying on the benefit for housing.

**Is accidental death coverage the same as employer life insurance?**

Accidental death coverage pays only for deaths that meet its accident terms. Read the certificate to see whether it’s a separate benefit, and use the life insurance amount when planning for a death that might not involve an accident.

**Do I need a separate policy if I get life insurance at work?**

The answer depends on what the group benefit covers, how much your family needs and what happens to the policy when you leave. Compare those numbers before deciding whether more coverage would help.
