**Quick answer:** You can use life insurance to plan for an adjustable-rate mortgage. Use the current payoff quote if your goal is to clear the loan. If you want to cover monthly payments, use the estimated payment after the next rate reset so the plan reflects what the household may owe.

## Start with the servicer’s estimate

An adjustable-rate mortgage, or ARM, has an interest rate that can change under the loan’s terms. A rate reset is the date on which the servicer adjusts that rate. The amount due can rise or fall as a result. If you received a notice of a reset, pull out the old payment, the estimated new payment and the first due date under the new terms.

The [CFPB’s guide to ARM notices](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/i-received-notice-of-an-upcoming-rate-change-on-my-adjustable-rate-mortgage-arm-why-did-i-receive-this-and-what-should-i-do-now-en-1843/) explains what those notices are meant to tell you. If a number is missing or unclear, ask the company that sends your statement to break out principal and interest, escrow, and any mortgage insurance. Escrow is the part collected for bills such as property taxes and homeowners insurance. You need the total payment plus any housing bills you pay separately to see what a surviving household would need.

## Check whether your plan is based on a payoff or monthly payments

Mortgage protection life insurance is life insurance purchased with a home loan in mind. Under an individual policy, the insurance company pays the named beneficiary if the insured person dies while coverage is in force and the claim is payable. The life insurance policy and the ARM are separate contracts. A change in one does not automatically change the other.

If you plan to leave enough money to pay off the loan, ask your servicer for a payoff quote and look at the remaining balance in your regular reviews. If your goal is to cover a temporary monthly shortfall, the reset estimate matters more. Compare the new payment and other bills with the money your household would still have available after losing an income.

## Work through the budget on paper

Imagine a household with an $1,800 monthly mortgage payment today. The notice estimates $2,125 after the reset. Under the family’s one-income budget, it could put $1,500 a month toward the mortgage. Here’s how that changes what the family would need to draw from savings or a life insurance benefit. All numbers are hypothetical.

| Planning figure | Current payment | Estimated after reset |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Mortgage payment | $1,800 | $2,125 |
| Available for housing | $1,500 | $1,500 |
| Monthly shortfall | $300 | $625 |
| Twelve months of shortfalls | $3,600 | $7,500 |

The difference in that worksheet is $3,900 over a year. It reflects the mortgage shortfall only. Add any bills outside the payment, such as homeowners association dues, and any other expected costs after an income loss. The mortgage-only result can make it easier to separate this need from broader income replacement.

## Read the reset rules before modeling later years

The index is a benchmark that can move over time. The margin is the amount the lender adds to the index to calculate a new rate, subject to the loan rules. Rate caps limit how much the rate can change at certain points. The [CFPB’s guide to ARM caps](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-are-rate-caps-with-an-adjustable-rate-mortgage-arm-and-how-do-they-work-en-1951/) explains the first adjustment, later adjustments and the life of the loan.

Ask your servicer what a higher payment scenario would look like under your contract. Avoid budgeting around a refinance you haven’t secured. Check the dates too. If your term life coverage ends before the mortgage does, decide how you’d handle the remaining payment years. Our [guide to matching a life insurance term to a mortgage](/resources/choosing-life-insurance-term-for-mortgage) walks through that timeline.

## Decide what to review now

If the new payment is difficult to meet today, call the servicer and ask about options for your loan. For planning ahead, write down the new payment, any other coverage you have, and the amount the household could afford on one income. You can [book a brief phone consultation](/book) to compare a monthly cushion with a benefit sized for a payoff.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does mortgage protection cover a rate increase?**

Life insurance pays a benefit for a covered death. A rate increase on its own does not trigger payment. Use the reset estimate to update your family’s housing budget.

**Will my life insurance premium rise when my mortgage resets?**

A change to your mortgage rate does not itself change the premium on a separate individual life policy. Check your policy for its own premium schedule, including what happens at renewal.

**Which payment should I use if the reset is still years away?**

Start with the current payment and build a second scenario using your loan’s adjustment rules. Refresh your figures when the servicer sends a more specific estimate.

**What if I refinance into a fixed-rate loan?**

Review the new balance, payment, term and your policy’s expiration date. Compare them with what you want the existing benefit to do before changing coverage.
