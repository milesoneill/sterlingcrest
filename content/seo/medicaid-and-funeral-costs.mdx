**Quick answer:** Standard Medicaid medical benefits do not pay a funeral home’s bill for burial or cremation. Some states or counties have separate funeral assistance programs that may use Medicaid or other public assistance enrollment when deciding who qualifies. Contact the local program before signing a contract or paying a deposit.

## Check whether a separate program exists

Medicaid is health coverage administered by states under federal rules. The federal [list of Medicaid state plan benefits](https://www.medicaid.gov/medicaid/benefits/mandatory-optional-medicaid-benefits) includes medical services such as hospital care and lists hospice as an optional benefit. A funeral home’s bill is a different expense. If someone says “Medicaid helps with burial,” they might mean a separate program run by a state or county that looks at whether the person received public assistance.

For example, [Maryland’s Burial Assistance Program](https://dhs.maryland.gov/weathering-tough-times/burial-assistance/) lists Medical Assistance among the programs it may consider when determining eligibility. It has separate conditions and directs payment to the funeral director. It also says it cannot reimburse expenses that were already paid. Your area may have different rules, a different name for the program or no comparable assistance.

## Make the call before you pay a deposit

Start with the human services or social services department where the person lived. If the death occurred in another county, ask which county handles the request. You can also ask a hospital or hospice social worker whether they know which local office takes applications.

Have these questions ready.

| Ask the local office | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Which office handles funeral or burial assistance for this address? | The county of residence and place of death may be different. |
| What is the deadline and do we need approval before signing or paying? | Some programs exclude expenses the family already paid. |
| Which funeral homes participate and which services can they provide? | Assistance may cover a basic arrangement or use designated providers. |
| Which assets or benefits do you need to know about? | The program may ask about available savings, insurance or other help. |
| Who receives the payment and what could the family still owe? | A payment to the provider may cover only part of the total. |

Ask for the application and a written list of required documents. A program might request proof of death, a funeral estimate and records showing available funds. Ask the funeral home for an itemized estimate you can share. If the home says assistance will cover the bill, confirm the amount and conditions with the program itself before making plans around that money.

## Understand what a burial fund means for eligibility

A **burial fund** is money a person sets aside for burial expenses. An **asset exclusion** means an eligibility program leaves a qualifying asset out of its calculation. It does not make a payment to your family.

People sometimes hear that they can set aside $1,500 for burial. The [Social Security Administration’s 2026 burial-fund guide](https://www.ssa.gov/ssi/spotlights/spot-burial-funds.htm) describes a general Supplemental Security Income rule allowing up to $1,500 for each eligible person and spouse, subject to other life insurance and burial arrangements. Supplemental Security Income, or SSI, is a separate program. The dollar figure is an eligibility rule under SSI, and Medicaid pathways can apply different rules.

The [federal Medicaid eligibility guide](https://www.medicaid.gov/medicaid/eligibility-policy) explains that Medicaid pathways using modified adjusted gross income, or MAGI, do not have an asset test. Other pathways, including many based on age, blindness or disability, can use different methods. If you are considering a prepaid funeral contract, dedicated savings or a life policy, [contact your state Medicaid agency](https://www.medicaid.gov/eligibility) about the specific eligibility pathway and arrangement before changing how funds are held.

## Work with the amount your family can confirm

In an **illustrative example**, a family gets a $2,400 direct cremation quote and hopes a local program will contribute $1,200. If the program confirms $1,200 in writing and the full quote is eligible, the expected gap is $1,200. If the family pays first and the program does not reimburse, they could owe the full $2,400. That is why the timing questions matter as much as the benefit amount.

If you’re arranging a funeral now, check whether available savings or an existing policy can help with the amount the program leaves unpaid. If you’re planning ahead, you can compare savings and existing coverage with the full estimate to see whether additional burial coverage might help. Ask your state Medicaid agency how it would treat a particular policy under your eligibility pathway before you buy it.

If you are planning ahead, record the arrangement you want, the current estimate and where your family can find the paperwork. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Will being on Medicaid automatically qualify someone for funeral assistance?**

No. Ask the local program about eligibility, deadlines and what costs it can cover. Enrollment can be one of several factors where a program exists.

**Does the SSI burial fund rule mean Social Security sends my family $1,500?**

No. It describes when certain money you already have can be excluded from an SSI resource calculation. The Social Security lump-sum death payment has separate rules.

**What should I do if the funeral home needs an answer today?**

Call the local program and ask what you can sign or pay without affecting a potential application. Ask the provider for its deadline and an itemized quote for a basic arrangement.
