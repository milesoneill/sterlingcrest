**Quick answer:** To file a mortgage protection life insurance claim, contact the insurer for the existing policy, report the insured person's death, and submit the documents it requires. First confirm what kind of insurance you have and who is entitled to the benefit. An individual life policy may pay a named beneficiary, while loan-linked credit life may pay the creditor.

## Find the company that issued the policy

A newly purchased policy cannot cover a death that already happened. Focus on the deceased person's existing policies and benefits, then make a separate plan for mortgage payments while the claim is reviewed. Don't assume the servicer automatically knows a life policy exists.

Look for the insurer's legal name, policy number, insured person's name, and current contact details. An insurance brokerage, lender, servicer, and life insurer can be different companies. A premium appearing near mortgage records doesn't prove which company handles the death claim.

Use an official company website or the contact information on confirmed policy records. Be cautious about unsolicited messages asking for a fee to release a benefit. If an old insurer name has changed, your state insurance department can help identify the appropriate company contact.

The [Texas Department of Insurance life insurance guide](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/pubs/consumer/cb018.html) explains policy and beneficiary basics. Its Texas deadlines and rights aren't universal rules, so use your state's regulator and the insurer for requirements that apply to your claim.

## Identify the payment recipient before planning a payoff

Read the contract and ask the insurer who may file and receive payment. A family member helping with paperwork isn't necessarily a beneficiary or authorized representative. The insurer may need additional proof of authority for an estate, trust, or another arrangement.

With an individual policy, a beneficiary may have choices about how to use the money. With credit life, the creditor may receive a payment tied to the covered debt. Don't build a family cash budget around proceeds payable elsewhere.

Our guide to [who receives a mortgage protection payout](/resources/mortgage-protection-insurance-beneficiary) explains the distinction. If names or ownership are disputed, don't change records or distribute expected funds based on an assumption; obtain the appropriate insurer and legal guidance.

## Ask for the actual claim requirements

Request the correct claim form, the documents needed, and the accepted way to submit them. A death certificate is commonly part of a life claim, but the insurer should specify its requirements, including whether it needs a certified copy or can accept another format.

Keep the deceased person's identity details and policy number consistent across the submission. If a beneficiary's name has changed, explain that and ask what supporting record is needed. Don't send original documents or sensitive information through an unverified channel.

The [Washington insurance regulator's claim guidance](https://www.insurance.wa.gov/insurance-resources/life-insurance/learn-how-life-insurance-works) starts with notifying the insurer or agent. This general step is useful across claims, while Washington-specific rules on that page should not be treated as rules for every state.

## Keep a record of what you sent

Use a simple claim log: date, company contact, documents submitted, confirmation number, and next requested action. Save copies before sending anything. If the company says an item is missing, compare the request with your log rather than resending the whole packet blindly.

Ask whether the file is complete and what happens next. A receipt confirming upload isn't the same as approval. If the insurer requests further information, find out what question it is trying to resolve and respond through the verified process.

There is no universal payment promise that fits every policy and claim. Review can depend on the contract, beneficiary information, required documentation, and circumstances. Keep a clear next follow-up date rather than treating an estimated date as guaranteed cash.

## Handle the loan while the insurance claim is pending

Contact the mortgage servicer using a verified number. Ask how to report the death, what records it needs, and how payments should be handled while the family's authority or ownership is being established. Life insurance claim forms don't replace the servicer's process.

Our guide to [what happens to a mortgage after a borrower dies](/resources/what-happens-to-mortgage-when-borrower-dies) explains the separate loan questions for co-borrowers and heirs.

The mortgage does not become paid merely because a claim has been submitted. Keep loan communications and insurance communications in separate files so a confirmation from one company isn't mistaken for an action by the other.

If payments may be difficult, ask the servicer promptly about available assistance and any requirements. Avoid assuming the family must wait for an insurance decision before discussing the loan. Legal questions about responsibility, ownership, or inheritance deserve qualified advice for the applicable state and documents.

## Decide on a payoff after the benefit is confirmed

If proceeds are payable to you, request the servicer's current payoff amount before sending a large payment. An ordinary statement balance may not be the exact figure needed to retire the loan on a given date. Confirm payment instructions directly with the servicer.

Consider other immediate household needs and the policy's purpose before committing every dollar. A benefit intended for housing and living support has to be evaluated against both. This guide doesn't recommend that every beneficiary pay off a mortgage immediately.

For a forward-looking review of how an existing benefit relates to the household's remaining needs, a [phone consultation about your insurance questions](/book) can be useful. It doesn't replace claim approval, legal advice, or a lender's payoff calculation.

## What if you can't find a policy?

Check known records and appropriate company contacts before assuming no coverage exists. The [NAIC's beneficiary guidance](https://content.naic.org/article/consumer-insight-what-know-about-life-insurance-beneficiaries) links to its Life Insurance Policy Locator, which may help locate missing policies. A search request isn't a promise that coverage will be found or paid.

Keep expectations separate from confirmed resources. A family can discuss the loan and current bills while a search continues, but should not treat an unknown policy amount as available money.
