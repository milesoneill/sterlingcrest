**Quick answer:** While shopping for burial insurance, pause when a promise is unclear, a written detail is missing, or you feel pushed to decide before understanding the offer. Ask for an explanation and check the source of the details. These signs are reasons to verify, not proof of fraud. Keep the actual documents for any follow-up discussion.

## Treat a broad promise as a question to resolve

If a claim sounds wider than the written explanation, ask what supports it. Write down the wording that concerned you and where you heard it. Do not repeat it as a fact in your own planning notes. The aim is to understand the actual offer, not to decide from one sentence whether a person or business has acted improperly.

## Ask for the details you cannot find

A short price or a slogan may leave important questions open. Ask where you can read the details being described. The Texas regulator’s [life insurance guide](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/pubs/consumer/cb018.html) offers consumer shopping background. Keep the written material with your questions rather than assuming that an advertisement, a brochure, and an individual offer all mean the same thing.

## Slow down when the explanation is rushed

If you cannot follow the discussion, ask for a pause or another explanation. A decision made before you understand the material is not made clearer by pressure. The New York regulator’s [consumer guide](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/consumers/life_insurance) is a place to start learning basic terms. Use the insurer or an appropriate professional for answers about the actual policy.

## Verify identity through a reliable source

Ask who you are speaking with and use your own state’s official insurance resources for questions about licensing or identity. Do not rely on this page to confirm a person’s status. If an answer remains unclear, keep it marked as open. A mismatch deserves a careful check, not an unsupported public accusation about a person or company.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does an unclear statement prove fraud?**

No. It is a reason to ask questions and verify details. Do not make an accusation based only on this checklist.

**What should I keep if an offer concerns me?**

Save the written material and your dated notes. Record the specific point that needs an answer.

**Can I ask for another explanation?**

Yes, you can ask for wording you understand. Mark any unresolved point before deciding what step to take next.

**Where should I check a licensing question?**

Use the official insurance resources for your own state. Ask the department which route is appropriate if you cannot find a clear result.
