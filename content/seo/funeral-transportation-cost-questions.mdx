**Quick answer:** Pickup and hearse use can be separate funeral expenses. For one West Palm Beach example, Northwood Funeral Home lists a $495 transfer charge and $595 for up to three hours of hearse use on its March 2026 price list. Mileage and other extras may apply. These are transport charges, not a complete funeral price.

## Pickup and a hearse are different services

A funeral can involve more than one journey. The first transfer brings a person to the funeral home. A hearse may be used later for a service or a trip to the cemetery. A single vehicle fee may concern just one of those journeys. Read the service description to tell which trip it covers.

The [FTC's funeral pricing checklist](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/funeral-costs-pricing-checklist) treats pickup, hearse use, other vehicles, and forwarding or receiving remains as separate categories. That helps explain why one broad “transport” number can hide different services.

## Actual local pickup, hearse, and mileage prices

For a dated local example, [Northwood Funeral Home in West Palm Beach](https://northwoodfh.com/services/) publishes the following charges on its price list effective March 16, 2026. The list says prices can change.

| Item | Listed charge | What it describes |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Pickup | $495 | This is the basic transfer to the funeral home. |
| Hearse | $595 | The charge covers up to three hours of use. |
| After-hours or holiday pickup | $145 extra | This is added to the basic transfer charge. |
| Extra mileage | $4 per mile | This applies to loaded miles outside the 30-mile radius. |

This is one provider's pricing, not a Florida average. The same list has other additions, so the table is not a promise of a complete transfer bill. It gives you a real starting point for checking which services your own plan needs.

## See how separate charges can add up

Suppose a family selects the listed basic transfer and the listed hearse service. Those two charges total $1,090: $495 plus $595. If the $145 after-hours pickup addition also applies, the subtotal becomes $1,235.

That example assumes the services are charged separately. It excludes all other funeral goods and services, and it should not be added on top of a package that already pays for either journey.

Mileage needs one more detail: the number of miles the provider will bill. If it confirms 15 extra billable miles at the listed $4 charge, that part adds $60. Do not assume every mile on a map is charged. Find out which journey, distance allowance, and loaded miles the estimate uses.

The useful comparison is the total for the same plan. A lower pickup figure may not stay lower after the actual distance and time are included. Compare a full explanation of the journeys, rather than the smallest number on either price list.

## Check a package before adding a separate pickup fee

A direct cremation package and a full funeral can include different services. The first transfer may be in a package even though the price list also shows a pickup charge.

For example, Northwood includes the transfer to its funeral home in its direct cremation option. Its list also says the basic fee, or a reduced fee, is included in some services. Read the package and price list together. Do not add up every line as though each one applies.

For each journey, mark it as included, separately charged, or not needed. If pickup is included, do not add the standalone $495 again. If you are choosing cremation without a procession, do not add a hearse merely because it appears on the list.

The [FTC's funeral shopping guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/shopping-funeral-services) supports choosing the goods and services you want instead of accepting a package containing unwanted items. A clear package comparison can prevent both double counting and paying for a service that does not fit your plan.

## Long-distance arrangements need a different total

A trip between funeral homes is different from a local pickup. One firm may prepare and send the remains. Another may receive them and handle the service where they arrive. Air or other carrier costs may be a further charge.

The FTC checklist has separate lines for sending remains to another funeral home and receiving them. For a long-distance plan, find out which firm handles each part. This shows where another bill may come from.

The local pickup and hearse examples above do not price a move across states or overseas. Give the firms the starting place, end point, and type of service. A useful estimate shows care before the trip, transfers, carrier charges, and work by the receiving firm.

Keep family travel in its own part of the budget. Flights, rooms, and rides for relatives are different from the funeral home's work. Put them under their own heading so you can see what each total covers.

## Turn the service details into a funding decision

For a local plan, the next step is to compare the journeys you need with what the selected arrangement includes. For a long-distance plan, settle who provides and bills for each stage before relying on one total.

Put the transport subtotal beside the other costs in your [funeral expenses plan](/resources/funeral-expenses-checklist). Label a missing amount as open; do not treat it as free. Date the figures so a reader can tell when the costs were checked.

Then look at the funds you want to set aside for these costs. Savings and insurance you already own can affect how much new coverage you want to explore. Keep other needs in mind. The same savings cannot pay for both a funeral and an urgent bill at the same time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is a hearse fee the same as the initial pickup charge?**

No. Pickup usually describes transfer to the funeral home; hearse use may concern a later service or cemetery journey. Check the package description to see which of those services is included and which is separately priced.

**Does the $1,090 example include the whole funeral?**

No. It adds only the $495 transfer and $595 hearse charges on one provider's dated list. It excludes other funeral services, merchandise, cemetery costs, and any extra charges that apply to the arrangement.

**Can I discuss insurance before choosing a funeral home?**

Yes. A consultation can begin with your goals, existing coverage, and a comfortable payment budget. Supplier estimates can add detail later, but you do not need one to book a phone call.
