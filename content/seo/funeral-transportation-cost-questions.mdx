**Quick answer:** About $800 is a rough planning reference for local pickup and hearse service together, based on two national median charges reported for 2023. It is not the full funeral price. Longer trips, extra vehicles, and other services can increase the transport total, while a package may already include the journeys you need.

## Pickup and a hearse are different services

A funeral can involve more than one journey. The first transfer brings a person to the funeral home. A hearse may be used later for a service or a trip to the cemetery. A single vehicle fee may concern just one of those journeys. Read the service description to tell which trip it covers.

The [FTC's funeral pricing checklist](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/funeral-costs-pricing-checklist) treats pickup, hearse use, other vehicles, and forwarding or receiving remains as separate categories. That helps explain why one broad “transport” number can hide different services.

## What is a useful budget for local funeral transport?

For a local funeral with a burial, about $800 offers a starting point for pickup and hearse use together. The [national cost breakdown published by Dignity Memorial](https://www.dignitymemorial.com/pre-planning/funeral-costs/average-funeral-cost) lists 2023 NFDA median charges of $395 for transfer to the funeral home and $375 for a hearse. Those two figures add to $770, rounded here to about $800 for planning.

This is a budget reference built from two reported fees, rather than a measured national median for every family’s full transport bill. It does not include every possible vehicle or trip. The figures also come from 2023, so local prices today can be different.

Start with the journeys your plan needs. A viewing and burial may involve pickup and a hearse trip to the cemetery. A simple cremation may have pickup and transfer built into its price, with no hearse procession. The right total follows the arrangements you choose.

## What can change the transport total?

Distance is one of the main details to settle. A nearby pickup and cemetery are different from a trip to another county, a move between funeral homes, or air travel. Also check whether the price includes the full time needed for the service and journey.

Other vehicles can add to the cost. A family car or van is a separate choice from a hearse carrying the person who died. Decide which transport the funeral home will provide and which travel relatives will arrange themselves. You do not need to buy every vehicle shown on a price list.

Describe the actual plan when comparing offers: the pickup location, service location, cemetery or crematory, and any additional stops. Ask for one transport total for those journeys, including any distance or timing additions. That is more useful than comparing low starting fees that cover different trips.

If an amount is not yet settled, keep it marked as open. An unknown price is not the same as a service being included.

## Check the package before adding another transport allowance

A package may already include the first pickup, use of a hearse, or transfer to a crematory. A separate vehicle price on the general list does not mean you owe that charge on top of the package. Read the description for the plan you select.

For each journey, mark it as included, separately charged, or not needed. If pickup is included in a cremation price, do not add a second pickup allowance. If you are choosing cremation without a procession, do not budget for a hearse only because it appears on the list.

The [FTC’s funeral shopping guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/shopping-funeral-services) supports choosing the goods and services you want instead of accepting a package containing unwanted items. A clear comparison can prevent both double counting and paying for transport that does not fit your plan.

Keep a copy of the complete total and its included services. If the plan changes, review the new total as a whole. One extra journey may affect more than the vehicle charge.

## Long-distance arrangements need a different total

A trip between funeral homes is different from a local pickup. One firm may prepare and send the remains. Another may receive them and handle the service where they arrive. Air or other carrier costs may be a further charge.

The FTC checklist has separate lines for sending remains to another funeral home and receiving them. For a long-distance plan, find out which firm handles each part. This shows where another bill may come from.

A local transport allowance does not price a move across states or overseas. Give the firms the starting place, end point, and type of service. A useful estimate shows care before the trip, transfers, carrier charges, and work by the receiving firm.

Keep family travel in its own part of the budget. Flights, rooms, and rides for relatives are different from the funeral home's work. Put them under their own heading so you can see what each total covers.

## Plan for the whole funeral, with transport included

Transport is one part of the funeral budget. The [NFDA’s 2023 national study](https://content.nfda.org/news/media-center/nfda-news-releases/id/8134/2023-nfda-general-price-list-study-shows-inflation-increasing-faster-than-the-cost-of-a-funeral) reported a median of $8,300 for a funeral with a viewing and burial, and $6,280 for one with a viewing and cremation. These are broad references for the main arrangement; the cremation figure is not for direct cremation without a viewing.

Those funeral figures already contain transport services in their stated arrangements. Do not add the local transport allowance again. Cemetery interment, markers, flowers and obituary notices are among the costs outside the study totals. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is a hearse fee the same as the initial pickup charge?**

No. Pickup usually describes transfer to the funeral home; hearse use may concern a later service or cemetery journey. Check the package description to see which of those services is included and which is separately priced.

**Should I add transport to the full funeral price?**

Only add transport that the quoted arrangement does not already include. Check pickup, hearse use, and any other trips against the package details. The rough $800 reference is for two common local services, not an extra charge to add to every funeral total.

**Can I discuss insurance before choosing a funeral home?**

Yes. A consultation can begin with your goals, existing coverage, and a comfortable payment budget. Supplier estimates can add detail later, but you do not need one to book a phone call.
