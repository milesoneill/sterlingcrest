Rates also shift with health status, tobacco use, coverage amount, and policy type. Comparing multiple carriers often uncovers 20 percent or wider rate differences for the same face value.

## How Final Expense Premiums Change With Age

Your age drives the price. Insurers know a 50-year-old will likely outlive a 70-year-old, so they charge less upfront.

Health matters, too. Simplified-issue policies ask health questions but skip the exam. They cost less than guaranteed-issue plans that accept everyone. Graded-benefit policies limit payouts in the first two or three years, so they carry lower premiums than immediate-benefit coverage.

Carriers evaluate applicants differently. One insurer may quote 20 percent less than another for the same face amount. Sterling Crest Financial Group, as an independent brokerage, compares options across a broad network of carriers to help you find competitive rates.

## Why Age Is the Primary Driver of Rates

Actuarial tables show life expectancy shrinks with age. The CDC reports a 50-year-old has roughly 30 years ahead, while a 70-year-old has about 15. That gap shapes every premium.

Mortality risk is the core mechanism. An 80-year-old is far likelier to file a claim in the next five years than a 60-year-old. Carriers offset that risk by charging more.

The Social Security Administration publishes life tables insurers reference when setting rates. Mortality accelerates after 70, which is why premiums climb steeply in the 70s and 80s. Gender, tobacco, and health also matter, but age remains the largest factor.

Underwriting varies by carrier. Some specialize in older applicants and offer better rates for seniors. Others focus on younger buyers. Comparing multiple insurers becomes more important as you age.

## Rates in Your 50s

Age 50 to 59 brings the lowest premiums and easiest underwriting. Most carriers offer simplified-issue policies with competitive rates for healthy applicants.

This is the best time to lock in coverage. A 55-year-old in good health can often secure $10,000 of immediate-benefit coverage for a level monthly premium. Tobacco users pay more, but the spread between smokers and non-smokers is narrower now than in later decades.

Carriers view you as lower risk. Controlled high blood pressure or high cholesterol may not disqualify you or spike your premium. Some insurers even offer preferred rates for excellent health.

If you're considering final expense insurance in your 50s, comparing carriers now helps you find coverage before age-related increases take effect.

## Rates in Your 60s

Age 60 to 69 brings higher premiums than the 50s, but rates stay moderate compared to later decades. Many carriers still offer simplified-issue policies, though health conditions play a larger role.

A 65-year-old with well-controlled diabetes or a heart-disease history may still qualify for simplified-issue coverage. Premiums will be higher than for a healthy peer. Some carriers specialize in chronic conditions and may quote better rates.

Guaranteed-issue policies become more common. They accept all applicants but cost more and include a two- or three-year graded benefit period. Full death benefits don't pay if you die from natural causes during that window.

Rate spreads between carriers widen in the 60s. Comparing options from multiple insurers is critical. Sterling Crest Financial Group can help you evaluate simplified-issue and guaranteed-issue policies to fit your health profile and budget.

## Rates in Your 70s

Age 70 to 79 brings significantly higher premiums. Guaranteed-issue policies become more common, and many carriers impose waiting periods that limit benefits if you die from natural causes in the first two or three years.

Health conditions that were manageable earlier now affect eligibility more. A 75-year-old with COPD, cancer history, or multiple chronic issues may find simplified-issue policies unavailable. Guaranteed-issue coverage may be the only option.

Rate spreads widen dramatically. One carrier may charge 30 percent more than another for the same coverage. Comparing multiple options is essential.

Immediate-benefit policies still exist in the 70s but cost more than graded-benefit plans. If you're in good health, you may qualify for immediate coverage at a reasonable premium. If your health is declining, a graded-benefit policy may be more affordable.

## Rates in Your 80s and Beyond

Age 80 and older brings the highest premiums, limited carrier options, and guaranteed-issue policies as the most common choice. Maximum issue ages—often 85 or 89—create urgency if you've delayed purchasing coverage.

Most carriers impose a two- or three-year graded benefit period. The policy pays a limited benefit if you die from natural causes during that time. Accidental death is typically covered in full from day one.

Health underwriting is minimal or nonexistent for guaranteed-issue policies. Applicants with serious health conditions can still obtain coverage, but premiums are significantly higher and coverage amounts are often capped at $25,000 or less.

Some carriers stop accepting new applicants after 85. Others extend coverage to 89. If you're approaching these limits, comparing carriers quickly matters. Sterling Crest Financial Group can help you identify carriers that accept applicants in your age range and compare rates.

Even in your 80s, final expense insurance can reduce the financial burden on your family. Comparing multiple carriers is the best way to find coverage that fits your budget.

## Other Factors That Affect Rates Beyond Age

Health status, tobacco use, gender, coverage amount, and policy type all shift premiums. Two people of the same age can receive different quotes.

Health matters most for simplified-issue policies, which require health questions. Applicants with well-controlled chronic conditions like diabetes or high blood pressure may qualify for standard rates. Those with recent hospitalizations or serious diagnoses may be declined or offered guaranteed-issue coverage instead.

Tobacco use increases premiums by 30 to 50 percent or more. Some insurers define tobacco use as any nicotine product within the past 12 months. Others allow occasional cigar use without penalty.

Gender affects rates because women have longer life expectancies. A 70-year-old woman typically pays less than a 70-year-old man for the same coverage.

Coverage amount directly affects premiums. A $10,000 policy costs less than a $25,000 policy, but the cost per thousand dollars of coverage may decrease as the face amount increases.

Policy type determines whether you pay for immediate coverage or accept a waiting period. Immediate-benefit policies cost more than graded-benefit policies, which limit payouts during the first two or three years. Guaranteed-issue policies typically cost more than simplified-issue policies that require health questions.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does final expense insurance cost at 65?**

Premiums at 65 vary by carrier, health status, tobacco use, coverage amount, and policy type. A healthy non-smoker may pay significantly less than a smoker with chronic health conditions for the same coverage. Simplified-issue policies typically cost less than guaranteed-issue policies. Comparing carriers can reveal rate differences of 20 percent or more.

**Do final expense insurance rates increase every year?**

No. Final expense policies are typically level-premium, meaning your monthly payment stays the same for life once the policy is issued. Premiums are based on your age and health at the time of application and don't increase as you get older. However, if you apply at 70 instead of 60, you'll pay a higher premium from the start.

**Can I still get final expense insurance in my 80s?**

Yes. Most carriers accept applicants into their 80s, though maximum issue ages vary. Some stop accepting new applicants after 85, while others extend coverage to 89. Guaranteed-issue policies are the most common option in this age range and accept all applicants regardless of health. Premiums are higher, and coverage amounts are often capped at $25,000 or less.

**What is the average cost of final expense insurance for a 70-year-old?**

Premiums for a 70-year-old vary widely by carrier, health status, tobacco use, coverage amount, and policy type. A healthy non-smoker may qualify for simplified-issue coverage at a lower rate than a smoker with chronic conditions who requires guaranteed-issue coverage. Comparing multiple carriers is the best way to find competitive rates.

**Why do final expense premiums increase with age?**

Insurers use actuarial tables that show life expectancy decreases with age, so the likelihood of a claim increases. A 50-year-old has roughly 30 more years of life expectancy, while a 70-year-old has about 15. Carriers charge higher premiums to offset the increased mortality risk as applicants age.

**What's the difference between simplified-issue and guaranteed-issue policies?**

Simplified-issue policies require health questions but no medical exam and typically cost less. Guaranteed-issue policies accept all applicants regardless of health but cost more and often include a two- or three-year graded benefit period that limits payouts if you die from natural causes during that window.
