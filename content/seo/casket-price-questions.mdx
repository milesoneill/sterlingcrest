**Quick answer:** Plan on roughly $2,000–$5,000 for a casket bought through a funeral home. Materials, size, features, and seller affect the price. This is only part of the funeral budget. NFDA reported an $8,300 national median for a funeral with viewing and burial in 2023. Cemetery and other separate costs are extra.

## What should you budget for a casket?

About **$2,000–$5,000** is a useful general range for a casket bought through a funeral home. Casket maker [Titan's July 2026 guide](https://titancasket.com/blogs/funeral-guides-and-more/how-much-do-caskets-cost) gives that range. This is a seller's broad estimate, not an independent national survey. It does not set a limit on what providers can charge. Simpler choices can cost less; premium or custom choices can cost more.

For a separate benchmark, the NFDA's 2023 study put the **national median for a metal burial casket at $2,500**, as shown in [Dignity Memorial's breakdown of that study](https://www.dignitymemorial.com/pre-planning/funeral-costs/average-funeral-cost). The year and type of item matter. It is not a current quote for every material or size.

Use these numbers to set an initial limit, then compare options within it. You are choosing something that suits your family and the service. You do not need to spend the top of the range simply because a more expensive model is available.

## Keep the casket inside the whole funeral budget

The [NFDA's 2023 study](https://content.nfda.org/news/media-center/nfda-news-releases/id/8134/2023-nfda-general-price-list-study-shows-inflation-increasing-faster-than-the-cost-of-a-funeral) reported an **$8,300 national median for a funeral with viewing and burial**. That total already includes a metal casket, along with the services counted in the study. Do not add the casket benchmark to it again.

The study excludes cemetery interment, a monument or grave marker, and cash advances such as flowers and an obituary. Your broader planning goal needs to allow for the parts of the farewell you want beyond the funeral home's services.

This is why one inexpensive item does not settle the whole budget. If a package includes a casket, compare its full total with the other arrangement you are considering. If that item is separate, include it once. Our [funeral expenses checklist](/resources/funeral-expenses-checklist) can help you see the full plan before choosing where to spend more or less.

## Compare the differences that actually matter to you

Material, size, finish, lining, and added features can change the price. Decide which of these matter before choosing a model. A particular color or simple design may be more meaningful to your family than an expensive material.

Ask for a clear description and a total price for each choice you are comparing. Photos can help with appearance, but they do not show every difference. If a model has a special feature or warranty, understand what that feature does and what the warranty covers.

The [FTC's casket guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/funeral-costs-pricing-checklist) cautions that no casket can preserve a body forever. A higher price does not create that result. Decide which look and features matter to your family. The price you pay does not measure the care or respect you feel for the person who died.

## Separate the casket from cemetery purchases

A casket and an outer burial container are different items. The outer container sits around the casket in the ground. It is not part of the casket itself. Buying one does not settle all of the cemetery charges.

A burial space, opening and closing the grave, and a marker also need a place in the total. Confirm the cemetery's own requirements and which costs your funeral-home arrangement already includes.

You do not need to master the whole merchandise list before deciding what to spend. Keep the main groups clear: funeral services, casket, cemetery arrangements, and personal extras. A simple summary makes it easier to see whether a change fits your overall limit or would use money reserved for another part of the plan.

## Compare complete offers before agreeing to a purchase

When a package includes a casket, identify the included choice and whether a different model changes the total. Ask for the revised complete price if you want a substitution. That is more useful than trying to adjust the package from a showroom label alone.

You can also compare a funeral-home offer with a separate retailer's offer. Include delivery, timing, and any applicable charges when looking at the complete purchase. The FTC explains that a funeral home must accept a casket purchased elsewhere without charging a fee for using it.

If the funeral budget is tight, give the person helping you compare options a clear limit. A family member can help keep the choice within that limit while you focus on the service. A respectful farewell does not require the most expensive choice on the list.

## Match the container to burial or cremation

A burial casket, a rental casket used for a viewing, and a direct-cremation container serve different purposes. Price the arrangement your family actually wants rather than applying a burial-casket budget to every type of funeral.

The [FTC's guide to funeral types](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/types-funerals) explains that direct cremation takes place without a viewing or visitation. A provider offering direct cremation must offer an alternative container. If you want a viewing before cremation, discuss the rental and container options for that plan and the complete price.
