**Quick answer:** Medicare does not pay for a funeral, burial or cremation. Medicare Part A can cover hospice care for eligible people during life. A surviving spouse or qualifying child may be able to get a separate $255 death payment from Social Security.

## Identify which bill you are looking at

In the days after someone dies, mail can arrive from the hospital, hospice agency, Medicare plan and funeral home. Even when those bills arrive together, they are for different services. Sort them by provider and date before paying. A bill for medical care that happened before death is different from a bill for transferring the person to a funeral home afterward.

[Original Medicare](https://www.medicare.gov/providers-services/original-medicare) consists of Part A, which helps cover hospital care and certain other services, and Part B, which covers services such as physician visits, tests and preventive care. Funeral arrangements are outside that coverage. A Medicare Advantage card identifies a health plan. If someone mentions an extra benefit tied to a particular plan, check the plan’s official documents or call member services to confirm exactly what it provides. Keep any separate life insurance policy or prepaid funeral agreement with your other records so it is easy to find.

## What Medicare hospice coverage can help with

**Hospice** is care that focuses on comfort at the end of life. [Medicare Part A hospice coverage](https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/hospice-care) can help with services that the hospice team provides or arranges under the care plan for eligible patients. Ask the team how it handles medication, equipment, short-term inpatient care or respite care. Respite is a short stay that can give a caregiver a break.

Medicare’s hospice page explains that it generally does not pay room and board for hospice care in a home or facility, with exceptions for qualifying short-term inpatient or respite care arranged by the hospice team. Ask which organization bills you if the person was receiving hospice in a nursing facility. If a hospital or ambulance bill arrives, confirm the date of care and talk to the hospice and Medicare or the plan before assuming the bill was processed correctly.

| Charge or question | Who can confirm it |
| --- | --- |
| Hospice services or symptom-control medication | Hospice agency and Medicare |
| Room and board at a nursing facility | Facility billing office and hospice team |
| Hospital care or ambulance ride before death | Medical provider and Medicare or the plan |
| Transfer to the funeral home, service or cremation | Funeral provider |
| Cemetery plot or opening and closing a grave | Cemetery office |

A hospice social worker can help identify local resources and walk through who to call. Funeral prices and payment dates come from the funeral provider or cemetery, so ask those businesses for written estimates.

## Look for separate funding for the funeral

The [Social Security Administration](https://www.ssa.gov/personal-record/when-someone-dies/lump-sum-death-payment) says an eligible surviving spouse may receive a **$255** one-time payment. If there is no eligible spouse, some children may qualify. The agency says you must apply within two years. Ask Social Security about your situation. This payment has its own eligibility and timing, so only include it in your available budget once you know it is coming and when it can be used.

For a veteran, check the [VA burial allowance](https://www.va.gov/burials-memorials/veterans-burial-allowance/) and other VA burial benefits. For a family with limited resources, ask county or state human services about possible local assistance before paying a deposit. Then look for a prepaid funeral agreement, existing savings or a life insurance policy. If you are the beneficiary of a policy, ask the insurer what it needs to process the claim and which payment options it offers.

## Make a plan for the money due first

In an **illustrative example**, a funeral provider quotes $3,200 for the selected arrangements and requires a $900 deposit. Even if Social Security later confirms the family qualifies for $255, the family still needs to plan for the $900 deposit if it comes due first. The $255 would reduce the full $3,200 cost to $2,945 if applied to the bill. Ask which expenses can wait and what can be adjusted if the first payment is hard to cover.

If you’re planning ahead, use the full estimate to check what savings and existing life insurance could cover. Once you’ve accounted for those funds and any benefits you can confirm, a remaining gap gives you a starting point for comparing additional burial coverage.

If you are planning in advance, keep one list of medical coverage contacts and another list for funeral costs and funding. To compare it with the savings or life coverage you already have, [schedule a phone consultation](/book) and bring the numbers you know.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does hospice pay to move someone to the funeral home after death?**

The funeral provider can tell you whether pickup and transportation are included in its price. Medicare hospice covers eligible end-of-life care under a care plan and does not pay the funeral home’s transfer bill.

**Do I need to contact Medicare after someone dies?**

[USAGov](https://www.usa.gov/report-a-death) says the funeral director should report the death to Social Security, which notifies Medicare. If the funeral director did not report it, notify Social Security as soon as possible.

**Is the $255 payment included with Medicare?**

No. It is a separate Social Security payment available to qualifying survivors. Contact Social Security to ask whether you qualify and how to apply.
