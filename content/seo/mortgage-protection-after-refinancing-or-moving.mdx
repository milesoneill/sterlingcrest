**Quick answer:** A refinance or move is a reason to review coverage, not a rule that you must replace it. Compare the new loan balance and payoff date with your current benefit and coverage period. A personal life policy and loan-linked credit life work differently. Read the contract before assuming an old plan follows the new loan or ends with the old one.

## Put the old and new timelines next to each other

A refinance changes your loan. [Freddie Mac explains the process](https://myhome.freddiemac.com/refinancing/planning-to-refinance): a new mortgage replaces the old one and has its own term, rate, and payment. A lower monthly bill does not always mean an earlier payoff.

For your coverage review, note the new balance, payment, and planned end date. Then find the current policy's benefit and end date. Do those two timelines still fit the family's goal?

Here is a hypothetical example. A term policy has 18 years left. A new loan is set to run for 25 years. There is a seven-year gap if the family wants life coverage for the full loan period. This does not prove that they need a new policy. They may have other resources or a different goal for those later years. It does show a gap that the lower loan payment alone would not reveal.

## Check whether coverage is tied to a person or a debt

A personal life policy used for mortgage protection is generally on a person's life. It is not insurance on the house. A move alone does not tell you that the policy needs replacing. Check its terms, required payments, owner, named recipient, and any assignment before deciding what continues.

Credit life linked to a lender has a different purpose. It is built around a covered debt, as the [OCC's credit life guide explains](https://www.helpwithmybank.gov/help-topics/personal-auto-loans/credit-life-insurance/credit-insurance-life.html). If that debt is replaced or paid off, review what the old plan's terms say. Do not assume it transfers to another loan or bank.

Also keep life coverage separate from homeowners insurance and default insurance for a lender. They do different jobs. Updating the homeowners policy for a new house does not settle what happens to life coverage. Each contract needs its own review.

## A larger balance and a longer term create different questions

A move to a more expensive home or a cash-out refinance may increase the amount the household owes. If the benefit stays the same, the portion of the loan it could cover may be smaller. Decide whether the goal remains a full payoff or has shifted to reducing the debt or providing time to adjust.

A longer term creates a timing question even if the balance barely changes. A shorter term can reduce the years of mortgage need, but it may raise monthly demands on the survivor. Review both amount and duration rather than using one as a shortcut for the other.

Also revisit costs beyond principal. A different property may change taxes, homeowners insurance, maintenance, association dues, or travel costs. Paying off a loan does not eliminate those bills. Include them when considering how the survivor would afford to remain in the new home.

## A smaller loan may leave other family needs

A move to a lower-cost home or a large payment toward principal may reduce the mortgage goal. That does not settle whether the family still needs the life benefit. Income support, childcare, help for a dependent, or moving costs could remain after the debt shrinks.

Separate why you first bought the policy from what it does now. Coverage first bought for a home loan may serve a broader need today. It may also be more than the family now needs if other resources have grown. Start with the current budget and goals.

Changing coverage can affect terms you cannot simply restore later. The [NAIC's life insurance buying guidance](https://content.naic.org/article/consumer-insight-want-purchase-life-insurance-here-are-tips-help-you-through-process) calls for care before dropping one policy for another. A smaller loan is useful information. It is not, by itself, a full review of the plan.

## Update records outside the mortgage closing

A closing and an insurer's file are separate processes. Confirm the insurer has your current address and payment details. Review the named recipient if your family changed, such as a marriage, separation, or new child around the time of the move.

If the policy has a collateral assignment, check who holds it. [Progressive explains this lender-related arrangement](https://www.progressive.com/answers/life-insurance-collateral-assignment/). Confirm whether any release or update is recorded. Do not assume selling the house removed every lender-related entry. The actual forms need review; the policy's nickname cannot answer that question.

For loan-linked coverage, check the terms for an end to coverage, cancellation, transfer, or refund. Do not assume a refund is owed just because the loan changed. Find the rule that applies to the agreement, then confirm the result for that debt. Keep a note of unresolved items so they can be checked rather than guessed.

## Decide what to keep, change, or investigate

Finish with three notes: your current housing goal, what the existing policy can do, and any open contract question. That is enough to focus the next discussion. A move does not have to become an automatic policy switch.

If you consider a new plan, keep an estimate or application separate from coverage in force. Do not cancel the old policy because you expect the new one to have the same price, benefit, or start date. Review both contracts and how the change would take place. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I have to buy new life insurance when I refinance?**

A refinance alone does not establish that need. Review whether your existing coverage still fits the amount and years you want protected, and confirm any loan-linked provisions. The result may be to keep the policy, investigate a gap, or consider a change.

**Will coverage automatically transfer to a new mortgage?**

Do not assume so. An individual policy may continue according to its own terms, while lender-linked coverage can be specific to the original debt. Identify the product and confirm the terms before treating a new loan as covered.

**What if I paid off the mortgage completely?**

Review remaining family needs before deciding the insurance has no purpose. Also check any lender-linked termination or refund terms and any recorded assignment. A payoff changes the planning question, but it does not answer every contract or household question.
