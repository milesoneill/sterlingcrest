**Quick answer:** After losing a spouse, separate two tasks: dealing with any insurance they had and reviewing the policies that cover you. Your own named beneficiaries, payment source, and funeral-cost plan may need attention. You do not have to make an immediate purchase decision to get those records in order.

## Your spouse’s claim and your own coverage are different tasks

A claim on an existing policy concerns insurance that was in place on the person who died. Reviewing your own coverage concerns the needs and records of a living insured person. Keeping the two tasks separate helps prevent confusion when company letters arrive close together.

The [Texas life insurance guide](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/pubs/consumer/cb018.html) discusses proof of death and checking who is named to receive the benefit. Its payment deadlines apply in Texas. Ask the company handling your spouse's policy what it needs for that claim. Your own policy needs a separate review.

Make two small groups of records if that is helpful. One can hold claim information and contacts for your spouse's insurance. The other can hold the policies that insure you. A payment from one policy does not tell you what the other one currently covers.

If a relative is helping, explain which task you want help with. They might organize a folder or take notes on a call without making insurance choices for you. You do not need to turn a practical records discussion into a same-day decision about new coverage.

## Check who is named on the policies that cover you

If your spouse was your beneficiary, look at who else is named on the insurer's record. There may be another person or group already named, or there may be a gap in the plan you need help addressing.

The [California life insurance guide](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/105-type/95-guides/07-life/life-ins-guide.cfm) recommends reviewing policies as your needs and finances change. For your beneficiaries, check the current names and contact details with the insurer. A name on a personal note is not a substitute for the company's record.

Before choosing a new person, think about the purpose of the policy. If it is intended to help with your funeral, who would most likely make the arrangements? If it serves other family needs as well, keep those needs in the discussion. Legal or trust questions may require separate professional help.

When you make a permitted change, use the insurer's process and keep its reply. Our guide to [choosing a final expense beneficiary](/resources/choose-final-expense-insurance-beneficiary) can help you organize the practical questions without assuming one answer fits every family.

## Make the payment and contact details manageable

Sometimes the person who handled the household bills was also the person who kept insurance details in their head. Check which account pays the premiums, how often the payment is due, and where notices are sent.

If an account will be changed or closed, confirm with the insurer how that affects your payments. Do not assume an old bank draft will carry on in the same way. A statement showing a past payment is useful history, but it does not confirm the next payment has been made.

Choose a way of keeping track that fits your routine. A calendar reminder or a short note beside other monthly bills may be enough. If someone else will help pay, agree on who will deal directly with the insurer so each person is not assuming the other has handled it.

Keep the insurer's phone number and policy number somewhere you can find without searching through every financial document. That small step makes a later question easier to handle, especially when several companies have similar names.

## Review your own funeral-cost plan at a pace that works for you

Your wishes may be much the same as before, or you may want to revisit them. Either is a reasonable starting point. The purpose is to give the person who may help with future arrangements a clearer picture of what you want and how it would be funded.

Start with the expenses you want to plan for and the money already intended for them. A change in household income can make an old monthly payment feel different, even when the policy itself has not changed. The [North Carolina shopping guide](https://www.ncdoi.gov/shopping-life-insurance) starts with the coverage you need and the premium you can afford.

Avoid counting the same funds toward several goals at once. Money needed for current living expenses should not quietly become the answer to every future funeral cost too. If you are unsure about a fund's intended purpose, keep that question open rather than force it into the estimate.

Our guide to [final expense planning when you live alone](/resources/final-expense-planning-when-you-live-alone) can help you think about the practical handoff. It does not require you to live alone for good or make every decision now.

## A review can lead to keeping a policy

A useful review may confirm that an existing policy still does the job you want. It may reveal a record update, a payment issue, or a question about the amount of coverage. Those are different findings, and none should be turned automatically into a replacement sale.

If you do explore new insurance, compare it with what you already have before considering a change. Keep the policy's actual terms and your current budget in view. A quote or application is not confirmation that new coverage has started.

You can begin a conversation with one question, such as who is currently named or what amount you want to discuss for future expenses. Claim decisions, legal matters, and changes to company records stay with the right source. Your own planning can move one clear step at a time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need to replace my policy because my spouse died?**

Not automatically. Begin with the current policy, payment, and beneficiary details. The review may call for a record update or simply a clearer plan. A new purchase should be a separate decision based on your needs and available options.

**Can I apply for a new policy to help pay for my spouse’s funeral?**

New life insurance cannot cover a death that has already occurred. For that funeral, focus on existing insurance, available funds, and any benefits that apply. Your own future coverage is a separate question.

**Can an adult child join a phone discussion?**

You can choose who helps you discuss your plans. Be clear about what information you want to share and what help you want. Joining a conversation does not by itself give another person ownership or permission to change your policy.
