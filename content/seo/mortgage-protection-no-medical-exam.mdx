**Quick answer:** Some life insurance policies used for mortgage protection can be issued without an exam. The insurer may still ask about your health and review outside information. Ask what happens if it needs more information, when coverage would start, and whether the policy amount and term fit your mortgage plan.

## What “no medical exam” actually means

Mortgage protection life insurance is life insurance purchased to help with housing costs if the insured person dies. It can be an individual term policy, which provides coverage for a set period. A no-exam application can let the insurer decide without collecting measurements or samples at an in-person exam. It may still require health questions and authorization to review information.

Underwriting is how the insurer evaluates an application and decides whether to offer coverage and at what price. The [NAIC explains accelerated underwriting](https://content.naic.org/insurance-topics/accelerated-underwriting), which uses application answers and other information to assess some applicants without the traditional exam. If the insurer can’t make a decision through that process, it may ask for additional information or an exam. Ask your agent how that next step would work before you apply.

## Compare the process and the policy

Two offers can both advertise no exam and still differ in the amount, term, premium and how the death benefit works. Read the illustration or policy information for the exact offer. Ask whether the initial premium is guaranteed for the period you want and how long you can keep the policy.

| What the insurer asks for | What to clarify |
| --- | --- |
| An application with health questions | Which answers or records could trigger follow-up questions or an exam? |
| Permission to check outside data | What information may be reviewed, and how can you correct an error if you find one? |
| A follow-up interview or exam if needed | What are the next steps and does the initial quote still apply? |
| A first premium payment and signed documents | What exact conditions must be met before the coverage takes effect? |

A policy that asks fewer questions is often described as simplified issue. Ask whether the insurer uses that label and what information it still reviews. The labels describe the application path. The contract spells out when a benefit is payable. If there is a waiting period for any causes of death or a limited benefit at first, ask how that would affect your goal of covering the house.

## Use the same coverage goal for every quote

Consider this hypothetical. A borrower wants $250,000 of coverage for 20 years to leave money for the household’s home loan. One exam-free application can consider that amount and term. Another offer tops out at $150,000. The second offer leaves a $100,000 gap before counting any existing life insurance. Comparing premiums alone would miss why the second offer costs less or why it may be a poor fit.

Decide how much life insurance you want for the home and how long you expect your family to need it. Use the [mortgage protection quote checklist](/resources/mortgage-protection-quote-checklist) to collect comparable terms. If you already have a policy, keep paying for it while you review new offers unless you’ve decided otherwise after confirming what coverage would be in force.

## Plan for a follow-up request

Before starting an application, ask whether the insurer can keep reviewing the same case if it needs an exam or whether you’ll need a different application. Ask who will schedule the exam, what information you’ll need to provide and when you can expect to hear about the next step. If you’re comparing coverage close to your home purchase, leave room for that follow-up before relying on the policy in your housing plan.

If the insurer offers a smaller benefit than you requested, check whether the offer would meet your goal with the coverage you already have. An amount that covers several months of payments may still be useful if you expected help with the monthly bill. A full-payoff plan needs its own calculation.

## Know what to bring to the application

Answer questions accurately. Gather the medication names and dates or details the insurer asks for rather than relying on memory. Ask the insurer or agent to explain any question that could be read two ways. Review the full application before signing. The [NAIC’s Life Insurance Buyer’s Guide](https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/publication-lig-lp-consumer-life.pdf) explains why accurate answers matter when the insurer later checks information in the application.

If an insurer changes the terms after reviewing the application, compare the actual offer with your original goal. A pending application does not establish that the policy is active. Get the effective date and any remaining requirements in writing. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does no exam mean the insurer won’t ask about my medical history?**

Many exam-free applications still ask health questions. The insurer may also review information you authorize or request more details before deciding.

**Does no-exam life insurance start the same day I apply?**

The effective date depends on the policy and the insurer’s requirements. Ask for written confirmation of when coverage begins.

**Will I get a lower premium if I skip the exam?**

The premium depends on the policy and the insurer’s underwriting decision. Compare the actual offers using the same death benefit, coverage period and premium schedule.

**Can I decline an exam if the insurer later asks for one?**

Ask the insurer or agent what happens to that application if you decline. You can compare other options, but another insurer may have different requirements and coverage terms.
