**Quick answer:** Homeowners insurance helps cover property damage and certain liability claims under the policy. Mortgage protection life insurance can provide money toward the home loan if the insured person dies. Check the covered event and who receives the payment. Those details tell you which kind of protection you’re looking at.

## Three names you may see when you buy a home

Homeowners insurance is a property policy. It can help pay for covered damage to the house and belongings and provide liability coverage, which helps if you’re legally responsible for certain injuries or damage to someone else’s property. Which losses qualify depends on the contract.

Mortgage protection life insurance is life coverage purchased with the home loan in mind. Its death benefit is the amount payable under the life policy if the insured person dies and the claim is covered. An individual policy often pays a person you name as beneficiary. Some loan-linked policies pay the lender. Check the policy before you assume where the money would go.

The third term is mortgage insurance. Private mortgage insurance, or PMI, is one example. Mortgage insurance protects the lender in certain circumstances if the borrower stops paying. That can be a separate charge on a home loan. If you see it listed in your paperwork, check our [guide to mortgage protection and PMI](/resources/mortgage-protection-vs-pmi) for a closer look.

| Coverage you see | When it may pay | Where to check what you have |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Homeowners insurance | A covered property loss or liability claim | Homeowners declarations page and property policy |
| Mortgage protection life insurance | A covered death of the insured person | Life insurance policy and beneficiary or lender-payment terms |
| Mortgage insurance | A covered lender loss related to borrower default | Loan documents and mortgage statement |

## What the homeowners policy does for your house

Start with the declarations page, a summary near the front of the policy that lists the insured address, coverage amounts and deductible. The deductible is the part of a covered loss you may have to pay before insurance pays, depending on the policy. Read the policy itself for exclusions, which are circumstances the contract leaves out. Ask your property insurer what would apply to losses you worry about, such as a kitchen fire, water entering the house or damage to belongings.

If you have a mortgage, your lender typically requires homeowners insurance. The premiums, or the cost to keep the policy in place, can be collected through escrow. Escrow is an account the mortgage servicer uses to hold money for certain costs like property taxes and insurance. Some borrowers pay those bills directly. You can check your Closing Disclosure or call your servicer to find out how you pay yours.

Ask the property insurer how a claim check would be issued and whether the mortgage company could be listed on it. A check intended to repair the home and a life insurance benefit meant to help the household keep up with the loan serve different needs.

## What life insurance can do for the people in the home

Consider how the monthly housing bill would get paid if a person whose income supports it died. With an individual policy that names a family member, the person receiving the benefit can generally choose to put money toward the mortgage or use it for other needs. If the policy pays the lender, read how the insurer determines the amount and what the policy covers.

Existing life insurance may already do this job. Look for the amount, the end date and who would receive the money. Decide how much of that coverage your household has earmarked for childcare, household bills or other debts. That exercise can show whether the mortgage is already accounted for.

## A hypothetical example with two separate events

Imagine Elena and Chris own a home with a monthly housing payment of $1,900. A covered kitchen fire damages cabinets and wiring. They would ask their property insurer how to file a claim, which repairs are covered and how the deductible applies. While repairs are under way, they still need a plan for the mortgage payment.

Now imagine Chris dies and Elena remains in the home. If Chris had an individual life policy in force that named Elena and the claim were payable, she could use part of the benefit toward the loan. Six months of the $1,900 housing payment would be $11,400. That’s an illustration of one possible use of the money. It’s separate from any claim for damage to the building.

## Find out which coverage appears on your paperwork

Find the name of the insurer and the type of policy. Your home loan documents may show homeowners insurance, escrow or mortgage insurance. A life insurance contract will identify the insured person and say who receives the benefit. If the description on a bill is unclear, ask the insurer or mortgage servicer which contract the charge belongs to.

If the documents show property coverage and you still want to understand what the household could afford after the death of a borrower, [book a brief phone consultation](/book) with your monthly payment and any life benefit you already have. You can also use our [mortgage protection coverage guide](/resources/how-much-mortgage-protection-insurance-do-i-need) to write down the amount you want the family to have available.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Will homeowners insurance pay off my loan if I die?**

Homeowners insurance covers property and liability losses described by the policy. Life insurance is the coverage to review if you want a benefit that could help your family pay the loan after your death.

**Does mortgage protection life insurance cover repairs?**

A life policy pays after a covered death. Review your homeowners policy for covered repairs, the deductible and any exclusions.

**Are both premiums part of escrow?**

Homeowners insurance may be paid through escrow. An individual life policy is usually billed separately. Ask the servicer or insurer if you’re unsure which contract a charge belongs to.

**Do I need a policy with mortgage protection in its name?**

You can compare ordinary life policies as well as products marketed for mortgages. Look at the benefit amount, who receives it and how long coverage lasts.
