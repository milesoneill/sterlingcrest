**Quick answer:** A VA-backed home loan helps eligible borrowers get a mortgage by protecting the lender against part of its loss if the borrower defaults. Veterans’ Mortgage Life Insurance (VMLI) is a separate VA program that can pay an eligible veteran’s lender after a covered death. A VA loan by itself leaves the mortgage in place when the borrower dies.

## What a VA loan guarantee covers

A VA-backed purchase loan usually comes from a bank, credit union or mortgage company. The Department of Veterans Affairs guarantees part of the loan, which means it can cover part of the lender’s loss if the borrower defaults. That backing can help eligible borrowers qualify for favorable loan terms. [VA-backed purchase loans](https://www.va.gov/housing-assistance/home-loans/loan-types/purchase-loan/) also don’t require private mortgage insurance (PMI), the coverage some conventional lenders require to protect themselves if a borrower falls behind.

You may see a VA funding fee on your closing paperwork. It’s a one-time charge for many borrowers that helps fund the VA loan program. Some borrowers are exempt. Life insurance comes from a separate policy. Check your insurance records to see what money would be available to your family after your death.

## Who can apply for Veterans’ Mortgage Life Insurance?

VMLI is a separate VA life insurance program for eligible service members and veterans with a severe service-connected disability. VA says you must have received a Specially Adapted Housing grant, have title to the home, have a mortgage on it and apply before you turn 70. The grant helps eligible borrowers buy, build or adapt a home for independent living. Your VA loan guaranty agent can help you check eligibility and complete VA Form 29-8636. The home covered by VMLI must be your primary residence. Confirm eligibility and next steps with the [VA’s VMLI program](https://www.va.gov/life-insurance/options-eligibility/vmli/).

A VA-backed purchase loan and a Certificate of Eligibility for a VA home loan are different from a VMLI policy. If you think you already have VMLI, look for written confirmation of the policy and its coverage. If you only have the documents from closing, contact the VA Insurance Center to check.

## How much can VMLI pay toward the mortgage?

VMLI is decreasing-term life insurance. Its benefit tracks the balance you still owe, up to $200,000, and ends when you pay off the mortgage. VA pays the benefit directly to the lender. Your family would need separate money for utilities, groceries, a funeral and other expenses outside the mortgage.

| Example mortgage balance | How the VMLI limit applies if coverage is in force |
| --- | --- |
| $165,000 | The balance is below the $200,000 limit, so the benefit can cover the $165,000 balance. |
| $245,000 | VMLI can pay up to $200,000. The example leaves $45,000 of the balance to fund separately. |

These examples show how the cap works. Ask the servicer for an actual payoff amount when you need one. The [CFPB explains](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-payoff-amount-and-is-it-the-same-as-my-current-balance-en-205/) that a payoff amount can include interest through the payoff date and other charges, so it can differ from the balance on the last mortgage statement.

## Compare what the money would do

| What you have | Who the arrangement protects | What it can do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| VA-backed home loan | The guarantee covers part of the lender’s loss | Helps an eligible borrower obtain a mortgage |
| VMLI policy | The mortgage lender receives the insurance payment | Pays the outstanding mortgage balance up to the program cap after a covered death |
| Individual life insurance naming a family member | The named family member receives the death benefit if the claim is payable | Can give the family money for the mortgage and other needs |

Start with the amount your family would need to keep the home for a few months or pay down the loan. Then list the life insurance you already own and who would receive each benefit. A policy that pays the lender can reduce the debt. A policy that pays a family member can give them flexibility with the rest of the household budget. Check the contract for the actual benefit, the term and the beneficiary before you count on that money.

If you want help comparing an existing policy with the balance you still owe, you can [schedule a brief consultation](/book). Keep a copy of the latest mortgage statement and policy summaries handy so the conversation starts with your figures.

## Check your policy before a move or refinance

VA asks VMLI policyholders to report changes such as moving, selling, refinancing or transferring the mortgage to another lender. A longer loan term or higher balance can affect your premium after a refinance. Before you sign the new loan, ask VA how the change would affect your policy and check whether your family’s other coverage still fits.

Before your next annual review, write the date next to each figure you use. The balance on a mortgage statement can change monthly, and a private term policy can have a different end date from the loan. Recheck both after a move, refinance or change in household income. Keep the VA confirmation, insurer details and mortgage servicer’s number somewhere your family can find them. If a borrower has already died, call the servicer to confirm the amount due and how to keep making payments while any insurance claim is being processed. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does a VA loan pay off the mortgage when the veteran dies?**

The VA loan guarantee protects the lender against part of a loss after a default. A life insurance policy, such as VMLI if eligible and in force, may pay toward the mortgage after a covered death.

**Does the VA funding fee buy mortgage life insurance?**

The funding fee helps support the VA home loan program. VMLI has its own eligibility requirements, application and premiums.

**Can VMLI pay my spouse directly?**

VA pays the VMLI benefit to the lender that holds the mortgage. A separate life insurance policy can name your spouse as the beneficiary.

**What if my mortgage is larger than $200,000?**

VMLI pays up to $200,000. Check your loan balance and ask the servicer for a payoff quote to see how much your household would need to cover from other sources.
