**Quick answer:** If you received life insurance proceeds, you can compare paying off the mortgage with keeping cash for near-term expenses. Get a dated payoff quote, then see how much cash each option would leave and which home costs would still be due. Your best fit depends on whether you plan to keep the home and how steady the household income is.

## Check who controls the proceeds

The beneficiary is the person or organization named to receive a life insurance benefit. When an individual policy pays a family member directly, that person can generally decide how to spend the money. Review the policy if a lender is named or if there’s a collateral assignment. An assignment can give the lender a claim on some of the benefit. Our [beneficiary guide](/resources/mortgage-protection-insurance-beneficiary) explains why the named recipient matters.

If the benefit is available to you, list any bills that are coming due before comparing the options. Include housing costs, funeral expenses, childcare and any changes to the household’s income. Decide who will keep track of the mortgage payment while you get the figures together.

## Get the real cost of paying off the loan

A payoff quote is a dated statement of the amount needed to clear the loan. It can differ from the balance shown on your monthly statement because it includes interest through the payoff date and may include fees. The [CFPB’s payoff guide](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-payoff-amount-and-is-it-the-same-as-my-current-balance-en-205/) explains how to get the amount from your servicer.

Ask whether an early payoff charge applies, how to send the payment securely and how the servicer will confirm it was applied. If there’s a second mortgage or home equity line, request a separate payoff quote for it. If part of your current mortgage payment goes into escrow, ask who will bill you for property taxes and homeowners insurance after payoff. An escrow account holds money the servicer collects for those expenses.

## Compare what you’d have left under each option

| Option | What it changes | What to check first |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pay off the mortgage | Removes principal and interest from the monthly budget. | How much cash remains after the payoff and how you’ll pay taxes, insurance and upkeep. |
| Continue monthly payments | Keeps more cash available as you decide what to do with the home. | Whether household income and savings can support the regular payment. |
| Make a partial principal payment | Reduces the amount you owe. | Whether the servicer offers a recast, which recalculates the scheduled payment after a large principal reduction on an eligible loan. |

A partial payment alone might leave the required monthly payment the same. Ask what your loan allows before sending money if a smaller monthly bill is the reason for paying extra. [Fannie Mae’s recast overview](https://singlefamily.fanniemae.com/job-aid/loan-delivery/topic/recast_mortgage.htm) explains the difference between paying principal and recalculating the required payment.

## Try a hypothetical household budget

Imagine a $250,000 life insurance payout, a $205,000 mortgage payoff quote and a $1,900 monthly mortgage payment. A full payoff would leave $45,000 from the benefit. Keeping the mortgage for six months would use $11,400 for those payments if the full $1,900 came from the benefit each month, leaving $238,600 before any other spending. The loan would still have a balance at the end of those six months.

That comparison shows the tradeoff. A payoff lowers the required housing payment, but it puts more of the cash into the home. Keeping payments current preserves cash in the near term, but you still owe the loan. Write down upcoming repairs and other costs before deciding how much money you need on hand. Consider how the numbers change if you expect to move soon, keep the home for years or support another family member.

## If you’re planning ahead, choose a goal your beneficiary can use

Put the loan statement, insurance carrier and policy number in a place your beneficiary can find. Share what you hope the benefit will help with. If your goal is to leave enough to pay off the home and still have money for other bills, plan for both amounts separately. The [coverage worksheet](/resources/how-much-mortgage-protection-insurance-do-i-need) can help you total those needs.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I have to pay off the mortgage with a life insurance benefit?**

If the benefit was paid directly to you as beneficiary, you can generally choose how to use it. Check the policy and any lender assignment if you aren’t sure who is entitled to the money.

**Does paying off a mortgage eliminate property tax and insurance bills?**

Those bills continue. If the servicer paid them through escrow, arrange to receive and pay them directly after the payoff.

**Can I make extra payments before deciding whether to pay off the loan?**

Ask the servicer how to apply an extra amount to principal and whether there are any restrictions. Get written confirmation if you expect the required monthly payment to change.

**Should I use my mortgage balance or payoff quote to compare options?**

Use the balance for a rough estimate and get a dated payoff quote before sending money. Interest and fees can change what is required to clear the loan.
