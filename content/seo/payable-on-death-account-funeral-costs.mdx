**Quick answer:** A payable-on-death account directs a bank to transfer the money in the account to named beneficiaries after the owner dies. The beneficiary can use available funds toward funeral costs. Ask the bank about its claim steps and timing, then make a plan for any bills that come due sooner.

## What the bank designation does

Payable on death, often shortened to POD, is a beneficiary designation on a deposit account. A beneficiary is the person you name to receive the funds. The bank uses the designation in its records after the owner dies. During the owner's life, the account still belongs to the owner. How a jointly owned account works depends on the ownership and the institution’s agreement.

Ask the bank to show you how it lists the owner and beneficiary. Keep the bank’s confirmation with your planning records and tell the beneficiary where to find it. If you switch banks or open a new account, check whether the designation was recorded for the new account. [FDIC guidance on trust accounts and POD designations](https://www.fdic.gov/financial-institution-employees-guide-deposit-insurance/trust-accounts)

## Think about when the funeral bill will be due

A POD balance can be part of a funeral plan. Its usefulness depends on how much is in the account and when the bank can release it. Ask what the beneficiary would need to show, such as identification or a death certificate, and which department handles the request. Find out whether the bank has special instructions if several people are named.

Call a funeral home for a written estimate and ask how much would be due before services. You can put that number next to the amount your family expects to have available right away. A relative may need a plan for the deposit even if the account balance is larger than the final bill. [FTC funeral pricing checklist](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/funeral-costs-pricing-checklist)

## Compare the balance, the bill, and the timing

Suppose you set aside $5,000 and the funeral estimate totals $5,800, with $1,500 due before services. You have an $800 gap in the total budget. If you’re planning ahead, check whether other savings or existing life insurance could cover that gap. Any amount still unfunded is a starting point for comparing additional burial coverage. The $1,500 deposit is a separate question. If the bank or insurer hasn’t released the funds when it’s due, your family will need another way to pay it on time.

| Write down | What it tells your family |
| --- | --- |
| Current bank balance | The amount earmarked today, which can change |
| Funeral home and cemetery estimates | The amount you’re trying to fund |
| Deposit and other due dates | Which payments need a short-term plan |
| Bank’s claim steps | Who should call and what papers to gather |

## Coordinate the plan with the person you name

Tell the beneficiary how you hope the money will be used and who is expected to speak with the funeral home. If a different relative will arrange the funeral, introduce the two roles in your written instructions. Ask the bank what happens if your named beneficiary dies first, and whether it accepts a backup designation. If your family needs a binding arrangement for how the money will be used, ask a qualified estate attorney about the options in your state.

Consider how expenses during your lifetime might change the account balance. For example, if you use $2,000 for a home repair, update the plan to reflect the smaller amount. Check it again after you obtain a newer funeral estimate.

## Update the plan when the account changes

If you switch to a new bank or move the money to another account, ask the institution which beneficiary instructions apply to the new account. A family member following an outdated note could call the wrong institution or give the bank an old account reference. Keep one current record with the bank’s name and the date you confirmed the designation.

## See whether savings or insurance fits the remaining need

The POD account is money you set aside. A life insurance policy pays a death benefit according to its terms when coverage is in force, and the benefit and premium are listed in the contract. Compare the balance you can build, how long it may take to reach your savings goal, and any existing policy your family could use. If those figures still leave a gap and you want to see how available policy terms and premiums would fit, [schedule a consultation](/book) with a licensed agent.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Will the bank pay the funeral home directly?**

A POD designation directs the bank to transfer funds to the named beneficiary. Ask the bank how it releases the funds and discuss payment with the person arranging the funeral.

**Can I name more than one person?**

Ask your bank whether it accepts more than one beneficiary and how it allocates the balance. Keep a copy of the confirmation and recheck it if your family situation changes.

**Can a POD beneficiary access the money while I’m alive?**

The designation directs payment after death. Ask the bank what separate authority, if any, would be needed for someone to help manage money during your life.
