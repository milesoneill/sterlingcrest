**Quick answer:** Direct cremation is cremation without a viewing or funeral ceremony with the body present. You can still hold a memorial afterward. If your family wants a viewing or funeral first, ask for a separate quote for the staff, room, preparation and any casket rental.

## Choose when your family wants to gather

Picture the people you want there. Do they want to see the person who died before the cremation, or would they prefer to gather around photographs and memories later? That one question can make the options clearer.

With **direct cremation**, the funeral provider arranges cremation without a viewing or ceremony with the body present. The provider returns the cremated remains according to the arrangements you make. Your family can plan a later memorial at a funeral home, a place of worship, a community room or another permitted venue. The later gathering has its own timing and budget.

With **cremation after a funeral or viewing**, the body is present for part of the arrangements before cremation. Ask what is included if you only want a brief private goodbye. A private viewing, a public visitation and a full ceremony can involve different space, staffing and preparation. If a package says “cremation with service,” ask whether it means a ceremony before cremation or a memorial afterward.

## Compare three ways to make the arrangements

| Arrangement | What the family can plan | What to ask the provider to price |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct cremation only | A private remembrance or a gathering you organize later | Transfer, crematory charge, container and return of remains |
| Direct cremation with a later memorial | A gathering when relatives can travel or the venue is available | Direct cremation separately from the venue, staff, urn or memorial items |
| Viewing or funeral before cremation | An opportunity to see the person who died or hold a ceremony before cremation | Viewing and ceremony fees, preparation, a rental casket if offered and cremation |

You can call several providers with the same description. “We want cremation without a viewing, and we will arrange a memorial ourselves. What is the total for pickup at this location and return of the remains?” If someone in the family wants a goodbye first, ask for a second total with just that private viewing added. Give each provider the same starting details.

## Use national costs to frame the local quote

The [National Funeral Directors Association’s 2024 report](https://nfda.org/Portals/0/2024_NFDA_Cremation%20and%20Burial%20Report.pdf) publishes U.S. median charges from its **2023** member price study. It lists **$2,645** for direct cremation with a family-provided container and **$2,750** with a funeral-home-provided container. It lists **$6,280** for an adult casketed funeral with a viewing and ceremony followed by cremation. The latter reflects a more involved service and excludes items such as cemetery charges and miscellaneous fees paid to outside vendors. A median is a midpoint in the study, so compare these older figures with current written quotes in your area.

If you choose direct cremation and a later memorial, the relevant total is both bills combined. In an **illustrative example**, a $2,700 direct cremation quote and a $650 memorial budget add to $3,350. If another provider quotes $5,600 for the viewing and cremation your family wants, check whether its estimate also includes the urn or any venue charge you would pay separately. Your family can weigh the difference alongside the kind of goodbye you want.

If you’re planning ahead and prefer the $3,350 arrangement in this example, use that figure to review what you’ve saved and any life insurance you expect your family to use. If a viewing matters to your family, do the same with the full cost of that option. Any gap can help you decide how much more to save or whether to compare burial coverage.

## Ask four questions before you sign

1. **Does this total include the cremation itself?** Ask whether a separate crematory charges you directly.
2. **Which container is included?** Under the [FTC Funeral Rule](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/ftc-funeral-rule), a provider that offers direct cremation must make an alternative container available. For a viewing, ask whether the home offers a rental casket and what comes with it.
3. **What could add to the total?** Give the provider the pickup location. Ask how it prices additional mileage, storage, certified death certificates or special scheduling.
4. **What do we take home?** Ask about the container used to return the remains, the expected pickup process and who can authorize release.

The FTC says funeral providers must give price information by phone when asked and give you an itemized statement after you choose the arrangements. For a broader list of costs you may encounter, see [cremation cost questions](/resources/cremation-costs-questions).

## Decide how to fund the plan ahead of time

Start with the price for the arrangements you want. Add separate expenses such as a memorial venue, travel or burial of the urn, if relevant. Then list which money your family could access when a bill is due and which would arrive later. A life insurance claim and a funeral home deposit can be on different schedules. If you want help comparing an estimate with the funds you expect to have, you can [schedule a phone consultation](/book).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can we still hold a memorial after direct cremation?**

Yes. You can plan a memorial after the cremation and ask the funeral provider to price it separately if you want its venue or staff.

**Is a casket legally required for cremation?**

The FTC says state and local laws do not require a casket for cremation. Ask what alternative container is offered for direct cremation and what the provider uses if you schedule a viewing first.

**Can we see the person privately before cremation?**

Ask the provider what it offers and how that affects the price and timing. A private goodbye may be available as a separate arrangement.
