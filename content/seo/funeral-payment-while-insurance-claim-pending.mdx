**Quick answer:** Call the funeral home and the insurer separately. Ask the funeral home what is due now and whether it offers a written payment arrangement. Ask the insurer what it still needs to process the claim. If the home offers to accept part of the insurance proceeds directly, read the terms before you sign.

## Start with the amount due this week

If the funeral home is asking for money now and the policy claim is still open, give yourself two separate questions. What do you need to pay to make the arrangements you want? What has the insurer actually told the beneficiary about the claim?

Ask the funeral home for an itemized estimate, the deposit amount and the date each remaining payment is due. The [FTC Funeral Rule](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/ftc-funeral-rule) requires covered providers to give you an itemized statement once you choose the arrangements. Before making a selection, ask which costs can change, including third-party services such as the cemetery. Ask whether you can pay the deposit now and set a later due date for the balance. If the home agrees, request those terms in writing.

## Get a claim update from the insurer

A **beneficiary** is the person or entity designated to receive a life insurance benefit. If that is you, contact the insurer’s claims department through its verified number or website. Have the policy number if you can find it, and ask whether the company needs a signed claim form, a certified death certificate or other records. [Navy Mutual’s explanation of its claim process](https://www.navymutual.org/wp-content/uploads/downloadable-forms/LifeInsuranceResourcesDownloadable/What-Happens-When-You-File-a-Life-Insurance-Claim.pdf) gives an example of common documents. Your insurer can confirm what it needs for your claim.

Ask for a claim reference number and a way to check its status. Note the date you sent each document and the date the insurer acknowledged it. If a funeral provider asks when the benefit will arrive, give only the information the insurer confirmed. An estimated review date is different from a payment date. If your insurer needs another document, see our [guide to death certificates and life insurance claims](/resources/death-certificate-life-insurance-claim).

## Compare the options the funeral home offers

| If the provider offers | What to ask for in writing |
| --- | --- |
| A deposit with the balance due later | The deposit, balance, due dates, fees and what happens if the claim is delayed |
| An assignment of some of the proceeds | The amount or rights transferred, who receives payment, any fee and who owes a shortfall |
| Financing through a third party | The total repayment, fees, interest rate, first due date and late-payment terms |
| A smaller arrangement paid up front | An itemized total and what a memorial or other gathering later would cost separately |

An **assignment** can transfer a right to collect all or part of an insurance benefit to someone else under the agreement. If a funeral home or funding company suggests one, ask the insurer whether it can accept that specific form. Confirm who handles the remainder of the benefit and who owes the funeral bill if insurance pays less than expected or the claim is denied. If the document is unclear, ask for time to review it. Our [guide to funeral home payments from life insurance](/resources/funeral-home-life-insurance-payment) goes into the differences.

## Put the dates and amounts on paper

Here is an **illustrative example**. A family selects arrangements that cost $4,800. The provider asks for $1,200 before the service. The beneficiary has filed a claim, but the insurer still needs a certified death certificate. If the provider agrees to a deposit and later payment, the family’s immediate budget is $1,200 and the remaining commitment is $3,600. If the provider offers an assignment with an added fee instead, compare that total with the written deposit plan and ask who is responsible if the claim remains pending on the due date.

If relatives offer to help, agree in advance on how much each person plans to contribute and whether they expect to be paid back. Before signing, check who is named as responsible for the bill. If no option is workable, ask the provider for a lower-cost quote and contact the county about possible local assistance before paying or committing to arrangements.

## Make the next set of calls easier

If you are planning ahead, write down where the policy is kept, how to reach the insurer and where relatives can find your funeral preferences. Keep sensitive records in a secure place. If you want to compare an expected deposit with the money your family can reach quickly, [schedule a phone consultation](/book) and bring those two figures.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can a funeral home require payment before the life insurance claim arrives?**

Ask what payment schedule the provider requires before agreeing to the arrangements. Some may offer a later balance due date or another option, and terms vary.

**Does signing a funeral contract also file the insurance claim?**

Contact the insurer to confirm who needs to file, which documents are required and whether it has received them. An arrangement with a funeral home does not establish that the insurer has everything it needs.

**Can I count on a payout by the funeral date?**

Ask the insurer for the current status. Budget around money already available or payment terms the provider has put in writing while you wait.
