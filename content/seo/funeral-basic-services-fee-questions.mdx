**Quick answer:** About $2,500 is a useful starting point for a funeral home’s basic services fee, based on the $2,495 national median reported for 2023. The fee covers shared work such as planning and paperwork. It is only part of the full funeral cost and may already be included in a package.

## What work does the fee cover?

A funeral takes work beyond the time spent at a viewing or ceremony. Someone needs to make plans, prepare forms, and work with the cemetery or crematory. The fee pays for this work and the business costs of running the funeral home.

The [FTC's funeral pricing guide](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/funeral-costs-pricing-checklist) describes planning, notices, care, and work with other providers. It includes the work of securing permits and death-certificate copies. That does not mean the charge for every certified copy is included in the fee. Check the actual list for those charges. It treats optional merchandise and other services as separate categories. A casket or hearse is not automatically included because the basic fee has been paid.

This explains why a basic services charge can appear even when a family chooses a simple plan. That charge alone does not tell you the final cost. You need both its description and the list of other items you selected.

## How much should you budget for the fee?

The national benchmark is roughly $2,500. [Dignity Memorial’s funeral-cost guide](https://www.dignitymemorial.com/pre-planning/funeral-costs/average-funeral-cost) lists the 2023 National Funeral Directors Association median basic services fee as $2,495. This gives families across the country a starting point, rather than a price from one local business.

A median is the middle reported figure, not a price every funeral home charges. The service you choose, the provider, and your area can change the amount. The study year also matters: a 2023 benchmark is not a promise of a current price.

Use the figure to understand the scale of this charge, then compare complete offers for the arrangements you want. A low basic fee is not proof of a low total. A provider may include more services in a package, or charge for them elsewhere. Your question is what the full plan costs and what remains outside that total.

## Put the fee beside the full funeral budget

For the wider picture, the [NFDA’s 2023 national study](https://content.nfda.org/news/media-center/nfda-news-releases/id/8134/2023-nfda-general-price-list-study-shows-inflation-increasing-faster-than-the-cost-of-a-funeral) reported a median of $8,300 for a funeral with a viewing and burial, and $6,280 for one with a viewing and cremation. The cremation figure is for a funeral with a viewing, not a simple direct cremation.

Those totals exclude cemetery interment, grave markers, and outside purchases such as flowers and obituary notices. They are useful benchmarks for the main funeral arrangement, not all-in promises. Cemetery costs and the personal touches you choose may raise the full amount your family needs.

The basic services fee is already part of those funeral figures. Do not add another $2,500 to them. For your own plan, use one complete provider total, then add only the costs that are not included. This prevents a budget that counts the same work twice.

## Keep four kinds of charges apart

Read the estimate in groups so you can see what the basic fee does and does not explain:

| Group | What you are checking |
| --- | --- |
| Basic services | Shared planning, paperwork, coordination and overhead |
| Other provider services | Transfer, preparation, viewing, ceremony or vehicles you select |
| Merchandise | Casket, urn or another container used in the plan |
| Other businesses | Cemetery, flowers, notices or other outside charges |

You may not need every item in every group. The table helps you see the full offer. It is not a list of things you must buy.

For example, a family choosing cremation without a service should compare complete direct-cremation offers. A family choosing a viewing and burial needs the relevant services and cemetery costs in its comparison. The same basic-fee label does not make those plans equal.

## Do not add the fee to a package that includes it

Suppose the page shows a package total and then lists a basic services charge elsewhere. There are two different possibilities. The package may already contain that work, or the separate charge may apply to a different way of selecting services. Read the package details to see which applies.

A useful question is: Does this total already include your basic services charge, and which other charges would apply to the arrangements we described? Keep the answer beside the package total. Do not add every fee on the general list to it.

If the provider changes the plan, compare the complete revised total with the earlier total. An added service might change more than one line. For instance, changing from a simple cremation to a viewing can affect facilities, care, and merchandise choices. A fresh itemized explanation is more useful than adjusting one number from memory.

## Compare the whole offer by phone

You can gather prices without visiting several showrooms. Describe the same service to each business, including whether a viewing or gathering is planned. Ask for the complete amount, what it includes, and which charges are not yet known.

The [FTC's phone-shopping guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/shopping-funeral-services-phone-or-online) says funeral homes must answer price questions by phone and must let consumers choose the services they want and need. You do not have to treat a package as the only way to buy services.

If a fee concerns an outside business, identify that supplier. For example, a cemetery price is not settled merely because you understand the funeral home's basic fee. The [funeral expenses checklist](/resources/funeral-expenses-checklist) can help you keep the different parts of the total in view.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is the basic services fee the full funeral price?**

No. It describes shared services and overhead. Other services, merchandise, and outside costs can apply to the arrangement you choose. Compare the complete plan rather than treating one line as the total.

**Why can the fee be lower in a direct-cremation package?**

A provider can describe its package as including the basic fee or a reduced one. Read that provider’s actual package terms. Do not add its standalone fee again or assume every business uses the same pricing structure.

**What should I have ready for a consultation?**

Your main question is enough to begin. If you have a rough funeral goal and monthly budget, those can help focus the call. A finished provider estimate is not required to discuss available burial-insurance options.
