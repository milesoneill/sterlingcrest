## What AFib Means

Atrial fibrillation, or AFib, is an irregular heartbeat. The CDC explains that it affects how the heart's upper chambers beat.

That medical definition does not tell you which insurance plan you can buy. Keep medical questions with your care team. For insurance questions, ask an agent to explain the current application and policy terms. The insurer makes the final decision.

## Information to Have Ready

If an insurer asks for health details, you may need an accurate medication list and health history. The NAIC notes that life insurance applications often request information about your medical background.

Have your doctor's name, diagnosis date, and current treatment details available if asked. Do not guess. Provide accurate information so the insurer can evaluate your application correctly. An agent can tell you which details a specific carrier needs.

## Policy Terms to Compare

Ask an agent to explain simplified issue, guaranteed issue, and waiting period. These are terms you may hear when comparing policies. The NAIC describes different policy types in its consumer guide to life insurance.

Check the actual contract for health questions, exams, age limits, state eligibility, benefit start date, coverage limits, exclusions, premiums, and claim terms. The written policy governs what you buy. An agent can walk you through the contract language before you apply.

Guaranteed issue does not mean everyone can buy the product. It means the insurer does not ask health questions, but age, state, and other rules still apply.

## Questions to Ask Before You Apply

Ask a licensed agent these questions about each policy:

- Can I apply for this plan in my state at my age?
- What health questions does the application ask?
- When does the full benefit begin?
- What is the monthly premium for the coverage amount I want?
- Are there exclusions or waiting periods I should know about?

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can this guide tell me if I qualify?**

No. Only an insurer can decide whether you qualify for a specific policy. Each carrier uses its own underwriting rules. An agent can tell you which plans you are eligible to apply for.

**Will I need to answer health questions or take an exam?**

It depends on the policy. Some plans ask health questions but do not require an exam. Others ask no health questions at all. Check the application requirements for each policy you consider.

**When would my family receive the full benefit?**

Read the policy's benefit schedule and exclusions. Ask when the full death benefit becomes available for a covered claim. Any waiting period or limits depend on the contract. A quote alone does not explain every term.

**How can I compare costs?**
