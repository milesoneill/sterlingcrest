## Can You Get Final Expense Insurance With AFib?

Yes. Many people diagnosed with atrial fibrillation can get final expense insurance. AFib causes irregular, often rapid heartbeats. Insurers ask about heart conditions, but a diagnosis doesn't automatically disqualify you.

What matters: your age, state, overall health, how you manage your AFib, and which carrier reviews your application. Each insurer uses different underwriting guidelines. One company's decision doesn't limit your options elsewhere.

## How AFib Affects Your Application

Insurers evaluate risk by asking about heart conditions, medications, recent hospitalizations, and other health factors. AFib is one consideration among many.

Your application may also be influenced by:

- How long you've had AFib and how you treat it
- Whether you take blood thinners or other medications
- Related conditions like high blood pressure or diabetes
- Your age and tobacco use
- Recent hospital stays or procedures

Carriers weigh these factors differently. Some offer standard coverage with higher premiums. Others offer modified or graded benefit policies. A few decline coverage based on their rules.

Comparing options from multiple insurers helps you find a policy that fits your circumstances and budget. A licensed agent walks you through the questions each carrier asks and explains what to expect.

## Types of Final Expense Policies Available With AFib

Final expense insurance comes in several forms. Each has different application requirements and benefit structures.

**Simplified issue policies** ask health questions but skip the medical exam. You answer questions about your medical history, current conditions, and medications. If your answers meet the carrier's guidelines, you may qualify for immediate coverage. Premiums and eligibility depend on your health profile.

**Guaranteed issue policies** skip health questions entirely. These policies are available to applicants within a certain age range who live in states where the product is offered. Because the insurer accepts applicants without reviewing health history, these policies usually include a graded or modified benefit period.

**Graded or modified benefit policies** pay a limited benefit if you pass away during the first two or three years, often returning premiums plus interest or paying a percentage of the death benefit. After the waiting period, the full death benefit is paid. Exact terms depend on the policy and carrier.

Not every carrier offers every type in every state. Availability depends on where you live, your age, and which products each insurer has approved for sale in your area. A licensed professional confirms which options are available to you.

## What to Expect During the Application Process

The application process is typically straightforward. Most policies can be applied for over the phone or online. Decisions often come quickly.

For simplified issue policies, you answer health questions:

- Have you been diagnosed with AFib or other heart conditions?
- What medications do you currently take?
- Have you been hospitalized in the past two years?
- Do you have other conditions such as diabetes, COPD, or kidney disease?

Answer every question honestly. Incomplete or inaccurate answers can delay your application or lead to a denial of benefits later. If you're unsure how to answer, ask the agent for clarification.

For guaranteed issue policies, the insurer confirms your age, state of residence, and that you meet other eligibility requirements. No health questions.

Once you submit your application, the carrier reviews it and makes a decision. Some approvals happen immediately. Others take a few days. If the carrier needs additional information, they contact you or your agent.

Working with a licensed professional makes the process smoother. They help you gather information, explain what each carrier is likely to ask, and guide you toward policies that match your health profile.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Will I be denied final expense insurance because I have AFib?**

Not necessarily. Many people with AFib qualify for coverage. The outcome depends on your age, overall health, how you manage your AFib, and the specific carrier's underwriting rules. Some insurers offer standard coverage, others offer graded benefit policies, and some decline based on their guidelines. Comparing options from multiple carriers increases your chances of finding suitable coverage.

**Do all final expense policies have waiting periods if I have AFib?**

No. Some simplified issue policies offer immediate full coverage if you meet the carrier's health requirements. Guaranteed issue policies often include a graded benefit period, meaning limited benefits are paid if you pass away during the first two or three years. The specific terms depend on the policy type, carrier, and your individual application.

**How much will final expense insurance cost if I have AFib?**

Premiums vary based on your age, tobacco use, overall health, the coverage amount you choose, and the carrier's underwriting decision. AFib may result in higher premiums compared to someone without heart conditions, but the exact cost depends on your individual profile. **Can I get immediate coverage with AFib, or will there always be a waiting period?**

It depends on the policy type and carrier. Some simplified issue policies provide immediate full coverage if you meet the insurer's health criteria. Guaranteed issue policies typically include a waiting period. The only way to know for certain is to apply and receive a decision from the carrier. Guaranteed issue policies typically don't ask health questions. Always answer honestly, as incomplete or inaccurate information can affect your coverage.
