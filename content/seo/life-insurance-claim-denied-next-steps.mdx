**Quick answer:** If a life insurance claim is denied, start with the insurer's written reason and the policy provision it relied on. You may be able to request another review, supply missing evidence, or file a complaint with the appropriate state insurance department. None of those steps guarantees that the decision will change.

## First confirm that it is a denial

For a family expecting insurance to help with funeral expenses, the immediate problem is often a bill that cannot wait. Separate the dispute from the resources available now: existing savings, other policies, confirmed benefits, and any arrangement the funeral provider actually accepts. A newly purchased life policy cannot cover a death that has already occurred.

A request for another document is different from a final denial. So is a notice that a claim remains under review. Find out whether the company has made a decision, which part of the claim is affected, and whether it still needs information.

For example, an insurer might be waiting for a death certificate, a beneficiary form, or medical records. The Florida Department of Financial Services recommends [asking the claims department for an explanation of a delay](https://myfloridacfo.com/division/consumers/understanding-insurance/faq/lifeandannuities). Its specific procedures and time periods concern Florida; they are not nationwide deadlines.

If you received a denial by phone, request the decision in writing. Keep the letter and its envelope or delivery date. Record the claim number and a reliable contact for the person handling it, so the next conversation concerns the same decision.

## Match the explanation to the actual policy

Read the precise provision cited in the letter alongside the insurer's explanation of the facts. A statement that coverage had lapsed raises different questions from a statement about an exclusion or an application answer.

For a lapse issue, identify the premium due date, payments received, notices, and the policy status the company says applied on the date of death. For a disputed application answer, locate the question, the answer recorded, and the evidence the company says conflicts with it. Do not rewrite a timeline from memory when bank records or application documents are available.

An early claim investigation also differs from a graded-benefit restriction. Our [contestability guide](/resources/final-expense-insurance-contestability-period) explains why those concepts should not be treated as interchangeable. The actual contract and applicable law govern the case; a general article cannot establish whether a particular denial is valid.

## Gather evidence that addresses the reason

Build the response around the stated issue rather than sending every family document. A payment dispute may call for a bank record and the insurer's payment history. An incorrect identity or date may call for an official record. A disagreement involving health information may require records from the relevant provider.

Keep a short timeline showing the application, policy issue, relevant payments, death, claim submission, and insurer correspondence. Label uncertain dates as uncertain. If you are correcting something, explain what the correction is and identify its supporting record.

Send sensitive material through the company's verified process. Keep a copy of what you submitted and evidence that it arrived. A folder containing only the insurer's letters will not show which documents your family supplied or when.

## Request a review with a focused explanation

Ask the insurer what review process applies, where to submit it, and whether a stated deadline affects your response. Do not assume that life insurance uses the same appeal procedure as a medical insurance plan.

A clear response can identify the decision, explain the point you dispute, and list the records supporting your position. For example: the denial says a premium was unpaid, but the attached bank record shows a debit on a particular date. Ask the insurer to reconcile that transaction with its payment history. That is more useful than simply saying the outcome feels wrong.

Keep correspondence factual. You can disagree firmly without guessing at motives or making an accusation the records do not support. Ask for a written explanation of the review outcome and any remaining options.

## When a state insurance complaint may help

The [NAIC's complaint guidance](https://content.naic.org/article/how-file-complaint-and-research-complaints-against-insurance-carriers) directs consumers to their state insurance department and recommends supporting documents, correspondence, and a log of conversations. The regulator can explain its complaint process and what it can examine.

Texas, for example, advises consumers to [contact the company, file a complaint when appropriate, and seek legal help if needed](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/consumer/annuity-life-insurance-complaint.html). A complaint is not a promise of payment or a substitute for legal representation. The responsible department and its powers depend on the circumstances.

Describe the result you are seeking: an explanation, correction of a record, review of specific evidence, or another response. An organized account helps someone understand the issue without reconstructing it from a stack of unrelated messages.

## Keep funeral payment decisions separate

An open dispute does not establish when money will arrive. Explain the situation to the funeral provider before agreeing to costs you expect the contested policy to pay. Ask what payment is due now and what arrangements, if any, the provider offers.

Check whether another existing policy or benefit may help, without assuming that finding it means a claim will be approved. Our guide to [paying funeral bills while insurance is pending](/resources/funeral-payment-while-insurance-claim-pending) addresses that timing problem separately.

For future planning, a [phone conversation about your own final-expense coverage](/book) can help identify benefits, exclusions, payment responsibilities, and remaining gaps before a family needs to make a claim. That conversation does not resolve the denial on someone else's policy.

## Know when to obtain legal advice

If the dispute involves contested beneficiary rights, a settlement offer, alleged misrepresentation, or a possible lawsuit, obtain advice about your specific facts and deadlines. Do not assume that a complaint or ongoing correspondence extends a legal time limit.

You do not need to solve every question before organizing the evidence. Start with the decision, the policy language, and the records that bear on the stated reason. Keep the funeral budget grounded in confirmed resources while the dispute is considered.
