**Quick answer:** Compare the total for the same arrangements at both funeral homes. Ask each provider to list what is included, what you would pay separately and what is still an estimate. The cheapest advertised package may cost more once both quotes cover the same services.

## Give both providers the same instructions

Pick the arrangements you want each funeral home to price. For example, you might ask for a brief viewing, a graveside service, a simple wood casket and transportation to the same cemetery. Tell both homes the pickup location and cemetery. A change in distance, hours or venue can make one quote look different for reasons that have little to do with the provider’s prices.

Ask for two totals if you are still deciding about an optional service. One could cover the arrangements with a viewing and the other could show the same plan without it. That makes it easier to see which line actually changes, including any preparation, room or staffing charges tied to the viewing.

Under the [FTC Funeral Rule](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/ftc-funeral-rule), covered providers must give prices by phone when asked and provide a written **General Price List** when you discuss covered arrangements in person. This is the provider’s list of prices for individual goods and services. Ask whether they will email you a copy and a written estimate for the same plan. When you make arrangements, the provider must give you an itemized statement of what you selected and what it costs.

## Compare the amount each quote leaves out

Here is an **illustrative example** using made-up figures for the same local burial plan. In this example the cemetery charges the same amount either way. One funeral home shows it in the estimate and the other leaves it out.

| Line item | Funeral home A | Funeral home B |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Basic services and staff | $1,900 | $2,000 |
| Transfer and transportation to the cemetery | $600 | $500 |
| Viewing, preparation and graveside staff | $1,000 | $850 |
| Casket | $950 | $1,050 |
| Cemetery opening and closing | $1,200 shown | $1,200 billed separately |
| Total shown on funeral home estimate | $5,650 | $4,400 |
| Comparable total for the listed arrangements | $5,650 | $5,600 |

In this example, the apparent $1,250 difference drops to $50. Both totals still need the plot and any marker if those are separate purchases. Write the cemetery and other third-party charges in their own rows, even if a provider collects them. An amount can be included in the estimate and still be payable to another business. Ask who issues the bill and when it is due.

If you’re planning ahead, add the plot and marker to the comparable total when they’re billed separately. Then compare the full estimate with savings and any life insurance you expect your family to use for those costs. A remaining gap gives you a starting point for comparing additional burial coverage.

## Check these details before you rely on either total

- **Basic services fee.** Ask which arrangements it includes and whether the package price already accounts for it. Our [basic services fee questions](/resources/funeral-basic-services-fee-questions) explain how to check the line.
- **Similar merchandise.** Have both providers name the casket or container in their estimate. If one quote includes a different product, request the price for a comparable option.
- **Services with a separate provider.** Confirm cemetery and crematory fees directly with the businesses involved. Ask if you will need to pay a separate deposit.
- **Estimated expenses.** The [FTC pricing checklist](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/funeral-costs-pricing-checklist) explains that a funeral home may list a good-faith estimate when the exact amount for an item it purchases on your behalf is unknown. Ask when the provider expects to update the figure.
- **Price changes.** Ask what happens if the date changes, the transfer is farther than expected or you add or remove a service. Request the updated amount in writing.

## Compare how much money you need first

Ask for the deposit, the date the balance is due and the payment types accepted. The smaller final bill can still require a larger deposit. If you are expecting money from relatives or a life insurance claim, ask the provider which costs can wait and get any agreed payment dates in writing.

For a plan you are making ahead of time, keep the two estimates together and date them. Tell the person who would make the arrangements where to find them. A price list can change, so update the quote before relying on an older total. If you want to compare the amount with your savings or life insurance, you can [schedule a phone consultation](/book) and bring the figures you have.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does a funeral home have to give prices over the phone?**

The FTC says covered funeral providers must give price information by phone when asked. It can still help to request an estimate showing which items are included and which ones are billed separately.

**What if the funeral home cannot give me the exact cemetery cost?**

Ask the cemetery directly about the plot, opening and closing and any required container. Mark the amount as an estimate until you have the cemetery’s price for your arrangements.

**Should I compare the deposit or the total first?**

Check the full cost for equivalent arrangements, then compare the deposit and due dates. They answer different questions about affordability.
