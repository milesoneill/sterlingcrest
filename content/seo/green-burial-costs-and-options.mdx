**Quick answer:** A green burial can cost less if it avoids embalming, a conventional casket and a vault. The total still depends on the cemetery plot, the grave opening and closing, transportation and the services you choose. Get separate written totals from the cemetery and funeral provider.

## Start with the cemetery

Green burial generally means placing an unembalmed body in the ground in a biodegradable shroud or container without a conventional vault. A shroud is a cloth used to wrap the body. Cemetery rules determine which materials are accepted and what can go on the grave. A green burial may take place in a dedicated natural cemetery or in a section of a conventional cemetery that permits green burials. The [Green Burial Council cemetery map](https://www.greenburialcouncil.org/cemetery-provider-map/) identifies certified sites in several categories.

A **conservation burial** adds a long-term land protection component. The [Green Burial Council](https://www.greenburialcouncil.org/conservation-burial/) describes it as a natural burial ground operated with a conservation organization and a conservation management plan. If land stewardship is important to your family, ask how the site is protected and maintained, how you locate the grave later and what happens if the operator changes.

Before you shop for a casket or shroud, ask the cemetery for its requirements in writing. A family might choose an untreated wooden casket only to learn the site has rules about metal fasteners or finishes. Ask about timing as well. A burial ground with limited winter access or a rural location may have a different scheduling process from the funeral home.

## Add the cemetery bill and funeral provider bill

| Cost | Ask who charges it | What to confirm |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Burial plot | Cemetery | Exact location, use rights and whether any stewardship fee is included |
| Opening and closing the grave | Cemetery | Standard price and any weekend or seasonal charge |
| Transfer and care | Funeral provider | Pickup, travel to the site and any refrigeration needed |
| Shroud or biodegradable casket | Funeral provider or seller | Cemetery approval and whether you need a board or carrier |
| Memorial and service | Cemetery, funeral provider or venue | Marker restrictions, equipment and fees for staff or space |

The [Funeral Consumers Alliance green burial guide](https://www.funerals.org/get-help/green-options/) cites a broad **$1,000–$4,000** range for a green grave site and interment of a body. It does not provide a dated national price study for that range. Treat it as a starting point for the **cemetery portion** and use a written local quote for your budget. Transportation, professional services and a memorial can be separate.

Here is an **illustrative example**. Suppose a cemetery quotes $2,200 for a plot and opening and closing. The funeral provider quotes $1,900 for transport, basic arrangements and a simple container. A $250 approved marker takes the combined total to $4,350 before any separate gathering. If a farther cemetery saves $400 on the plot but costs $450 more for transportation, the lower plot price does not save money overall.

If you’re planning ahead, keep the cemetery and funeral provider totals together and include a separate gathering if you want one. Compare that full estimate with savings and existing life insurance you’ve set aside for the burial. If those funds cover only part of the estimate, use the difference to plan additional savings or compare burial coverage.

## Ask which choices actually change the price

Start with a direct question for the provider. “What would immediate burial cost if we provide an approved container and use this cemetery?” Immediate burial generally means burial without a formal viewing or ceremony with the body present. You can also ask for a second quote if you want staff at a graveside gathering. A quote labeled “green package” can include extra services, so compare both estimates line by line.

Under the [FTC Funeral Rule](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/ftc-funeral-rule), covered funeral providers must provide prices by phone when asked and cannot charge you a handling fee for a casket you buy elsewhere. The rule generally does not cover cemeteries without an on-site funeral home. You can still ask the cemetery to list what is required and what is optional. See our [cemetery costs checklist](/resources/cemetery-costs-checklist) for more items to check.

Also ask what happens between pickup and burial. If relatives want a private goodbye, ask whether the funeral provider can arrange one while meeting the cemetery’s preparation requirements. Ask the cemetery about access for guests with limited mobility, weather contingencies and whether your family can participate in the graveside service.

## Leave a short plan for your family

Write down the cemetery, the funeral provider, who has the price sheets and what your family should do if the location is unavailable. Add whether you prefer a shroud or casket and whether you want a service. Share the plan with the person likely to make the calls. If you plan to use savings or life insurance, identify where the documents are kept and what cash may be available first. You can [schedule a phone consultation](/book) if you want to compare that funding with the two estimates.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is green burial always cheaper than a conventional burial?**

Costs depend on the plot, grave opening and closing, transportation and any services. An estimate can be lower without a vault or a conventional casket, but a higher plot price or longer transfer can offset the difference.

**Can I have a funeral or graveside service with a green burial?**

Ask the cemetery and funeral provider about their options. Check preparation requirements, any restrictions on equipment or flowers and the cost of staffing the gathering.

**How can I find a green burial cemetery?**

Start with a provider directory such as the Green Burial Council map. Confirm the burial rules, current availability and total in writing with each site you consider.
