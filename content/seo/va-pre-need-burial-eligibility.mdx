**Quick answer:** Use VA Form 40-10007 to ask whether you or a qualifying family member can be buried in a VA national cemetery. Apply online, by mail, or by fax. VA’s decision gives your family a starting point for future arrangements. They’ll schedule the burial when it’s needed.

## Decide which cemetery you want to plan for

A pre-need determination is VA’s decision about eligibility made before the burial is needed. The application on this page is for VA national cemeteries. If you’re considering a state, territorial, or tribal Veterans cemetery, call that cemetery about its own process. For Arlington National Cemetery or the United States Soldiers’ and Airmen’s Home National Cemetery, VA directs families to Arlington for separate arrangements.

Start with the place your family would want to visit. Then ask about the options that matter to you, such as burial of a casket or placement of cremated remains. Writing down a preferred cemetery is part of the VA application. Availability at the time of burial determines which location your family can use. [VA pre-need eligibility instructions](https://www.va.gov/burials-memorials/pre-need-eligibility/)

When you’re planning the budget, separate the cemetery benefits you confirm with VA from the funeral home services your family may still need to pay for, such as preparation or a gathering before burial. Compare those estimated charges with savings and life insurance your family could use. If a gap remains, use that amount as a starting point when comparing additional burial coverage. [National Cemetery Administration burial benefits](https://www.cem.va.gov/burial-memorial-benefits/)

## Gather the information VA asks for

| What to collect | Where to start |
| --- | --- |
| Applicant’s personal information | Have the date of birth and Social Security number available to enter through the official VA process. |
| Military service information | Use the Veteran’s or sponsoring service member’s military history and separation records if available. |
| Preferred VA national cemetery | Write down the full cemetery name so it can be listed on the application. |
| Documents for a representative or eligible family member | Check VA’s applicant instructions for the specific records your situation requires. |

A DD214 is a military separation document that shows details of a service member’s record. VA can try to locate relevant records if you don’t have a copy, but including available separation records can help VA process your request. If you’re applying for someone else, read VA’s requirements about who can apply and what proof of authority to attach. [VA Form 40-10007](https://www.va.gov/forms/40-10007/)

## Submit the application and keep the decision

Use VA’s online application or download the paper form and follow the current mail or fax instructions. VA asks applicants to submit once, because duplicates may delay a decision. If you apply online, save the confirmation for your records.

VA may contact you if it needs more information. After you receive the decision letter, keep it with your funeral instructions and tell the family member who would make arrangements where to find it. Keep sensitive records in a safe location and share where to look rather than sending a Social Security number or service record to a family group chat. [VA online pre-need application](https://www.va.gov/burials-and-memorials/pre-need/form-10007-apply-for-eligibility)

## Give your family a short note they can use later

Write down the preferred cemetery, a second location you’d be comfortable with, whether you prefer burial or cremation, and where you filed the decision letter. Add the name of the family member who knows the plan. It can be a single sheet next to the other papers.

If the decision confirms eligibility, your family or the funeral director can contact the National Cemetery Scheduling Office when arrangements are needed. VA may review eligibility again if the law or your circumstances changed. If the person has already died, use the scheduling process now. [VA burial scheduling instructions](https://www.va.gov/burials-memorials/schedule-a-burial/)

## Keep each applicant’s paperwork together

If both spouses want their eligibility determined, use the form instructions to check what each person must submit. VA’s paper form calls for a separate application for each claimant. Keep each confirmation and decision with the name of the person it covers. One family folder can hold both sets of documents, but putting the applicant’s name on each can save a call later. [VA Form 40-10007](https://www.va.gov/forms/40-10007/)

## Check the costs before you put the file away

Ask a funeral home for an itemized estimate, including transportation, preparation, and any service your family would want. Check which costs would come from the funeral provider, even if the cemetery provides eligible services. For instance, a family may choose a funeral home gathering before the cemetery service. Ask for the staff and facility charges in writing so the family has an amount to plan for.

Keep the estimate with the decision letter and note what money your family expects to have when each bill is due. You can [schedule a consultation](/book) to discuss available coverage for a specific expense you still need to fund.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does VA’s letter reserve a gravesite?**

The decision establishes eligibility in advance. Your family and VA work out the cemetery and burial arrangements when needed, based on availability.

**Can a spouse apply before the Veteran dies?**

A legally married spouse or another potentially eligible family member can review VA’s eligibility rules and use the pre-need process for their own determination.

**How do I apply if I cannot find a DD214?**

Use the military service information you have. VA can try to locate missing separation records. Its online instructions also link to the military records request process.
